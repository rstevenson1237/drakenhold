## TRUTHS

**The old tongue compounds, and a name is a claim about a line.** Dwarven names are built from roots, and reading them is reading a genealogy. New roots are added to this record rather than coined in passing.

- Roots: *az* high or royal, *kel* stone, *mor* deep, *rud* iron, *bal* light or flame, *vak* fire, *thur* oath, *gan* hammer, *gir* gate, *dun* hall, *ith* blood or lineage, *drak* wyrm.
- Further roots, recovered from place-names within the hold: *tak* trade, *mek* work or machine, *khor* hearth or forge, *bran* grain or store, *grath* judgement, *kar* arms or guard, *vald* hoard or keeping, *thal* feast or gathering, *nur* the dead, *sig* rune or mark, *zar* trial or ordeal, *bryn* span or crossing.
- Further roots, recovered from the road and the waystones: *rok* road or way, *lath* league, *ven* toward, *ost* water or river, *vir* wood or standing timber.
- The Leader of the Guard was **Karn Rudgir** — *kar* and *rud*, arms and iron, a name that was a job before it was a person.
- Clan names take the *-ith* suffix. The founding lineage was **Clan Azkelith**, now largely unwritten in the upper halls. Surviving clan names in the hold: **Rudgir**, **Balvak**, **Kelmor**.
- The last King: **Baldrun Azkelith**, struck from most surfaces and recoverable from a few. The Knight of Drakenhold: **Torvin Ganthur**, found only in graffiti, ledgers and personal writing, never in official carving. Steward: **Vessa Rudgir**. Leader of the Guard: **Karn Rudgir**, her brother. Guild seats: **Dovrek Balvak** of the Smithing houses, **Ismelda Kelmor** of the Trade houses. Runemaster primarch: **Ulgrin Thurvak**.
- Thornhaven is a human town and names plainly: Reeve **Aldis Marrow**, wharfmaster **Ganner Skeed**, and the woman at the wood's edge who calls herself **Wyla Fenn**.

**Names hold power.** Speaking a name out of the dwarven past opens secret doors, wakes magical effects, and lets a party address the wraiths of the dead as people rather than as hazards. Royal names open what nothing else will, and they are a resource to be collected rather than a single answer to be found. The King's name was struck from the stone for a reason, and the reason is not the one most people give.

**Runic script is legible to anyone who pays attention on the way in.** Public surfaces through Thornhaven, the trail, the River Crossing and the Outer City carry bilingual inscription, and the waystones alone will teach a careful party the script before they ever need it.

- Deciphering is a test of player expertise, not a class ability. The module always gives the inquisitive an adequate cue.
- Read Magic is required only for runes that unlock a magic-like effect, and only for the effect — never for the meaning.
- The Runemasters used the same script for wards and incantations, which is why a party that can read a warning can usually also read a threat.

**Carved stone tiles gate movement, and they gate it sideways.** Complex tiles fitted to matching receptacles grant passage — the Skybridge termini at all three peaks, and the seal set across the servants' main run between them. **A tile buys lateral movement**: across the hold, between the peaks, past the ground that everything watches. It does not buy vertical movement. The great ramps between levels and the two Processionals are open to anyone who walks up to them, and their price is being seen on them rather than being stopped at them. A tile is a place you may go. A broken tile opens nothing, and tiles are scavenged, traded and buried across the whole setting by people who do not know what they hold.

**Metallic rods gate action.** Precious-metal rods carved with runes stand in place of keys. Where a tile lets a party move, a rod makes something happen — waking machines, throwing vault bolts, opening valves, setting the old workings running.

- Rods fall into House, Guild, Runemaster and Royal tiers.
- Some open many things poorly; the highest security demands several very specific rods together, and the Royal locks answer to no single rod alone.

**The dwarves built machines and earth-powered magic, and much of it still has power in it.** Deep vents drive forge, lift, ward and lamp. Any function a party restores changes what a level is capable of and what it costs to be there — and every restoration is its own puzzle to interrogate. A rod is the common thread through most of them and never the only answer.

**Seven seats governed the hold.** Two from the Trade Guilds, two from the Runemasters, the Steward, the Leader of the Guard, and the King. Every surviving record of the years before the Breaking is a record of that table, and the vote that split it is recoverable from more than one side.

**Vermakith demands tribute from everything that lives in his domain.** Tribute is the only form of address he accepts, it must be fitting, and it is never enough. Kobold, lizardman and intruder alike are measured by what they carry up to him. The tribute chain is a river of wealth moving upward through the hold every day, and it can be tapped, diverted, poisoned or broken.

**The dark of Drakenhold is hostile to light.** Flame carries, and what it carries to is the risen dead and the older things sleeping in the earth, both of which are drawn to snuff it. Below the Under Levels a torch is a declaration, and in the Lost Caverns it is answered.

**There was an underclass, and a few of them are still alive.** Beneath the Runemasters, the Guildmasters and the Royal Family were the servants, staff and slaves who kept the hold running and were never counted among its citizens. A handful survive in the servants' passages of all three peaks. They were given no crypt, so they bury their own where they can.

**A lance lies with the Knight of Drakenhold, and it may bring ruin upon the dragon.** It is an heirloom of the dragon war three centuries gone, forged with Runemaster workings whose exact purpose has been lost even to those who kept it. It was laid with Torvin Ganthur by the last dwarves who could reach him, as tribute to his honour rather than as a weapon meant to be used again.
