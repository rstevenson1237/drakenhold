# DRAKENHOLD

**Drakenhold — Ruined Hold, Dragon Peak, Dwarven Lineage**

---

## OVERVIEW

The Breakstone mountains run northwards and slightly west for hundreds of miles, open grassland to their east and the Ironwood to their west. At the northern edge of the Ironwood, where the forest breaks into hills and scrub, the range turns hard east for three single peaks before resuming north into the impassable Broken Teeth. Those three peaks are Drakenhold. The road through them was the northernmost passable route from the west — through the Ironwood to Thornhaven, where the river carried goods south to civilization and the roads carried them west to the farming country. That road has been closed for thirty years.

The dwarves called it Kelmordun. *Drakenhold* is a trade-tongue corruption they never used, and it is the only name anyone alive says.

Three peaks, three halls, three descents. Peak 1 (F) was the working heart — trade and craft and the feeding of the hold. Peak 2 (G) was its authority — judgement, arms and the King. Peak 3 (H) was its spirit — celebration, the dead, and the Runemasters. Each peak carries five levels: an Under Level cut below the Main Level, and three upper levels climbed into the mountain above it. Beneath all of it lie the Lost Caverns, which the dwarves opened and never truly closed.

The hold did not fall to the dragon. It fell to itself, and the dragon came afterward to a mountain that could no longer answer him. An artifact was taken out of the earth forty years ago, was banned, was stolen, and was broken apart in the Runemaster Sanctuary in the same season the King was assassinated and the civil war began. What broke with it was a yoke laid on the spirits of the Wyrms below. Thirty-one years ago a young Knight preached the clans back together. Thirty years ago Vermakith descended on them and scattered them for good. For ten years nothing has crossed the Ironwood and no smoke has risen from the peaks.

The campaign's spine is extraction and survival. Drakenhold is a place to rob and escape from, not a boss to defeat. Vermakith is weather — an apex hazard to be routed around, paid in tribute, and killed only by a party that has decided to and has spent the whole campaign earning it. Rumours of his deeper purpose should be seeded generously and are all wrong. He is not here for the artifact, or for vengeance, or for prophecy. He is here because it is the largest and richest thing within reach and he intends to be lord of it.

## TRUTHS

**The old tongue compounds, and a name is a claim about a line.** Dwarven names are built from roots, and reading them is reading a genealogy. New roots are added to this record rather than coined in passing.

- Roots: *az* high or royal, *kel* stone, *mor* deep, *rud* iron, *bal* light or flame, *vak* fire, *thur* oath, *gan* hammer, *gir* gate, *dun* hall, *ith* blood or lineage, *drak* wyrm.
- Further roots, recovered from place-names within the hold: *tak* trade, *mek* work or machine, *khor* hearth or forge, *bran* grain or store, *grath* judgement, *kar* arms or guard, *vald* hoard or keeping, *thal* feast or gathering, *nur* the dead, *sig* rune or mark, *zar* trial or ordeal, *bryn* span or crossing.
- Further roots, recovered from the road and the waystones: *rok* road or way, *lath* league, *ven* toward, *ost* water or river, *vir* wood or standing timber.
- The Leader of the Guard was **Karn Rudgir** — *kar* and *rud*, arms and iron, a name that was a job before it was a person.
- Clan names take the *-ith* suffix. The founding lineage was **Clan Azkelith**, now largely unwritten in the upper halls. Surviving clan names in the hold: **Rudgir**, **Balvak**, **Kelmor**.
- The last King: **Baldrun Azkelith**, struck from most surfaces and recoverable from a few. The Knight of Drakenhold: **Torvin Ganthur**, found only in graffiti, ledgers and personal writing, never in official carving. Steward: **Vessa Rudgir**. Leader of the Guard: **Karn Rudgir**, her brother. Guild seats: **Dovrek Balvak** of the Smithing houses, **Ismelda Kelmor** of the Trade houses. Runemaster primarch: **Ulgrin Thurvak**.
- Thornhaven is a human town and names plainly: Reeve **Aldis Marrow**, wharfmaster **Ganner Skeed**, and the woman at the wood's edge who calls herself **Wyla Fenn**.

**Names hold power.** Speaking a name out of the dwarven past opens secret doors, wakes magical effects, and lets a party address the wraiths of the dead as people rather than as hazards. Royal names open what nothing else will, and they are a resource to be collected rather than a single answer to be found. The King's name was struck from the stone for a reason, and the reason is not the one most people give.

**Runic script is legible to anyone who pays attention on the way in.** Public surfaces through Thornhaven, the trail, the River Crossing and the Outer City carry bilingual inscription, and the waystones alone will teach a careful party the script before they ever need it.

- Deciphering is a test of player expertise, not a class ability. The module always gives the inquisitive an adequate cue.
- Read Magic is required only for runes that unlock a magic-like effect, and only for the effect — never for the meaning.
- The Runemasters used the same script for wards and incantations, which is why a party that can read a warning can usually also read a threat.

**Carved stone tiles gate movement, and they gate it sideways.** Complex tiles fitted to matching receptacles grant passage — the Skybridge termini at all three peaks, and the seal set across the servants' main run between them. **A tile buys lateral movement**: across the hold, between the peaks, past the ground that everything watches. It does not buy vertical movement. The great ramps between levels and the two Processionals are open to anyone who walks up to them, and their price is being seen on them rather than being stopped at them. A tile is a place you may go. A broken tile opens nothing, and tiles are scavenged, traded and buried across the whole setting by people who do not know what they hold.

**Metallic rods gate action.** Precious-metal rods carved with runes stand in place of keys. Where a tile lets a party move, a rod makes something happen — waking machines, throwing vault bolts, opening valves, setting the old workings running.

- Rods fall into House, Guild, Runemaster and Royal tiers.
- Some open many things poorly; the highest security demands several very specific rods together, and the Royal locks answer to no single rod alone.

**The dwarves built machines and earth-powered magic, and much of it still has power in it.** Deep vents drive forge, lift, ward and lamp. Any function a party restores changes what a level is capable of and what it costs to be there — and every restoration is its own puzzle to interrogate. A rod is the common thread through most of them and never the only answer.

**Seven seats governed the hold.** Two from the Trade Guilds, two from the Runemasters, the Steward, the Leader of the Guard, and the King. Every surviving record of the years before the Breaking is a record of that table, and the vote that split it is recoverable from more than one side.

**Vermakith demands tribute from everything that lives in his domain.** Tribute is the only form of address he accepts, it must be fitting, and it is never enough. Kobold, lizardman and intruder alike are measured by what they carry up to him. The tribute chain is a river of wealth moving upward through the hold every day, and it can be tapped, diverted, poisoned or broken.

**The dark of Drakenhold is hostile to light.** Flame carries, and what it carries to is the risen dead and the older things sleeping in the earth, both of which are drawn to snuff it. Below the Under Levels a torch is a declaration, and in the Lost Caverns it is answered.

**There was an underclass, and a few of them are still alive.** Beneath the Runemasters, the Guildmasters and the Royal Family were the servants, staff and slaves who kept the hold running and were never counted among its citizens. A handful survive in the servants' passages of all three peaks. They were given no crypt, so they bury their own where they can.

**A lance lies with the Knight of Drakenhold, and it may bring ruin upon the dragon.** It is an heirloom of the dragon war three centuries gone, forged with Runemaster workings whose exact purpose has been lost even to those who kept it. It was laid with Torvin Ganthur by the last dwarves who could reach him, as tribute to his honour rather than as a weapon meant to be used again.

## HISTORY

**1273 years ago — The Founding of Kelmordun.** An outpost is cut into the first peak by a lineage whose name is nowhere written in the hold that grew from it. Over three hundred years the three great halls are carved and driven downward until the digging opens something older. *Trace: the lowest courses of all three main halls are laid in a plainer, older runic hand than anything above them and carry no clan mark at all.*

**973 years ago — The Waking of the Deep.** Minor wyrms are found crawling in the lightless caverns below, living off volcanic heat. They are hateful, they strike back, and the dwarves bleed for every foot of depth they take. *Trace: shafts in the Lost Caverns sealed with warning inscription rather than with stone, and middens of wyrm bone left where they fell.*

**960 years ago — The Driving Down.** The clans unite under their first King, who organises a final push that forces the wyrms into the deep dark permanently and leaves the dwarves sole rulers of Drakenhold. *Trace: the driving-down is the single most repeated motif in the hold's relief carving, and the first King's is the first name cut on the black obelisk.*

**900 years ago — The Guild Compact.** A long peace establishes the Guild Hall and the founding traditions that would eventually produce the council of seven. *Trace: the compact is carved where the guilds could see it daily, and the standard weights and tally-stones it fixed are still the ones sitting on the scales.*

**752 years ago — The Silver Veins.** Precious metal is struck nearby and prosperity follows. Each peak is expanded upward into three new levels, and the Skybridge is thrown between them, making one hold out of three outposts. *Trace: the seam is visible — everything above the Main Level is finer, later work in a different stone, and the join runs clean around all three peaks.*

**311 years ago — The Dragon War.** Increasing attacks from dragons lead to open conflict. Ancient magic repels and finally defeats them, and the Runemasters who wielded it are given the Third Peak as reward. The watchtowers are raised and the Skybridge fortified. Vermakith is of that ancient kin but no descendant of those that fell here, and would name any ancestor of his beneath him.

**40 years ago — The Artifact.** Something is brought up out of the deep that grants the dwarves the ear of a bound demon with a connection to the earth. What they took for a demon was a yoke laid on the spirits of the wyrms below, whose kinship with the deep let them name every vein of metal in the mountain. Mining expands enormously and the hold grows rich beyond its history.

**35 years ago — The Ban.** Evil begins coming up out of the caverns. The King grows fearful of what the artifact truly holds and forbids its use, over the outcry of the Runemasters.

**33 years ago — The Theft.** The artifact is stolen. The schism between the Crown and the Third Peak becomes open.

**32 years ago — The Breaking.** The King is assassinated and civil war follows. Loyalists make a last stand in the Armory beneath the Judgement Hall. In the same season the artifact is broken apart in the Runemaster Sanctuary, its yoke shattered, and the freed wyrm spirits turn on the peak that held them. The pieces are carried out and hidden across the hold by dwarves who did not live to say where. Far to the north, something old and greedy feels the leash come off.

**31 years ago — The Knight's Peace.** A young dwarven knight casts off his sword and leaves his wealth behind, preaching peace and humility and brotherhood to the broken clans. They begin to return. For one year the hold stirs with fragile hope.

**30 years ago — The Descent.** A great red dragon comes down on Drakenhold out of the north. The reuniting clans are scattered for good, and the Knight's fate is unknown.

**10 years ago — The Last Sighting.** Vermakith is seen away from the mountain for the last time. After that, nothing.

**Today — The Silence.** *(present day)* Ten winters without a shadow crossing the Ironwood and no smoke from the peaks. Thornhaven's wharf quarter stands empty and the upper town's trade houses have been shuttered so long that saplings have come up through the boards. The road is still there. Every year someone walks up it, and every year Thornhaven counts who does not come back.

## FACTIONS

*Faction relations begin at their stated defaults, and not every faction knows the others exist. Ignorance is its own relation, and discovery should be worth something.*

**Vermakith** - 6d6 - Apex Hazard, Tribute Sovereign, Immovable

- **Resources:** The hoard; the whole of Peak 2's upper levels (GD, GE); the tribute chain; the Lizardmen entire.
- **Knowledge:** Every corridor of his own domain by scent and echo; the location of most of the gold in Drakenhold; nothing whatsoever about the artifact he is assumed to have come for (rumours 1, 12).
- **Reactions:** Hatred - 1, d4; insult is answered immediately and disproportionately. He wakes for three things and three only — fire in the vents, theft at scale, and the sound of his own name.
- **Tactics:** He does not act, he demands. Tribute is levied, deadlines are set, the Lizardmen collect. He is fat, slow, and sleeps more than he wakes, and none of that has made him less certain.
- **Goals:** Standing — to be named lord and master of Drakenhold by everything that lives in it. Immediate — to hold every valuable thing within the mountain, and to be brought more.
- **Relations:** Lizardmen - Tolerated 4, d10 — useful and replaceable. Kobolds - Neutral 3, d8 — a source of volume, individually beneath notice. Everything else - Hatred 1, d4 or unknown to him entirely.

**Lizardmen** - 3d6 - Dragon's Voice, Toll-Keepers, Skimmers

- **Resources:** GD and GE; standing patrols on the Skybridge span and the upper ramps; first claim on anything the kobolds bring up (rumour 11).
- **Knowledge:** The tribute schedule; which vaults remain sealed; roughly how long the Dragon has been asleep.
- **Reactions:** Antipathy - 2, d6; intruders are assessed for tribute value before violence, and a party that arrives carrying something worth taking is a transaction rather than a fight.
- **Tactics:** Checkpoint and toll. Never open pursuit far from the lair.
- **Goals:** Standing — keep the tribute flowing at volume. Immediate — skim enough off it that no one notices, and keep anyone from carrying word of the skim upward.
- **Relations:** Vermakith - Fear 1, d4. Kobolds - Antipathy 2, d6 — livestock that occasionally counts. Hobgoblins, Goblins, the rival crews - unknown.

**Kobolds** - 2d6 - Leaderless Warren, Tribute Fodder, Willing to Deal

- **Resources:** The granaries, water tanks and vent chimney of FB; numbers.
- **Knowledge:** Every scavenging route in the Outer City and the lower peaks; where the Lizardmen skim (rumour 11).
- **Reactions:** Neutral - 3, d8; they will bargain before they fight and run before they lose (rumour 9).
- **Tactics:** Swarm, collapse, flee. Traps and noise over confrontation.
- **Goals:** Standing — meet the tribute without being eaten. Immediate — fill the chieftain's seat, empty since he walked up to HC and did not come back.
- **Relations:** Vermakith - Fear 1, d4. Lizardmen - Fear 1, d4. Dwarven Survivors - Antipathy 2, d6 — known to exist, hunted half-heartedly. Goblins - unknown, and they do not know the Ironwood is inhabited at all.

**Dwarven Survivors** - 1d6 - Last of the Underclass, Hidden Passages, Keepers of the Dead

- **Resources:** The servants' passages of all three peaks; caches; water; knowledge of every back way in the hold.
- **Knowledge:** The true layout of Drakenhold; where several artifact pieces were hidden; where Torvin Ganthur was laid.
- **Reactions:** Fear - 1, d4, moving to Neutral or better for anyone who shows the hold and its dead real respect. They have been hunted for thirty years and it shows.
- **Tactics:** Avoid, observe, disappear. They do not fight and they do not treat with anyone twice until they have watched them once.
- **Goals:** Standing — survive. Immediate — for a few of them, to see the hold's dead finally laid properly.
- **Relations:** Kobolds - Fear 1, d4. Lizardmen - Fear 1, d4. Vermakith - Hatred 1, d4. The rival crews - unknown, and the crews do not believe they are real.

**Hobgoblins** - 2d6 - Disciplined Raiders, Crossing Toll, Arithmetic of Force

- **Resources:** The far guard post at the River Crossing (rumour 7); a camp in the broken ground between the crossing and the Outer City; the only real discipline outside the hold.
- **Knowledge:** Who has come up the road and who has come back down it.
- **Reactions:** Antipathy - 2, d6; they respect force and price, in that order, and will take payment over a fight when the arithmetic favours it.
- **Tactics:** Toll first, banditry second, and a raid on Thornhaven when the season is lean.
- **Goals:** Standing — hold the crossing as a revenue point. Immediate — work out what is worth taking inside the hold, without going in far enough to find out the hard way.
- **Relations:** Goblins - Hatred 1, d4 — vermin who use their road. Thornhaven - Antipathy 2, d6. The Grellick Charter - Neutral 3, d8 — a paying customer so far.

**Goblins** - 1d6 - Three Warrens, Divided, Losing Scouts

- **Resources:** Three ragged camps in the Ironwood; the trail; scavenged Outer City goods, including a carved stone tile they cannot read.
- **Knowledge:** The safe passage past the hobgoblins at the crossing; what the Outer City looks like from the outside (rumour 6).
- **Reactions:** Fear - 1, d4 toward anything organised; Neutral - 3, d8 toward anyone who brings them meat or kills the owlbear.
- **Tactics:** Toll ambush at chokepoints, immediate flight from anything that holds ranks. No coordination between the three camps.
- **Goals:** Standing — eat. Immediate — stop losing scouts to the thing nesting near the camps.
- **Relations:** Hobgoblins - Fear 1, d4. Thornhaven - Antipathy 2, d6. Kobolds - unknown, and neither knows the other exists.

**The Grellick Charter** - 2d6 - Licensed Salvage, Methodical, Renegotiators

- **Resources:** Twelve strong, camped in good order below the overlook at D; pack mules, rope and tackle, two mercenary swords, a surveyor; a written charter granting them everything they can carry out.
- **Knowledge:** A purchased copy of a partial Outer City survey; the going price of dwarven goods downriver.
- **Reactions:** Neutral - 3, d8; rivals are competitors rather than enemies until the moment a specific prize is in view.
- **Tactics:** Methodical and cautious. Entirely willing to hire the party as guides and then renegotiate the split.
- **Goals:** Standing — extract volume, cheaply. Immediate — be gone before winter.
- **Relations:** Thornhaven - Tolerated 4, d10 — they reprovision there and pay well. Ashen's Crew - Antipathy 2, d6. Hobgoblins - Neutral 3, d8, tolls paid without complaint.

**Ashen's Crew** - 2d6 - No Charter, No Patience, One Score

- **Resources:** Seven treasure hunters working out of a squat in the Outer City's government quarter; two who can read runic script; one House rod nobody has identified.
- **Knowledge:** Fragments, enough to be dangerous — including the rumour that something in Peak 3 is worth more than the hoard (rumours 5, 14).
- **Reactions:** Neutral - 3, d8 until they believe the party is ahead of them, then Hatred - 1, d4 without warning.
- **Tactics:** Follow someone competent at a distance, let them open the door, take the room.
- **Goals:** Standing — one score large enough to never come back. Immediate — get into Peak 3 ahead of anyone else. They are the ones who will open the wrong vault.
- **Relations:** Thornhaven - Antipathy 2, d6, known and unwelcome. The Grellick Charter - Antipathy 2, d6. Dwarven Survivors - unknown, and dismissed as a story when described.

## BESTIARY

**Wolf** (Beast) – AD: 2d6 Description: Lean, pack-hunting predators that test a target's footing and numbers before committing, peeling off the moment the odds turn. Rarely encountered alone — treat a "wolf" as one stat block among several working in concert.

**Giant Spider** (Beast) – AD: 2d6 Description: Ambush predators lurking in webbed thickets just off the trail, dropping from cover onto anything that lingers. Their bite carries a paralytic venom, and a patient eye can spot the husks of prior prey wrapped and stored nearby.

**Owlbear** (Beast) – AD: 5d6+3 MA: 2 Description: A hulking fusion of owl and bear, silent until the moment it isn't. Hunts by sound in total dark and territorial well past the point of reason, tearing into anything that lingers in its den.

**Kobold** (Humanoid) – AD: 1d6-2 Description: Small, cowardly reptilian opportunists who fight only in numbers and flee the instant those numbers thin. Favour traps, tunnels and noise over any direct confrontation.

**Kobold Warrior** (Humanoid) – AD: 1d6 Description: A kobold given crude armour, a spear, and just enough drilling to hold a line — as long as someone braver is visibly watching.

**Kobold Chieftain** (Humanoid) – AD: 3d6+1 Description: Rules the warren through fear and a stolen trophy weapon, always flanked by its most loyal warriors. Quick to trade information for its life if ever caught alone.

**Goblin** (Humanoid) – AD: 1d6-1 Description: Small, wiry scavengers with slimy green hide, breeding fast and roaming in ragged packs. Skittish around real threats but vicious the moment they sense an advantage, and desperate to stay off the notice of bigger things in the dark.

**Goblin Chieftain** (Humanoid) – AD: 3d6+1 Description: Clawed its way to the top of the tribe through cruelty and cunning, hoarding the tribe's best scavenged loot personally. Sacrifices underlings without hesitation to save its own skin.

**Hobgoblin** (Humanoid) – AD: 2d6 Description: Broad, disciplined and organised where their lesser kin are not, holding ground in ranks and posting sentries that stay awake. They understand tolls, tribute and terms, and will take payment over a fight when the arithmetic favours it.

**Lizardman** (Humanoid) – AD: 2d6+1 Description: Tall, cold-blooded and unhurried, wearing scavenged dwarven mail and carrying the Dragon's authority as though it were their own. They speak, they assess, and they are the only creatures in the hold that expect to be obeyed.

**Dwarven Survivor** (Humanoid) – AD: 2d6 Description: One of the last dwarves of the original hold, alive only because it has spent thirty years in passages even the Automatons do not patrol. Avoidant and suspicious by default, and will not fight unless cornered.

**Sellsword** (Man) – AD: 2d6+1 Description: Hired steel with a season or two of real fighting behind it, competent, unheroic and acutely aware of what it is being paid. Holds while the terms hold.

**Delver** (Man) – AD: 2d6 Description: A treasure hunter far enough into their trade to be dangerous and not far enough to be careful. Reads a room for its exits and its valuables and rarely in that order.

**Camp Hand** (Man) – AD: 1d6 Description: Muleteer, porter, cook, surveyor's boy — the people who make an expedition possible and who did not agree to go inside. They will carry, dig and hold a rope, and they will run.

**Automaton** (Construct) – AD: 3d6+2 Description: A dwarven war-machine, mostly engineering with a thread of rune-work woven through it, still executing whatever order it was last given centuries ago. Mindless and literal — it cannot be reasoned with, only redirected by satisfying the letter of its instruction.

**Living Statue** (Construct) – AD: 3d6+1 Description: A stone guardian animated purely by rune magic, holding a single post or threshold with total indifference to anything beyond the bounds of its watch. Slow and nearly silent, unsettling mainly for how long it stays motionless before moving at all.

**Skeletal Warrior** (Undead) – AD: 2d6+1 Description: The drilled remains of a fallen soldier, still working through old formation and weapon drills with no fear, fatigue or judgement left to interrupt them. Holds a post or a patrol route with total indifference to the cost.

**Shadow** (Undead) – AD: 2d6-1 Description: A sliver of negative life-force clinging to total darkness, cold and nearly silent until it is already touching you. Flinches from real light rather than press a losing fight, and drains warmth from anything it gets a grip on.

**Dwarven Wraith** (Undead) – AD: 4d6+2 Description: The vengeful spirit of a dwarven defender who died holding this place, bound by oath, grudge or both. Recognises every intruder as the next in a long line of looters, but may still parley in old dwarven for someone who shows the hold real respect. Every one of them carries some private fear or fury toward the Dragon, and that is the lever.

**Drakmorith** (Horror) – AD: 5d6+2 Description: A wyrm spirit thirty years free of the collar the dwarves put on it, no longer shaped like anything that was ever alive. It arrives as wrongness in the architecture before it arrives as a thing — a corridor that has gained a turn, a door that opens from a side the room does not have. It does not negotiate and does not appear to want. The name is dwarven and it is a confession: the survivors know exactly what these were.

**Lesser Wyrm** (Wyrm) – AD: 4d6+1 Description: Blind, pale, serpentine things bred down in the lightless deep on volcanic heat and whatever falls to them. Individually little more than a hazard of the terrain, but they hunt by vibration and they are never truly alone in a tunnel.

**Elder Wyrm** (Wyrm) – AD: 6d6+2 MA: 2 Description: The old ones, distant and diminished kin of the bloodline that produced Vermakith, horrible to look upon and swollen past the size of the passages they occupy. They remember the yoke laid on their spirits and they remember it breaking, and they are still angry about both.

**Vermakith** (Wyrm) – AD: 15d6+5 MA: 4 Description: A great red dragon out of the north whose descent thirty years ago shattered the reunited dwarven clans outright, come for the mountain and the mastery of it rather than for any grievance. Thirty years of tribute have made him enormous, indolent and slow to wake. He is not an encounter to be met in open battle and survived — a lair fight against Vermakith is fought against the room, the crater, the crumbling floor and the heat, and the Referee should never present a straight exchange of blows as a viable line of play.

**Ooze** (Fantasy Creature) – AD: 2d6 Description: A mindless corrosive mass that has pooled and fed in the hold's flooded lower halls for decades, swollen on collapsed stores and whatever drowned alongside them. Flows toward movement, warmth or noise with nothing behind it, and eats through cheap gear left sitting in it.

**Wood Sprite** (Fey) – AD: 1d6-2 Description: Palm-sized, light-bending fae bound to the Ironwood and unable to stray past its edge. More trickster than threat — they scatter trail markers, mimic voices and lead travellers in circles, preferring riddles and petty bargains to any kind of fight.

*Unique NPCs and one-off variants — a named hobgoblin captain, a mad Runemaster, a particular wraith — are stated inline within their Gazetteer entry. The Bestiary holds only the reusable templates.*

## UNANSWERED QUESTIONS

*What the module leaves open **at the table, on purpose**. These are not tasks. They are the places where the Referee rules and the setting declines to.*

- Hireling statlines are deliberately postponed to Referee ruling.
- The properties of the lance are unfixed. `HB.16` holds the line that nothing about how it was laid suggests it was meant to be used again.
- The Knight's fate is preserved as unknown in History and resolved in HB; this is intentional and should be checked at each pass to confirm no other section contradicts the public record.
- Baldrun Azkelith's interment at `HB.6` was prepared and never filled. Nothing in the module answers it, and nothing will.

*Questions the **authors** have not yet settled are not kept here. They live in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, which is architect material and is not part of the deliverable. This section is referee-facing, and adding to it is a deliberate act: it means the setting has decided **not** to answer something.*
