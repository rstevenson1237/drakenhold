# `A Thornhaven`

**Classification:** SAFE · **Difficulty Die:** d6 · **Tags:** Faded Trade Town, River Landing, Last Safe Bed

*Region file. Companion to the Drakenhold Setting Outline and the Setting Relational Diagram. Tables authored at the location-stub pass (procedure step 7); location outlines written at step 9, with the region diagram reconciled against them.*

---


**Overview:** The last safe bed on the road and the only SAFE region in the module. Thornhaven is a stockaded river landing raised on and partly built out of salvaged Drakenhold stone, and everything about it is smaller than it was — the upper town holds the well, the old toll-house and what is left of the trade houses, while the wharf quarter below has gone to transients and warehouses idle since the road closed a generation ago. Its job is resupply, rumour, downtime and the module's first lesson: the haunted farmstead outside town is a two-session dungeon that teaches every rule the hold will later enforce, and it teaches them at survivable scale. Common provisions, rope, oil and porters are readily had; anything skilled, specialist or dwarven must be sent downriver for, which is a clock the party will feel later. The town has two live problems of its own — the witch at the wood's edge and the graves under the farmstead — and both are winnable by negotiation rather than force, which is the register the whole region plays in. The d6 is small-town scale, not danger.

**Ambiance:** Wet timber and river mud, woodsmoke, tar. The palisade is new work in old stone — dwarven blocks with the runes still on them, laid by people who could not read them and did not care, some set upside down. The upper town is swept and quiet and too large for the number of people in it; the wharf quarter below smells of rope, fish and spilled beer, and half its warehouses have doors that have not been opened since the road closed. Saplings have come up through the boards of the shuttered trade houses.

**Layout:** A stockaded landing on the east bank, built up a shallow slope in two tiers. The upper town gathers around the well and the old toll-house — reeve's hall, temple, two inns, the shuttered trade houses. The wharf quarter runs along the water below it, warehouses and drinking-halls and the boat landing. The road out runs north-west toward the Ironwood; the river runs south to civilisation. Wyla Fenn's grove sits an hour's walk out along the wood's edge, and the haunted farmstead half that, inland. Small enough to cross on foot in a quarter hour.

**Features:** Commerce, at the availability tiers the region's poverty sets — common provisions, rope, oil and hands over the counter, anything skilled or specialist or dwarven sent downriver for and waited on. Downtime in all its forms, and the only Rest that recovers everything. Two live problems, one at the wood's edge and one under the farmstead, both winnable by negotiation and both expensive to force. That register — talk first, and force priced badly — is the region's whole instruction, and every named location teaches some part of it.

**Dangers:** None standing. The town does not answer intrusion; it gossips about it. Its two hazards are consequences rather than threats, and both are held at the locations that cause them: a boundary that answers when it is crossed, and a dead that rise when they are robbed.

**Creatures:** People, mostly, and the three who matter keep their own ground — `A.4`, `A.9` and `A.13`. Hired labour is `Camp Hand`, engaged at the landing. The Grellick Charter reprovisions here openly and in good order. Ashen's Crew are known and unwelcome, which has never once stopped them. The farmstead's dead are the town's own refugees from the year after the descent, and they are the only thing in the region that fights.

**Secrets:** That the town is built on dwarven work and out of dwarven stone, and carries a dwarven name it has never used — legible in three separate places before anyone is told to look for it. That the older families of the upper town were trade partners of the hold and still hold its goods. That one person alive in this region was inside Peak 3.

**Treasure:** Modest in coin throughout, and the region's real reward is information. Dwarven work is the only portable find here, in two kinds — what was buried with the refugees, and what was sold to their partners while both were still living — and each is held at the location that carries it. Nothing else in Thornhaven is worth the slots.

### `A.1 The Landing Gate` — Stolen Stone, Runes Upside Down, First Impression

*Player's Overview: The palisade is young timber standing on old grey blocks, and the blocks were cut by someone who cared. Letters run across them, deep and square and unhurried, and they run in every direction the masons happened to lay them — a line of them upside down beside the gate, another marching into the ground and out of sight. Two men lean on the trestle and watch the road, and neither has ever looked up at the stone above their heads.*

**Referee Overview:** The town's only landward gate, in the north-west face of the stockade. The gate lane is twelve feet wide and runs thirty paces north to the well square, hard-packed and rutted. The palisade is eight feet of split timber set on a footing course two blocks high — dressed grey stone, hauled up from the old landing below the town over three generations. The gate stands open from first light to full dark and is barred at night by two men who would rather not. The road out leaves west of the gate, dropping north-west toward the Ironwood.

**Features:**

* **The Footing Course:** dwarven blocks, laid without regard to which way up the inscription runs, mortared with river clay by masons who could not read them. Reading the course is a party's first exposure to runic script and nothing here translates it. The letters are the same hand as the waystones on the trail.
* **The Inverted Lintel:** one long block beside the gate post carries two roots and nothing else — *ost* and *gir*, water and gate. It is the town's dwarven name, set upside down at its front door, and the full reading waits at the third waystone on the trail.
* **The Gate Watch:** two men on the trestle, unpaid beyond a share of the toll that no longer comes in. They log nothing and remember everything: who went up the road, when, and with how many. They will trade that for a drink and are the cheapest information in the region.
* **The Gate Lane:** rutted, thirty paces, north into the square -> `A.2`
* **The Toll-House Door:** immediately inside the gate on the east side -> `A.3`
* **The Road Out:** north-west, dropping toward the wood -> `B.1`
* **The Wood's-Edge Path:** east along the treeline, an hour on foot -> `A.12`
* **The Field Track:** inland, half an hour, toward the outlying holdings -> `A.14`

**Connections:** `A.2` the well square, north through the gate lane · `A.3` the toll-house, immediately inside the gate · `A.12` the cutting ground, an hour along the wood's edge · `A.14` Ketter's farmstead, half an hour inland by the field track · `B.1` the road out, north-west.

### `A.2 The Well Square` — Swept and Too Large, Public Ground, Where Talk Starts

*Player's Overview: The square was cut for a town three times this size and it is swept every morning regardless. A stone well stands at its centre with a roof over it and a chain worn bright. Around the edges: a hall with the shutters open, a temple with the doors open, an inn with a painted bird on its sign, and a whole row of houses running downslope with the shutters nailed shut and young trees growing out of the porch boards.*

**Referee Overview:** Roughly sixty paces across, cobbled in the centre and packed earth at the margins. The well is dwarven-cut and older than the town; its chain and windlass are local work. The toll-house stands north, the reeve's hall east, the temple west, the Kingfisher east of the temple, and the shuttered trade houses run from the square's south-west corner downslope toward the wharf. The gate lane enters from the south. This is where the town's business is done in the open, and anything said here is repeated within the hour.

**Features:**

* **The Well:** dwarven-cut, twenty feet to good water, the shaft dressed square in the same grey stone as the palisade footing. Nobody in town thinks this is remarkable. It was here before the town was.
* **The Morning Square:** the town gathers here to trade small goods, hire, argue and pass news. Gather Information is at its cheapest and most reliable in Thornhaven standing here in daylight, and the answers skew toward what the upper town wants to believe.
* **The Charter's Mules:** when the Grellick Charter is reprovisioning, their pack train stands roped along the square's north side and their surveyor is visibly bored. He talks.
* **Exits:** the gate lane south -> `A.1` · the toll-house north -> `A.3` · the reeve's hall east -> `A.4` · the temple west -> `A.5` · the Kingfisher -> `A.6` · the shuttered row, downslope south-west -> `A.7`

**Connections:** `A.1` the gate lane, south · `A.3` the toll-house on the square's north side · `A.4` the reeve's hall, east · `A.5` the temple, west · `A.6` the Kingfisher, east · `A.7` the shuttered trade houses, the row running downslope.

### `A.3 The Old Toll-House` — The Notice Board, Ledgers Nobody Reads, Closed Road

*Player's Overview: A long low room with a counter across it and a board beside the door, and the board is the only thing in here anyone still uses. Behind the counter the shelves go back into the dark, and they are full — ledger after ledger, spine after spine, a wall of them, thirty years of somebody writing down every cart that came through this door. The dust on the last one is even.*

**Referee Overview:** Twenty paces east to west, five deep, counter running its length. Door south to the gate lane, door east through the party wall into the reeve's hall — one building divided into two offices when the traffic died. The notice board is a plank frame beside the south door carrying whatever the reeve wants done. The shelving on the north wall holds the toll ledgers, complete, in order, and unconsulted.

**Features:**

* **The Notice Board:** the reeve's problems, posted as paid work. Standing notices concern the outlying fields, missing stock, and a rate offered for anyone who will go out to Ketter's holding and come back saying what is happening there. This is where the farmstead becomes a job.
* **The Toll Ledgers:** thirty years of traffic thinning to nothing, kept in one hand and then another and then none. Read backward, they are a graph of a road closing. The last four years of dwarven entries are written in two scripts, trade-tongue and runic, side by side by a clerk who was evidently learning — which makes them a usable primer for anyone patient enough to sit with them.
* **The Counter Weights:** a set of trade weights still in the drawer, dwarven-standard, stamped. They are worth little and they match the standards a party will meet inside the hold.
* **Exits:** the gate lane -> `A.1` · the square -> `A.2` · the connecting door east -> `A.4`

**Connections:** `A.1` the gate, immediately outside · `A.2` the square · `A.4` the reeve's hall, adjoining — one wall, one door, two offices that used to be one.

### `A.4 The Reeve's Hall` — Aldis Marrow, Outlying Families, A Problem He Cannot Solve

*Player's Overview: A working room, warm, with a table too big for it and a map of the district pinned flat under four stones. A heavy man sits behind the table and does not stand up when you come in. He looks at your boots, and your gear, and the road-dirt on both, and something in his face settles — not relief exactly. Something he has been waiting to feel.*

**Referee Overview:** Fifteen paces square, east of the square and adjoining the toll-house through the west door. Fire, table, district map, strongbox. Marrow holds the reeve's seat by consent rather than appointment, has held it eleven years, and has no armed force beyond the gate watch and whatever the town will turn out for.

**Features:**

* **Aldis Marrow:** reeve, sixty, careful, and out of ideas. Four holdings east and inland have gone quiet or gone empty since spring, and the families still out there are two bad weeks from abandoning their fields — which would end the town, slowly and completely. He knows the Ketter place is the centre of it and he cannot make anyone go and look. He pays in coin, in standing, and in what he knows, and the third is the valuable one: he will name Wyla Fenn as somebody who knew this country before the road closed, and he will do it reluctantly, because the palisade timber is his doing.
* **The District Map:** the holdings, the field tracks, the wood's edge, the river. Marked in his own hand. Four holdings are crossed through and the crosses are recent.
* **The Strongbox:** the job's payment, and the town's remaining cash, and the two are close to the same number.
* **Exits:** the square -> `A.2` · the toll-house through the connecting door -> `A.3`

**Connections:** `A.2` the square · `A.3` the toll-house, through the connecting door.

### `A.5 The Riverside Temple` — Underattended, Rites for the Drowned, Quiet Charity

*Player's Overview: Cold stone, plain benches, and more of both than there are people to fill them. Along the north wall the names of the drowned are cut in rows going back further than the town has records, each with a mark beside it — the same mark, over and over, made carefully every time. A woman sweeps between the benches. She does not ask what you want, and when you tell her, she says only that you should bring them in out of the weather first.*

**Referee Overview:** Thirty paces by twelve, oriented east to west, doors east onto the square. A side door at the west end opens onto the head of the drowned's stair — sixty steps cut into the slope, steep, wet, no cargo ever carried on them — descending to the boat landing. Two attendants, no hierarchy. The temple keeps a cold room off the nave where bodies wait, and it takes them without asking who they were.

**Features:**

* **The Drowned's Wall:** every name the river has taken, cut in rows, each with the same mark beside it. The mark is a rite, not a decoration: the name is spoken, the mark is cut, and the dead is considered settled. Nobody here calls this a mechanism and it is the working model of what the hold's dead expect. A party that has read this wall and then meets `A.20` has already been told the answer.
* **The Cold Room:** off the nave, north. The temple will receive a body without questions and will do the rite for anyone, paid or not. It is also, in practice, the only place in the region that can hold a corpse for a week.
* **The Attendants:** two, unarmed, uninterested in adventuring, and completely unshockable. They know what has been brought in from the outlying holdings this season and they will say, plainly, that none of the four were drowned.
* **Exits:** the square, east -> `A.2` · the drowned's stair, sixty steps down at the west end -> `A.8`

**Connections:** `A.2` the square · `A.8` the boat landing, by the drowned's stair — sixty steps cut into the slope, the shortest way down and the one nobody uses for cargo.

### `A.6 The Kingfisher` — Upper-Town Inn, Respectable, Where the Charter Drinks

*Player's Overview: Clean floor, banked fire, beds with blankets on them, and a bird painted on the sign that nobody has let fade. The common room is half full of people who are plainly an expedition — roped kit stacked by the wall in an order somebody enforces, mules heard through the back, a man at the corner table drawing a line on a chart and rubbing it out again.*

**Referee Overview:** Two storeys, six rooms above, common room below, stable and mule yard behind. One door, on the square. Prices are upper-town and worth it. This is the only Rest in the module taken in a SAFE region: a day here, and everything recovers — Combat, Skill and Magic Dice alike. Nowhere else in Drakenhold does that.

**Features:**

* **The Beds:** a full day's Rest, and the whole pool comes back. The party will not meet this again after they leave the region, and the module never tells them so.
* **The Charter's Table:** the Grellick Charter lodges here whenever it is down from the crossing — twelve strong, chartered, methodical, paying on time. Their surveyor will talk to anyone who buys the second round, and what he has is a purchased partial survey of the Outer City. They are entirely willing to hire the party, and equally willing to renegotiate the split afterward.
* **The One Door:** the landlord likes knowing who is in his house, and so does the Charter. Nobody gets in the back way, because there is no back way -> `A.2`

**Connections:** `A.2` the square. One door, on the square, and the Charter likes it that way.

### `A.7 The Shuttered Trade Houses` — Saplings Through the Boards, Locked Attics, Dwarven Partners

*Player's Overview: Nine tall houses in a row down the slope, and seven of them are shut. Good houses — carved door-frames, painted lintels, the kind of frontage built by people expecting to be looked at. Ash saplings have come up through the porch boards of two, and one has come up through a window. The two houses still lived in are lived in by very old people.*

**Referee Overview:** The row runs south-west from the square's corner downslope, backing onto the lanes above the warehouses. Nine houses; two occupied, five shuttered but maintained by descendants, two derelict. These were the families who traded with Kelmordun for six generations, and their money left with the road. Nothing here is guarded and nothing here is for sale in any ordinary sense.

**Features:**

* **The Attics:** locked, forgotten rather than hidden, and holding the settled residue of a trade partnership — carved tiles, House rods, ledger fragments, cups, tally-sticks. Nobody has been up there in thirty years. Obtainable by asking, by buying, by marrying into, or by being the sort of people the old families decide to tell. Theft works and is the single fastest way to make the whole upper town stop talking to a party.
* **The Old Families:** two households, both elderly, both proud, both perfectly clear that they were partners and not customers. They will not sell heirlooms to strangers. They will give things to people who ask about the hold as though it had been a place where people lived.
* **The Door-Frames:** the carving on the surviving frontages is dwarven-cut, commissioned, and signed. It is the same hand as the palisade blocks and it was paid for rather than stolen — which is a distinction a party can see from the street.
* **Exits:** the square, at the head of the row -> `A.2` · the back lanes, downslope to the warehouses -> `A.10`

**Connections:** `A.2` the square, at the head of the row · `A.10` the idle warehouses, by the back lanes behind the trade houses.

**Wharf quarter**

### `A.8 The Boat Landing` — The Way Downriver, Specialist Goods, A Clock

*Player's Overview: Three jetties out into brown water, two of them sound. The stone under the boards is not the same stone as anything else here — long dressed courses running out into the river, square-cut, older than the timber by a very long way. A dozen men sit along the edge waiting to be hired for the day, and they are still there at noon.*

**Referee Overview:** The town's working front, running north to south along the water below the wharf quarter. The drowned's stair comes down at the north end from the temple; Skeed's office stands at the landing head; the warehouse row runs north along the water and the Sunken Oar south. Boats come up from downriver roughly every ten days in season and less in winter.

**Features:**

* **The Dwarven Courses:** the jetties are footed on a dwarven landing — dressed stone running out into the river on a line the current has not moved in eight centuries. This is where the palisade blocks were quarried from, by prising them up. The town has been dismantling its own foundation for three generations and calls it a stone supply.
* **Sending Downriver:** anything skilled, specialist or dwarven-made is not bought here, it is ordered. Three weeks minimum, longer in winter, paid in advance. The party will feel this as a delay at exactly the moment it hurts, and they should meet it as a plain fact now, in daylight, with nothing at stake.
* **The Hiring Line:** men waiting for a day's work — `Camp Hand`, in numbers. Muleteers, porters, a cook, somebody's boy. They will carry, dig and hold a rope, and every one of them has heard what is up that road. They do not go inside. They do not always run in a useful direction.
* **Exits:** the drowned's stair, up to the temple -> `A.5` · Skeed's office at the landing head -> `A.9` · the warehouse row, north along the water -> `A.10` · the Sunken Oar, south -> `A.11`

**Connections:** `A.5` the drowned's stair, up to the temple · `A.9` Skeed's office, at the landing head · `A.10` the warehouse row, north along the water · `A.11` the Sunken Oar, south along the water.

### `A.9 Skeed's Wharf Office` — Twenty Years of Manifests, Ganner Skeed, Who Came and Who Did Not

*Player's Overview: A plank office the size of a stable, hot from a stove that is always lit, and papered floor to ceiling with paper. Manifests, mostly, hung on nails in bundles by year. The wharfmaster does not get up either, but he does put down his pen, and he asks your names before you have offered them, and he writes them down.*

**Referee Overview:** At the head of the landing, one room, stove, desk, twenty years of manifests on the walls in order. Skeed is fifty, sharp, unsentimental, and the closest thing the region has to an archive. He is not secretive; he simply requires that a question be worth his afternoon.

**Features:**

* **The Manifests:** every boat up the river for twenty years and every crew that walked off one and up the road. Cross-read against the arrivals, they are a register of who went into Drakenhold — and, by simple subtraction, of who has not come out. Ashen's Crew are on it. The Grellick Charter are on it. So is every party the Referee wants to have gone before.
* **Ganner Skeed:** he writes names down because he has spent twenty years being the last person to write some of them anywhere. He will trade freely with anyone who tells him where they are going and lets him record it, and he will tell them, flatly and without any attempt at persuasion, how many of the names above theirs came back down.
* **The Manifest Yard:** hard standing behind the office where cargo is checked against paper before it goes into the sheds -> `A.10`
* **Exits:** the landing -> `A.8` · the manifest yard, through to the warehouse row -> `A.10`

**Connections:** `A.8` the landing · `A.10` the warehouse row, through the manifest yard.

### `A.10 The Idle Warehouses` — Doors Unopened Since the Road Closed, Rot and Rope, Squatters

*Player's Overview: A row of sheds along the water, seven of them, and only the first two have tracks in the mud outside. The rest have doors swollen shut in their frames. Through a gap in the boards of one there is a stack of crates still roped in place on a pallet, and the rope has gone green, and the stencil on the crates is a mark none of you can read.*

**Referee Overview:** Seven sheds running north from the landing, backing onto the lanes that climb to the trade houses. Two in use, one squatted, four sealed since the road closed. Doors face west onto the boards. The sealed sheds are not locked so much as abandoned; the town would rather nobody made this its business.

**Features:**

* **The Sealed Sheds:** an economy that stopped mid-sentence. Cargo still palletted and roped, consigned to houses in the hold, waiting for a cart that never came. Most of it has rotted into nothing. Some of it is metal, dwarven-marked, consigned and paid for and never delivered — which makes its ownership genuinely unclear and makes selling it in this town a social act.
* **The Squatters:** transients, off-season boatmen, and two people hiding from something downriver. They know which sheds have been opened recently and by whom, and they will say so for food rather than coin.
* **The Back Lanes:** the climb to the trade houses, steep, unlit, and the way the upper town gets to the wharf without being seen doing it -> `A.7`
* **Exits:** the trade houses, up the back lanes -> `A.7` · the landing -> `A.8` · the manifest yard -> `A.9` · the Sunken Oar, along the boards -> `A.11`

**Connections:** `A.7` the trade houses, up the back lanes · `A.8` the landing · `A.9` the manifest yard · `A.11` the Sunken Oar, along the boards.

### `A.11 The Sunken Oar` — Drinking Hall, Rough Custom, Ashen's Crew Are Known Here

*Player's Overview: The floor is below the level of the boards outside and the whole room smells of it. Long benches, a plank bar, a fire that draws badly. It is loud, and it goes quiet in patches when strangers come in, and it comes back up again. A man at the end of the bar has been watching the door since before you opened it.*

**Referee Overview:** Half-sunk into the bank at the south end of the landing, one long room, benches, no rooms above. Rough beds in the back for coin. Carousing happens here and nowhere else in the region. Rumours told in the Oar run harder, further and less true than the same rumours told in the square, which is a difference a party can learn to use.

**Features:**

* **The Benches:** Carousing, and the full consequence table that goes with it. Downtime spent here buys rumours the upper town will not repeat, at the price of being remembered by people worth avoiding.
* **Ashen's Crew:** seven treasure hunters, no charter, no patience. They are unwelcome here and drink here anyway. Two of them can read runic script. They buy rope, oil and silence, they ask the same questions the party is asking, and they leave before dawn. Anyone who follows them up the road is following people who are already ahead.
* **What Is Said Down Here:** the rumours that circulate in the Oar are the setting's advertising at its least reliable — the wyrm is dead, the King's name opens every door, the third peak was never touched. All of it goes out from this room and some of it is true.
* **Exits:** the landing -> `A.8` · the warehouse row, along the boards -> `A.10`

**Connections:** `A.8` the landing · `A.10` the warehouse row.

**Outskirts**

### `A.12 The Cutting Ground` — Fresh Stumps, The Live Feud, Timber for the Palisade

*Player's Overview: The wood's edge has been pushed back a hundred paces along a front the width of a field, and the stumps are fresh and pale. They stop. Not at a fence, not at a ditch — they simply stop, in a line as straight as a drawn cord, and beyond that line the trees go on as though nobody had ever thought of touching them. A saw lies where it was dropped. Nobody has been back for it.*

**Referee Overview:** An hour east of the gate along the treeline. Cut ground roughly two hundred paces across, stumps and brash, hauling ruts running back toward town. The cutting front stops dead on a line running north to south — Fenn's boundary, marked, and marked plainly enough that crossing it is a choice rather than an accident. The same line meets the Ironwood Trail further north-west at `B.13`. The field track to Ketter's holding leaves south.

**Features:**

* **The Line:** her boundary, marked at intervals with cut and bound saplings, at head height, unmistakable and entirely unfenced. It is a statement rather than a barrier. Everything the town has done wrong here is on the town's side of it and visible from the town's side of it.
* **The Dropped Tools:** a saw, a wedge, a jacket over a stump with the sleeves still turned back. The last crew cut past the line and came out one short, and the town's account stops there. **The module does not say what became of him and does not intend to** — the evidence is a saw left mid-cut, a jacket nobody came back for, and a crew who will talk about the first of those and not the second. Whether he is dead, walked out somewhere else, or is still in there is the Referee's to develop, and Fenn will not be drawn on it either way.
* **The Feud, and Its Price:** the town needs palisade timber and takes it from her wood. She has warned them twice. This is winnable — by cutting elsewhere, by paying, by hauling stone from the landing instead — and every one of those answers costs the town something Marrow will have to be argued into.
* **Exits:** the gate, an hour west -> `A.1` · the grove, a quarter hour further along the line -> `A.13` · the field track south to the farmstead -> `A.14` · the same boundary where it meets the trail -> `B.13`

**Connections:** `A.1` the gate, an hour east · `A.13` Wyla Fenn's grove, a quarter hour further along the wood's edge — the stumps stop where her boundary starts · `A.14` the farmstead, by the field track south · `B.13` Fenn's boundary as the Ironwood Trail meets it, the same line approached from the other side.

### `A.13 Wyla Fenn's Grove` — Runemaster-Trained, A Boundary Marked Plainly, The Only Living Witness

*Player's Overview: Old trees, spaced as though someone had planned them, and a low house among them with a stone step. The step is dwarven — square-cut, letter-cut, and the letters have been worn shallow by one person's feet over a very long time. She is at the door before you reach it. She is not old. She looks at what you are carrying, and at what you are wearing, and she says: you have come from the town, and I have nothing to sell you.*

**Referee Overview:** A quarter hour along the boundary from the cutting ground, an hour and a quarter from the gate. Perhaps forty old trees standing in worked ground, a turf-roofed house, drying racks, a spring. She is human by every appearance and was trained by the Runemasters of the Third Peak, which is a thing that happened and which she will not discuss with anyone who arrives as a customer. She left Drakenhold before the Breaking finished and has been here since, living as a hedge-witch because there is nowhere else for her to be — not hiding, and not waiting for anything either.

**Features:**

* **The Step:** dwarven, carried here by her, and cut with a Runemaster's mark. It is the only thing on the property that says what she is, and it is under her front door where every visitor stands on it.
* **What She Knows:** the only living account of what happened inside Peak 3 — the Sanctuary, the breaking of the artifact, what came off the mountain afterward and in what order. She gives none of it for coin, for rescue, for threat, or for the town's goodwill, and she is candid that the town's goodwill is worth nothing to her.
* **Her Price:** one object, still inside the mountain, in the rooms where the Runemasters wrote down what they thought of their initiates. She was failed there, for the wrong reasons, and the written assessment of that trial is filed where it was filed. It is a plaque of no value whatever to anyone who does not already know what it is, and a party she has not talked to will walk straight past it. She will not describe it well enough to be found by anyone who has not earned the description. It is at `HD.13`.
* **The Boundary:** hers, and she watches both approaches — the town side and the trail side. A party that has already crossed her line on the trail arrives here with a history it did not know it was building.
* **Exits:** the cutting ground -> `A.12` · her boundary where the trail meets it -> `B.13`

**Connections:** `A.12` the cutting ground · `B.13` her boundary on the trail side. The grove has two approaches and she watches both.

### `A.14 The Farmyard` — Abandoned Mid-Season, Neighbours Watching From Afar, Nightly Visitors

*Player's Overview: A yard, a house with its door standing open, a byre with the beasts gone out of it, and a crop in the field beyond that nobody has cut. It is the wrong time of year for that. Up on the rise half a mile off, three people are standing where they can see you and no closer, and they have been standing there since you came over the track.*

**Referee Overview:** Half an hour inland from the gate by the field track, or south from the cutting ground by the same track. Yard forty paces across, house north, byre east, midden and well south. The house door stands open. In the yard's west corner, under a sagging cover, is a plank hatch set flush in the ground — the cold store's old loading way, barred from beneath. The neighbouring families will come no closer than the rise and will answer questions shouted across it.

**Features:**

* **The Uncut Crop:** the Ketters left in high summer with the harvest standing, and the yard says the same thing in six ways — a full water butt, tools left out, a byre unbarred from the inside. They were not driven out in a night. They left over about a week, and they took the cart.
* **The Watchers on the Rise:** three of the four remaining outlying families, who will not set foot on this ground after dark and are increasingly unwilling to before it. They know what comes up out of the yard at night — but only if the relics have been disturbed. A party that arrives before that has happened is investigating a desertion. A party that arrives after is investigating a killing, and the difference is entirely in the timing.
* **The Yard Hatch:** planks over a stone-lined shaft in the west corner, barred from beneath, the loading way the cold store was built around. It opens by force from above, by the bar being lifted from below, or by whatever is coming up it -> `A.17`
* **Exits:** the gate, half an hour by the field track -> `A.1` · the cutting ground, north by the same track -> `A.12` · the farmhouse door, standing open -> `A.15` · the yard hatch -> `A.17`

**Connections:** `A.1` the gate, half an hour by the field track · `A.12` the cutting ground, by the same track north · `A.15` the farmhouse door, standing open · `A.17` the cold store, by the outside hatch in the yard — the loading way, still barred from within.

### `A.15 The Farmhouse` — Interrupted Household, The Family That Dug, What Is Gone For Good

*Player's Overview: Four rooms and a hearth with cold ash in it. The table is laid for a meal that was eaten. On the dresser, in a row, are three things that do not belong in a farmhouse: a cup of grey metal with letters around the rim, a stone disc the size of a palm, and a child's carving of an animal, worn smooth. Beside them is a folded paper, and the paper is a receipt.*

**Referee Overview:** Kitchen, two sleeping rooms, store. Door south to the yard. Under the kitchen boards, at the north-west corner, a stair descends — and the boards over it were lifted and never properly put back. The Ketters were four: two adults, two children. They are alive, downriver, and they are not coming back into this module.

**Features:**

* **The Dresser:** three pieces of dwarven grave-goods, kept back because somebody in the family could not bear to sell them. They are the smallest and least valuable of what was taken and they are what makes the laying-to-rest possible.
* **The Receipt:** a buyer's note, dated, itemised, signed, for eleven pieces sold downriver three months ago. The eleven are gone — to a city this module does not visit, to a buyer who is not coming back, and no amount of play recovers them. The dead can still be settled without them. The lesson is that some losses are simply permanent, and it is taught here, cheaply, before the party is anywhere that would charge properly for it.
* **The Lifted Boards:** the kitchen floor at the north-west corner, opened and left half-covered, and the reason the cold air moves through this house -> `A.16`
* **Exits:** the farmyard -> `A.14` · the cellar stair -> `A.16`

**Connections:** `A.14` the farmyard · `A.16` the cellar stair, under the kitchen boards.

### `A.16 The Cellar Stair` — Cut Too Wide, Older Than the House, First Dwarven Cutting

*Player's Overview: The stair down is too good. Eleven steps of dressed stone, each one the same as the last to the width of a fingernail, running down into cold air — and the house above it is timber and clay and forty years old. Somebody built a farmhouse on top of a staircase and then built a kitchen floor over the top of that.*

**Referee Overview:** Eleven steps, four feet wide, descending north-west from the kitchen to the cold store. Dressed grey stone, the same stone as the palisade footing and the landing courses. It predates the house by eight centuries and the Ketters simply used it. Cold air rises constantly, which is why the family put their store at the bottom and why the house never held its heat.

**Features:**

* **The Cutting:** dwarven work, unmistakable to anyone who looked at the gate or the well or the landing on the way in — and this is the third time the region has shown a party the same stone. The town's ground is full of it. Nobody in the town has drawn the conclusion.
* **The Draught:** the cold comes up the stair and it does not come from the cold store. It comes from further in, past the west wall, and it moves.
* **Exits:** the kitchen above -> `A.15` · the cold store at the foot -> `A.17`

**Connections:** `A.15` the kitchen above · `A.17` the cold store at the stair's foot.

### `A.17 The Cold Store` — Provisions Gone Over, The Breach in the Wall, Cold Air From Below

*Player's Overview: A stone room stacked with the ruin of a winter's food — barrels gone over, a cheese with its rind grey through, hams still hanging and long past. Above, a hatch to the yard, barred with a beam. And in the west wall, at the back, someone has knocked a hole through good masonry with a pick, and stacked the rubble tidily against the wall beside it, and the hole is dark, and the cold is coming out of it.*

**Referee Overview:** Twelve paces by eight, stone-lined, at the stair's foot. Hatch in the ceiling at the east end opens to the yard, barred from within by a beam any adult can lift. The west wall is dressed masonry to shoulder height with a breach in it three feet across, opened from this side with a pick, the tool marks fresh and the rubble stacked rather than scattered — the work of someone who was curious rather than desperate.

**Features:**

* **The Breach:** the origin of everything that has happened since. The Ketters broke through a wall they did not build, into a gallery they had no claim on, and took what was inside it. The masonry they went through is deliberate, dressed on both faces, and was sealing rather than supporting.
* **The Barred Hatch:** barred from this side, which means the last person out of this cellar came up the house stair and left the loading way shut behind them. Lift the beam and the yard is a second way in and a second way out -> `A.14`
* **The Store:** a season's provisions, all of it spoiled. Worth nothing, and worth reading: they laid in for a winter they intended to spend here.
* **Exits:** the cellar stair -> `A.16` · the yard hatch above -> `A.14` · the breach, west -> `A.18`

**Connections:** `A.16` the cellar stair · `A.14` the yard hatch above · `A.18` the first gallery, through the breach the Ketters made in the west wall.

### `A.18 The First Gallery` — Refugee Interment, Names in Two Scripts, Grave-Goods Disturbed

*Player's Overview: The hole opens into a room that was never meant to be entered this way. Low, long, running west, with shelves cut into both walls at three heights, and a body laid in most of them wrapped and dressed. In front of each is a stone the size of a hand, and each stone is cut with a name — some in letters you can read, and some in the square deep letters you have been walking past since the gate. Six of the shelves nearest the breach have been rifled. The wrappings are pulled back. The stones in front of those six are face down.*

**Referee Overview:** Forty paces east to west, seven feet high, shelved on both walls. Thirty-one interments, six of them disturbed. These are Drakenhold refugees who came down the road in the year after the descent and died within eighteen months, buried by their own survivors in ground the dwarves already owned. Four cut steps descend north at the western end to the lower niches. The west wall of the gallery is dressed stone and continues past where the shelving stops, which is where the walled processional way runs.

**Features:**

* **The Name-Stones:** every interment named, and the older hand is the same script as the waystones and the palisade. Read across the whole gallery they are a list of the last people out of the mountain, with their houses. Two of them are Kelmor. This is where a party learns, at no cost and with nothing chasing them, that names cut in stone are worth reading.
* **The Six Disturbed:** the Ketters took from six and stopped. The stones lie face down in front of those six, which is not carelessness — one of the Ketters turned them over deliberately, and turned them over before the taking rather than after.
* **The Direction on the Stones:** the older inscriptions do not only name. They orient: each names the direction of the one who led them, and every one of them points west, past where the shelving ends and into blank wall. The processional way behind that wall was walled and dressed to match the gallery, and a party that reads rather than loots is told exactly where it is -> `A.20`
* **Exits:** the breach, east -> `A.17` · four cut steps down, north-west -> `A.19` · the walled processional way, west — **hidden** -> `A.20`

**Connections:** `A.17` the breach · `A.19` the lower niches, down four cut steps north · `A.20` Brannek Kelmor's grave — **hidden**. The processional way runs straight to it and was walled and dressed to match; the gallery stones name the direction for anyone who reads them.

### `A.19 The Lower Niches` — Deeper Cutting, Undisturbed, What the Ketters Did Not Reach

*Player's Overview: Four steps down and the air changes. This run is older and better cut than the gallery above, and it is entirely undisturbed — every niche closed, every stone upright, the dust across the floor unbroken except by whatever small things live down here. There is nothing here to take. There is a great deal here to look at.*

**Referee Overview:** Twenty-five paces, running west, ceiling six feet, niches at two heights, all sealed. Fourteen interments, none opened. The run ends at the grave chamber. The Ketters came down the four steps, looked, and went back up — the floor dust records exactly that, one set of prints in and the same set out.

**Features:**

* **The Sealed Niches:** intact, respectful, and worth nothing whatever to a looter. That is the point, and it is delivered by withholding: the module's first genuinely rich-looking room contains no treasure at all, and the party that opens all fourteen learns it the long way.
* **The Prints:** in and out, one man, no hesitation and no second visit. Whatever stopped the Ketters here, it was not fear of what was ahead — the grave chamber was still walled when they came, and they simply found nothing worth the labour.
* **Exits:** the gallery, up four steps -> `A.18` · the chamber at the end of the run, west -> `A.20`

**Connections:** `A.18` the first gallery · `A.20` Brannek Kelmor's grave, at the end of the niche run.

### `A.20 The Grave of Brannek Kelmor` — The Name That Settles It, Laid With Care, The First Key

*Player's Overview: A chamber at the end, and a single interment in it, and everything about it says that the people who did this were doing it for the last time and knew it. The stone is dressed. The name is cut deep and cut well by someone working with a bad tool and enough time. Around him they left what they had — not much, and all of it useful, and none of it hidden. The floor in front of the stone is worn in a way that no floor in a sealed room should be.*

**Referee Overview:** Twelve paces square, at the western end of the niche run, and the eastern end of a walled processional way that runs back to the gallery. One interment, dressed stone, deep inscription. The wear on the floor in front of the stone is thirty years of the same handful of people standing in the same place, and it stops abruptly — the last of them died, or left, and nobody has stood there since.

**Features:**

* **The Name:** **Brannek** — *bran* and *mek*, store and work, a name that was a job before it was a person. Kelmor, of the Trade houses, of the same clan as the two names in the gallery above. He was a stores clerk in Peak 1 and he led the refugees out of the mountain, and they buried him with what they still had.
* **The Laying to Rest:** the dead of this interment are settled by returning what remains of what was taken and speaking his name at this stone — the same rite, exactly, that the temple performs on the drowned's wall, which the party has probably already watched being done. The eleven pieces sold downriver are not required and are not recoverable. Force works, badly, and is meant to: burning or breaking the interments ends the nightly killings and closes every other door in this paragraph.
* **What the Name Opens Later:** it has two further lives, and the module never says so. It is cut on the black obelisk outside the Celebration Hall of Peak 3, small and easily missed among the honoured dead — a party that buried him will recognise it thirty sessions later. And it opens a stores tally-niche in the servants' warren of Peak 1, his own, holding a cup, a tally-stick and a child's carving and nothing of value at all. Names are keys. This is where the module says so for the first time, at the cheapest possible price, and it is `FA.22` that proves it.
* **Exits:** the niche run, east -> `A.19` · the walled processional way, east to the gallery — **hidden** from that side -> `A.18`

**Connections:** `A.19` the niche run · `A.18` the walled processional way, **hidden** from the gallery side.

**Reading the diagram.** Solid edges are open passage. The dotted edge `A.18`–`A.20` is hidden: the processional way from the gallery to the grave was walled and dressed to match, and the gallery stones name the direction for anyone who reads them. Every other edge in the region is open, on foot, in daylight, without a roll.

**What the drawing found.**

- **Two ways into the cellar.** The stubs implied one — house, stair, cold store — which would have made the farmstead a corridor with a single throat. The yard hatch (`A.14`–`A.17`) is the answer that is not the gate: it is the cold store's old loading way, barred from within, and it is opened by force, by the breach from the gallery side, or by whatever comes up it at night once the relics are disturbed. The house route is still the obvious one and still the one that shows the party the Ketters' record at `A.15` first.
- **Two ways to the grave.** The niche run (`A.19`–`A.20`) is walked; the walled processional way (`A.18`–`A.20`) is read. A party that loots the gallery and turns back never learns the second one exists.
- **Two approaches to the grove.** `A.12` from the town side and `B.13` from the trail side are the same boundary met from opposite directions, and Wyla Fenn watches both. A party that has already crossed her line on the trail arrives at the cutting ground with a history.
- **`A.6` is a single-edge leaf and stays one.** It is an inn with one door on the square. That is not an unreachable location; it is a building.
- **The square is the region's only hub.** Six edges. Everything upper-town routes through `A.2`, which is why Gather Information lives there and why the region reads as small.

**What the outlines found, and what the reconciliation changed.**

- **No edge was added, moved or retyped.** The finished outlines generate no passage the stubs did not anticipate, which is unusual and is a property of this region rather than a result to expect elsewhere: `A` is a town, its geography is public, and it has exactly one concealed route in it. The step-8 diagram stands as drawn and is now the reconciled one.
- **Two places where the prose reached for an edge and did not get one.** The temple's cold room receives bodies from the outlying holdings, and the trade-house attics are reached by asking rather than by walking. Both are errands along edges that already exist — `A.14`→`A.1`→`A.2`→`A.5`, and `A.2`→`A.7` — and neither is passage. They are recorded here so that a later pass does not draw them.
- **The dwarven stone became a chain rather than a motif.** The palisade footing at `A.1`, the well at `A.2`, the landing courses at `A.8` and the cellar stair at `A.16` are the same stone shown four times before anything explains it, and the inverted lintel at `A.1` carries the town's own unused name. That chain lands at `B.8`, where the waystone names Ostgir in full. It is a clue chain across a region boundary and it costs neither region an edge.
- **The rite is taught before it is needed.** The drowned's wall at `A.5` performs, in public and for free, the same rite that settles the dead at `A.20` — name spoken, mark made. J3's requirement that a clue live outside the location holding the secret is satisfied twice over here: at `A.5`, and on the name-stones at `A.18` that point west into blank wall.

**Route ends recorded, not assumed.** Two edges leave region A for locations in B: `A.1`–`B.1` (the road, already named in `## CONNECTIONS`) and the `A.12`/`A.13`–`B.13` pair at Fenn's boundary. Both were closed from B's side at B's step 8 pass and both are drawn identically there. Nothing in this region is now held open at one end.
