# `GB Karmor, Peak 2 Under Level Prison and Barracks`

**Classification:** DANGEROUS · **Difficulty Die:** d10 · **Tags:** The Last Stand, Fighting Arena, Deep Cells

*Region file. Companion to the Drakenhold Setting Outline and the Setting Relational Diagram. Tables are authored at the location-stub pass (procedure step 7); location stubs, outlines and the region relational diagram follow in the engineer phase.*

---


**Overview:** Two regions sharing a floor, and the hold's worst decisions kept in one place. Half is militia housing and training, centred on a sandy fighting arena ringed with stone benches, with administration, an armoury and planning rooms close in and soldiers' quarters further out. The other half is prison — central branching corridors of tightly packed cells, and beyond them the deep cells. The Armory is where the loyalists made their last stand, and Skeletal Warriors still hold that formation across the arena floor in ranks that have not broken in thirty-two years, with Karn Rudgir's wraith among them, willing to parley for anyone who addresses the formation correctly rather than charging it. The deep cells hold three things: an Elder Wyrm taken alive during the expansion and kept for study, which is how the Runemasters learned to build the yoke at all; an oath-bound wraith jailer who cannot leave and will not open the doors; and a Runemaster imprisoned before the schism who knew what the artifact truly was. He slipped his bonds long ago and went mad, and he has never left, and he is still down there talking.

**Ambiance:** Sand still on the arena floor, raked into ridges by thirty-two years of draught. Stone benches in tiers. The barracks half smells of old iron and old bedding; the prison half smells of nothing at all, which is worse. From very far down, at irregular intervals, a voice.

**Layout:** Two halves sharing a floor. The barracks half centres on a sandy fighting arena ringed with stone benches, with administration, an armoury and planning rooms close in and soldiers' quarters further out. The prison half runs as central branching corridors of tightly packed cells, and beyond and below them the deep cells, hidden and separately gated. The stairwell up to GA. Around forty rooms.

**Features:** The formation. Skeletal Warriors hold the last stand across the arena floor in ranks that have not broken in thirty-two years, with Karn Rudgir's wraith among them — willing to parley for anyone who addresses the formation correctly rather than charging it. The Armory behind them, which is what they died holding. The deep cells, which are the hold's three worst decisions kept in one place.

**Dangers:** The formation, which is a battle nobody wins and a conversation anyone can have. The deep cells, individually and in what happens if more than one of them is opened.

**Creatures:** Skeletal Warriors in ranks. Karn Rudgir, Dwarven Wraith, Leader of the Guard. In the deep cells: an Elder Wyrm taken alive during the expansion and kept for study, which is how the Runemasters learned to build the yoke at all; an oath-bound wraith jailer who cannot leave and will not open the doors; and a Runemaster imprisoned before the schism who knew what the artifact truly was, slipped his bonds long ago, went mad, never left, and is still down there talking.

**Secrets:** That the yoke was learned here, from something still alive and still in the building. What Karn Rudgir will say about his sister the Steward, and about the King's death, to someone who addresses him as an officer. What the mad Runemaster is right about, which is more than he is wrong about.

**Treasure:** The Armory, intact behind the formation — the best conventional arms in the hold. The prison holds confiscated goods never returned.

## LOCATION STUBS

*Procedure step 7. Code, name and three thematic tags per location. The working note under each is scaffolding for step 8. Block-level material — the tribute chain, Valdgir, the wake conditions and the room budget — lives in `PEAK_2_BLOCK.md`, which covers GB, GC, GD and GE together.*

*40 rooms budgeted; **20 are stubbed as locations** and the balance is unnamed fill inside the stated groupings — bunk rows, kit rooms, empty cells by the dozen, the ordinary rooms of a garrison with the garrison taken out.*

**The barracks half**

### `GB.1 The Stairwell Foot` — Sand on the Bottom Steps, No Sound, Iron Smell
*Working note: the level announces itself with arena sand carried up thirty-two years of draught. The quiet here is not the quiet of the Trade Hall. Something is holding still.*

### `GB.2 The Arena` — Sand Raked Into Ridges, Tiered Benches, Built for Watching
*Working note: a training floor the whole hold came to watch. Open ground with no cover and no flank, which was a virtue when it was built and is the level's central problem now.*

### `GB.3 The Formation` — Ranks Unbroken Thirty-Two Years, Dressed and Holding, Facing the Armoury Door
*Working note: the last stand, still standing. Skeletal Warriors in order, not shambling — drilled soldiers still holding a line, indifferent to cost. They do not attack anything that does not advance on what they are holding.*

### `GB.4 Karn Rudgir` — Leader of the Guard, Among His Own Ranks, Will Answer an Officer
*Working note: a Dwarven Wraith who died holding this door and has not left it. Addressed as an officer rather than charged, he parleys — and what he has is the King's death from the inside, and his sister the Steward, whom he will not discuss and cannot stop discussing.*

### `GB.5 The Armoury` — What They Died Holding, Intact, The Best Arms in the Hold
*Working note: the conventional prize of Peak 2 and the most useful room in the module for a party that intends to survive the upper levels. Behind the formation, which is the price.*

### `GB.6 The Muster Office` — Rolls, Rosters, Who Was On the Wall That Night
*Working note: names in quantity, which in this setting is currency. It also records who was *not* on the wall, and that list is short and interesting.*

### `GB.7 The Planning Rooms` — Cut Maps of the Whole Interior, Accurate, Thirty-Two Years Old
*Working note: **Peak 2 only.** The garrison mapped its own peak in detail and held the other two as summary sheets that did not survive the burning. What remains is right about the parts nobody has touched and confidently wrong about the parts that killed people — the same problem as the surveyor's satchel at `C.9`, at the scale of one peak rather than the hold.*

### `GB.8 The Soldiers' Quarters` — Ranks of Bunks, Kit Left Ready, Nobody Came Off Duty
*Working note: the human cost at the scale of personal property. Little of value and a great deal that is specific.*

### `GB.9 The Benches` — Where the Hold Sat, Names Scratched Under the Seats, Ordinary Lives
*Working note: thirty generations of graffiti under the tiers, which is the informal name-index of the hold and includes hands that appear nowhere official — Torvin Ganthur among them, twice, as a young man.*

### `GB.10 The Fallen Ground` — Where the Line Broke Once, Bodies Never Moved, Both Sides
*Working note: the dead of the civil war lying where they fell, in two liveries, and the formation has walked around them for thirty-two years without disturbing them. Who was on which side is legible and does not match the story told upstairs.*

**The prison half**

### `GB.11 The Prison Gate` — Rod-Locked, Still Locked, Locked From Inside
*Working note: the detail that reframes the half. It was secured from within, in the last days, by somebody who then stayed.*

### `GB.12 The Cell Corridors` — Branching, Tightly Packed, Mostly Empty
*Working note: the level's negative space and its most oppressive ground. Dozens of cells and almost nothing in them, which is the point — the fear is the walk, not the contents.*

### `GB.13 The Confiscation Store` — Goods Taken and Never Returned, Catalogued, Owners Named
*Working note: property with names attached, which makes taking it a decision rather than a haul. Among it, at least one thing whose owner is still alive in the warren.*

### `GB.14 The Register` — Every Prisoner, Every Charge, One Entry Without a Charge
*Working note: the prison's own record, complete. The uncharged entry is Morgrin Thurvak, held before the schism on no stated grounds and on somebody's authority the register declines to name.*

### `GB.15 The Jailer's Rod` — Not Where It Should Be, Not Far, Still Held
*Working note: the deep cells' key, and the wraith below has it. It is not hidden and it cannot be found — it can only be given.*

**The deep cells**

### `GB.16 The Descent` — Rod-Gated, Below the Prison, On No Plan
*Working note: separately gated and separately built, in a plainer hand than the prison above it. `GA.25`'s older course surfaces here, in the one place in the hold where it should not be — whoever cut these cells was working in the founding lineage's manner, nine hundred years after the founding lineage stopped being named.*

### `GB.17 The Jailer` — Oath-Bound, Cannot Leave, Will Not Open the Doors
*Working note: a Dwarven Wraith holding a post nobody has relieved him of. He is not hostile, he is not mad, and he is the most reasonable person on the level. He will discuss anything, at length, and he will not open a door. Releasing him from the oath is the level's real puzzle and there are two ways to do it. **The lie is telling him the hold has fallen and there is nothing left to guard** — true in every sense except the one that matters, since the thing he is guarding is very much still down there, and it works. The honest release is slower and is not written yet.*

### `GB.18 The Talking Cell` — Morgrin Thurvak, Bonds Slipped Long Ago, Never Left
*Working note: a Runemaster imprisoned before the schism because he knew what the artifact truly was. He could have walked out at any point in thirty years and has not. He is mad and he is right about more than he is wrong about, and separating the two is player work of the hardest and best kind. He is `HE`'s prologue and he will tell a party what is up there if they can stand to listen to him long enough to hear it.*

### `GB.19 The Kept Thing` — An Elder Wyrm Taken Alive, Studied, Still Here
*Working note: the hold's worst decision, in a cell built to hold it. This is where the Runemasters learned to build the yoke — not from a book, from a living thing, over years. It has been in that room since the expansion. It remembers all of it.*

### `GB.20 The Broken Floor` — How It Came Up, How It Could Go Back Down, Nobody Cut This
*Working note: the secret descent into `J`, at the bottom of the deep cells. It is not dwarven work. The route existed before the cell was built around it, which means the dwarves did not capture the wyrm and bring it here. They built a cell over the hole it was already using.*
