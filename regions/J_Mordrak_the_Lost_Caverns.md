# `J Mordrak, the Lost Caverns`

**Classification:** WILD · **Difficulty Die:** d8 · **Tags:** Mythic Underworld, Vast and Quiet, Where the Wyrms Went

*Region file. Companion to the Drakenhold Setting Outline and the Setting Relational Diagram. Tables are authored at the location-stub pass (procedure step 7); location stubs, outlines and the region relational diagram follow in the engineer phase.*

---


**Overview:** WILD by scale rather than by safety. A single great tunnel runs the length of the mountain beneath F, G and H, and off it open six or seven discrete territories of wildly different character — flooded galleries gone to ooze, exhausted dwarven workings, natural cavern, the volcanic vent system that still feeds the forges, the vein the artifact was found in, and the deep dark where the wyrms went when they were driven down. A party can travel it for watches at a time in eerie, mysterious quiet. The danger is concentrated in specific places and specific triggers, and those triggers fire precisely when a party has decided the caverns are harmless. Light is a declaration here and is answered: an open flame below the Under Levels draws what is nearest, and the Referee should let a party learn that from the aftermath of someone else's torch before it learns it from its own. Every peak has both a marked descent and at least one hidden one.

**Ambiance:** Enormous quiet. The great tunnel runs beyond the reach of any light a party carries, and the sound of footsteps goes away and does not come back. Water somewhere. Warm air from one direction and cold from another. This is the mythic underworld and it answers to nothing above it.

**Layout:** A single great tunnel running the length of the mountain beneath F, G and H, with six or seven discrete territories opening off it — flooded galleries gone to ooze, exhausted dwarven workings, natural cavern, the volcanic vent system that still feeds the forges, the vein the artifact was found in, and the deep dark where the wyrms went when they were driven down. Every peak has both a marked descent and at least one hidden one. WILD by scale rather than by safety.

**Features:** Travel itself, for watches at a time in eerie quiet. The vein, which is where the artifact came out of. The vent system, which connects to FB's chimney and to FD's channels and is the mechanism behind every restoration in Peak 1. The territories, each with its own character and its own rules.

**Dangers:** Concentrated in specific places and specific triggers, and those triggers fire precisely when a party has decided the caverns are harmless. Light is a declaration here and is answered — an open flame below the Under Levels draws what is nearest, and the Referee should let a party learn that from the aftermath of someone else's torch before it learns it from its own.

**Creatures:** Lesser Wyrms in the deep tunnels, hunting by vibration and never truly alone. Elder Wyrms in the deep dark, who remember the yoke and remember it breaking and are still angry about both. Oozes in the flooded galleries. Nothing that can be bargained with except the Elder Wyrms, and only by someone who knows what happened in GB.

**Secrets:** Where the vein runs and what else is in it. That the wyrms below and the Drakmorith above are the same story from two ends. The hidden descents, one per peak at least, which are the module's answer to any gate a party cannot open.

**Treasure:** Mined gems and precious metals in the workings, in quantity, heavy. Whatever the dwarves left when they stopped digging in a hurry.

### `J.4 The Sealed Shafts` — Warning Inscription Rather Than Stone, Nine Hundred Years Old, Still Holding
*Working note: from the Waking of the Deep. They sealed with words instead of rock, which is either confidence or a different kind of engineering, and reading them is the region's script reward.*

*Ratified: **one of these inscriptions seals the northern road** — rumour 19's road under the mountain, out the far side of the Broken Teeth, that the dwarves traded down with things that do not have a country. It existed. It was cut off at the Driving Down and it was closed with words rather than with rock like everything else here. **The rumour is half true and the half that is true is readable, not walkable.** The inscription names the road, names what it went to, and states plainly why it was shut — which makes it the region's best script reward and the module's only account of what lies north. **No edge, no stub, nothing passes.** A party that can read it learns that the road is real and that the dwarves were more frightened of what used it than of what they had driven down.*

**Connections:** `J.1` the great tunnel. Sealed with words instead of rock nine hundred years ago, and still holding.

**Reading the diagram.** Solid edges are open cavern and open dwarven cut. Dotted edges are the hidden descents from the peaks and the one way out that is not a way in. **Not one edge in this region is locked**, and the two lines that look like exceptions — the sealed shafts and the outfall — are a warning and a current respectively. The distances are not drawn because they cannot be: every solid edge on this graph is watches long.

**What the drawing found.**

- **`J.1` is a hub with nine edges and a corridor is exactly what it should be.** Anywhere else in the module a region that resolved to one spine with everything hanging off it would be the failure this step exists to catch. Here it is the region: a single tunnel the length of the mountain, six territories opening off it, and travel measured in watches. **The check that would fail any other region passes this one by stating the design out loud.**
- **Every descent lands on the spine, so the region is entered identically from six different places.** `J.2` and `J.3` both hang off `J.1` and off nothing else. Whichever peak a party falls out of — marked or hidden, Peak 1, 2 or 3 — it arrives in the same enormous quiet with the same nine choices in front of it and no idea which way is which. **That is why the light rule can be taught once**, and why `J.6` sits on the spine one edge from the tunnel where it cannot be walked past.
- **`J.16` is the answer to the light rule and the drawing puts it exactly where it belongs.** Warm from one side, cold from the other — and the two things it touches are `J.14`, the vents, and `J.20`, the deep dark. **The region's navigation method is literally drawn as the line between the only two places where light does not matter at all.** A party navigating by skin is walking the edge between the heat that would kill it and the blind old things that cannot see it either way.
- **The vent system and the hidden descents are one thing approached from two ends.** `J.3`–`J.14`. Khorven, the slag channel and the vents are not three facts, they are one system, and a party that comes down the chimney has already been in the territory that restores everything in Peak 1. **The whole chain from Peak 1's prize to Peak 2's crater is walkable and it starts at a descent somebody took to get away from something.**
- **The seventh piece is not on the spine.** `J.1`→`J.10`→`J.17`→`J.18`. The last artifact piece sits two territories deep, behind the ground the region itself describes as *nothing left worth digging*. A party working from `HE.10`'s register has a name and no place; the place is through the one territory the module gives them every reason to skip. **Negative space doing the hardest work it does anywhere in the module.**
- **`J.20` carries six edges and `J.24` is one of them.** The deep dark is the second hub of the region, and hanging off it are the only negotiation available down here, the driving-down seen from the losing side, the bottom of the water, and the place the module declines to describe. **The floor is not the floor, and it is one edge from the only thing down here that will talk.**
- **`J.5` and `J.22` close a loop that is nine hundred years long.** The middens are on the spine, walkable in the first watch; the driven ground is at the bottom, past the deep dark. Wyrm bone stacked as a message, and the ground the message was left about, at opposite ends of the region. A party sees the statement long before it can stand where the statement was made.
- **Nothing in `J` is gated.** Third region running — `GE`, `HE`, and now `J` — where the drawing finds no lock at all. Here the locks are the dark and the distance, and both of them are procedures rather than doors.

**One route drawn outward and priced as steeply as it deserves.** `J.9`–`D.6`, **one-way, hidden, and water the whole way.** `J.9` says plainly that the outfall is where the water goes and that a party thinking sideways will see an exit; the drawing writes it. It comes out below the near bank at the river crossing, two hundred miles of module behind it, and **nothing goes back up it.** *`D` is a closed region and is not amended here: the edge is one-way, so it asks nothing of `D`'s connections, and whether `D.6` should carry a referee-facing line about what else comes out of that culvert is proposed and batched.*

**Route ends recorded.** Six edges leave region `J`. `FB.14`–`J.2` and `HB.19`–`J.2` are two of the three marked descents; `FB.4`, `FD.16` and `GB.20` all land at `J.3`. **All five were drawn from the peak side at earlier passes and all five are reciprocated here unchanged.** `J.9`–`D.6` is drawn outward from this side only.

**One gap carried, not filled.** `J.2` is *the marked descents, one per peak*, and only two of the three have a named stub at the far end. **Peak 2's declared descent is still in `GB`'s unnamed fill** — recorded at the Peak 2 block's pass, left there deliberately against a ratified count of twenty, and left there again here. `J.2` is drawn to accommodate it whenever `GB` names it, and the diagram does not pretend it exists.
