# `GC Azdun, Peak 2 Level 2 Throne of the King`

**Classification:** DANGEROUS · **Difficulty Die:** d8 · **Tags:** Defiled, Burned, A Name Chiselled Out

*Region file. Companion to the Drakenhold Setting Outline and the Setting Relational Diagram. Tables are authored at the location-stub pass (procedure step 7); location stubs, outlines and the region relational diagram follow in the engineer phase.*

---


**Overview:** The level where a party learns that names are a resource to be collected rather than an answer to be found. Administrative chambers hold clues to the Breaking, and the central throne room leads to a wide overlook balcony giving outward views of the kingdom — a nobles' balcony, not a military one, with no access to the Skybridge from it. The defacing here is total and obsessive: Baldrun Azkelith's name is chiselled out of every surface on the level. Recovering it is the region's prize and is not a master key. Royal names in general open what nothing else will, and there are more of them than one.

**Ambiance:** Burned and defiled and then defaced, in that order and by different hands. Soot over the ruin, and chisel-work over the soot. Every surface at eye height has been struck. The overlook balcony admits weather and daylight, and the throne room is bright, which is the wrong feeling for it entirely.

**Layout:** The central throne room with administrative chambers around it, opening rearward onto a wide overlook balcony giving outward views of the kingdom — a nobles' balcony, not a military one, with no access to the Skybridge from it. The great ramp arrives from GB by way of GA. Around thirty rooms.

**Features:** The defacing, which is total and obsessive: Baldrun Azkelith's name chiselled out of every surface on the level. Recovering it is the region's prize and is not a master key. This is where a party learns that royal names are a resource to be collected rather than one answer to be found. The administrative chambers hold the clues to the Breaking, written by people who did not know it was coming.

**Dangers:** Structural, at the balcony end. Little else. The level was emptied rather than held.

**Creatures:** Passing Lizardman patrols from above, infrequent. Nothing lives here.

**Secrets:** Where the name survives — under something, behind something, in a hand that did not know it was writing it. Who ordered the defacing, which is not who a party will assume. The administrative record of the ban, from the Crown's side, which contradicts the guild account in FE on one specific point.

**Treasure:** Stripped. What remains is documentary, and worth more than the gold that was taken.

## LOCATION STUBS

*Procedure step 7. Code, name and three thematic tags per location. The working note under each is scaffolding for step 8. Block-level material lives in `PEAK_2_BLOCK.md`.*

*30 rooms budgeted; **15 are stubbed as locations** and the balance is unnamed fill — stripped offices, burned wings, ante-rooms, the ordinary chambers of a court that was emptied twice.*

**The throne**

### `GC.1 The Ramp Head` — Open, Undefended, Somebody Walked Away From This
*Working note: no barricade, no bodies, no sign of a fight. The level was emptied rather than held, and that is the first thing it says.*

### `GC.2 The Throne Room` — Bright, Which Is Wrong, The Seat Still There
*Working note: daylight down the length of it from the balcony, soot over the ruin, chisel-work over the soot. Burned, then defiled, then defaced, in that order and by different hands. The throne itself was not taken.*

### `GC.3 The Struck Surfaces` — Every Wall at Eye Height, Obsessive, They Came Back
*Working note: Baldrun Azkelith's name removed from the entire level, repeatedly, by people who returned to check. This is not a location so much as a condition, present in every room, and its thoroughness is itself the clue — nobody works this hard to erase a man they merely deposed.*

### `GC.4 The Overlook Balcony` — Wide, Outward, A Nobles' Balcony Not a Military One
*Working note: views of the kingdom the hold used to serve. **No access to the Skybridge**, deliberately, and a party that assumes otherwise loses a day. Structurally the worst ground on the level.*

### `GC.5 The Burned Wing` — Fired From Inside, Records First, Floor Untrustworthy
*Working note: whoever burned this went for the archive before the furnishings, which tells a party what the fire was for.*

**The chambers**

### `GC.6 The Chancery` — The Crown's Own Account, Written Before They Knew, Self-Serving in One Place
*Working note: the administrative record of the ban from the Crown's side, by people who did not know what was coming. It contradicts the guild account in `FE` on one specific point, both versions are self-serving in an obvious way once read together, and `GA.21` is the third account that settles it.*

### `GC.7 The Bypass Mouth` — A Capillary That Reaches, Comes Up Inside, Past Whatever Is On the Ramp
*Working note: **the payoff from `GA.26`.** Arrives in the administrative chambers, which is the level's actual wealth, and skips the checkpointed approach entirely.*

### `GC.8 The Petition Rooms` — Where Citizens Waited, Ordinary Business, The Last Day's Queue
*Working note: the day-to-day of a functioning kingdom, stopped mid-morning. Names, grievances, and the texture of a place worth mourning.*

### `GC.9 The Name Beneath` — Under Something, Behind Something, Missed
*Working note: where Baldrun Azkelith's name survives — not one place but a short list of them, each requiring a different kind of attention. Under a fitting, on the reverse of a mounted plate, inside a lock housing. **Recovering it is the region's prize and it is not a master key.***

### `GC.10 The Line of Kings` — Nine Hundred Years of Them, Untouched, Nobody Bothered
*Working note: the level teaching its real lesson. The defacers went after one man and left every other royal name standing, in full, cut deep. **Royal names are a resource to be collected, not an answer to be found**, and there are eight of them on this wall.*

### `GC.11 The Order` — Who Commanded the Defacing, Filed Properly, Not Who You Assume
*Working note: it was ordered, minuted and filed like any other business, and the authority on it is **Vessa Rudgir, the Steward** — the most competent person in the hold's last year, signing in her own hand, through proper channels, at a point when there was no government left to sign for. This is the level's best single revelation and it recontextualises the whole civil war: the erasure was not a mob and not a faction. It was administration. Read against `GC.14` and `GB.4` it stops being a revelation about a king and becomes one about her.*

### `GC.12 The Rod Schedule` — Which Rod Opens Which Royal Lock, Complete, Kept Where Clerks Kept Things
*Working note: the Crown's own key-list, naming the combination each Royal lock at `GD` demands. It does not contain the rods. `FE.6` holds two of them and the rest are a campaign.*

### `GC.13 The Steward's Rooms` — Vessa Rudgir, Working to the Last Hour, Nothing Personal Kept
*Working note: the most competent person in the hold's last year, and her rooms are all work and no self. Her hand is on everything and she is on nothing — including, filed correctly and cross-referenced, the order at `GC.11`.*

### `GC.14 The Seventh Chair` — Taken Out and Carried Here, Not Destroyed, Kept
*Working note: **the answer to `GA.2`.** The missing chair from the council table is in the Steward's rooms, whole, brought up and kept rather than broken — which means she removed her own seat and did it deliberately. Taken with `GC.11`, the sequence is legible and damning in a way that is not simple: she withdrew from the council and then, with no council left, kept governing. Her brother is downstairs in the formation, on the other end of the same night, and he will not discuss her — which is now a refusal with a subject.*

### `GC.15 The Treasury Approach` — Where the Ramp Goes Up, Sight of the Lamps, The Last Free Ground
*Working note: from here upward the party is traffic. Lamplight above, and somebody will ask what they are carrying.*
