# `GC Azdun, Peak 2 Level 2 Throne of the King`

**Classification:** DANGEROUS · **Difficulty Die:** d8 · **Tags:** Defiled, Burned, A Name Chiselled Out

*Region file. Companion to the Drakenhold Setting Outline and the Setting Relational Diagram. Tables are authored at the location-stub pass (procedure step 7); location stubs, outlines and the region relational diagram follow in the engineer phase.*

---

**Overview:** The level where a party learns that names are a resource to be collected rather than an answer to be found. Administrative chambers hold clues to the Breaking, and the central throne room leads to a wide overlook balcony giving outward views of the kingdom — a nobles' balcony, not a military one, with no access to the Skybridge from it. The defacing here is total and obsessive: Baldrun Azkelith's name is chiselled out of every surface on the level. Recovering it is the region's prize and is not a master key. Royal names in general open what nothing else will, and there are more of them than one.

**Ambiance:** Burned and defiled and then defaced, in that order and by different hands. Soot over the ruin, and chisel-work over the soot. Every surface at eye height has been struck. The overlook balcony admits weather and daylight, and the throne room is bright, which is the wrong feeling for it entirely.

**Layout:** The central throne room with administrative chambers around it, opening rearward onto a wide overlook balcony giving outward views of the kingdom — a nobles' balcony, not a military one, with no access to the Skybridge from it. The great ramp arrives from GB by way of GA. Around thirty rooms.

**Features:** The defacing, which is total and obsessive: Baldrun Azkelith's name chiselled out of every surface on the level. Recovering it is the region's prize and is not a master key. This is where a party learns that royal names are a resource to be collected rather than one answer to be found. The administrative chambers hold the clues to the Breaking, written by people who did not know it was coming.

**Dangers:** Structural, at the balcony end. Little else. The level was emptied rather than held.

**Creatures:** Passing Lizardman patrols from above, infrequent. Nothing lives here.

**Secrets:** Where the name survives — under something, behind something, in a hand that did not know it was writing it. Who ordered the defacing, which is not who a party will assume. The administrative record of the ban, from the Crown's side, which contradicts the guild account in FE on one specific point.

**Treasure:** Stripped. What remains is documentary, and worth more than the gold that was taken.

**Reading the diagram.** Solid edges are open floor — the level was emptied rather than held and almost nothing on it is locked, which is why its difficulty is reading and not passage. Dotted edges are hidden or secret: the three places the name survives, the bypass from the warren, and the Crown's own stair. `GC.3` is drawn as edges rather than as a room because the defacing is a condition of the level.

**What the drawing found.**

- **`GC.3` touches three rooms and pointedly not the fourth.** The struck surfaces are on the throne room, the chancery and the petition rooms, and **they are not on `GC.10`, the line of kings.** Nine hundred years of royal names stand cut deep and untouched one edge from a wall where one man's name was chiselled out repeatedly by people who came back to check. The lesson the region exists to teach is drawn rather than stated: this was not iconoclasm, it was one man, and every other name is still there to be collected.
- **`GC.9` is three hidden edges off three different rooms and never a room of its own.** Under a fitting in the throne room, on the reverse of a mounted plate in the petition rooms, inside a lock housing on the line of kings. Each wants a different kind of attention and no single search finds all three. **The prize of the region is a scavenge across the level rather than a door**, which is what keeps it from behaving like a master key.
- **`GC.6` is the real hub of the level and the throne room is only its front.** Seven edges: the throne room, the struck surfaces, the burned wing, the bypass, the order, the rod schedule and the Steward's rooms. Everything documentary hangs off the chancery, and the burned wing sits directly beside it — **whoever fired this level was walking toward the chancery and did not get there.** That is now geometry rather than an assertion, and it is the strongest single argument that the burning and the defacing were done by different people for different reasons.
- **The Steward's chain is three nodes long and closes on itself.** `GC.6`→`GC.13`→`GC.11` and `GC.13`→`GC.14`, with `GC.11` also filed in the chancery. A party can reach the order from the paperwork or from her rooms, and either route ends up in the other. The seventh chair is a leaf off her rooms and off nothing else. **The whole revelation about Vessa Rudgir is a closed triangle with one leaf**, and there is no way to enter it that does not pass through her handwriting.
- **The Crown's own stair leaves from inside that triangle.** `GC.13`–`GB.12`. The stair does not open off the throne room or the chancery; it opens off the Steward's rooms. A party finds the seventh chair, the order and the secret way down to a prison in the same three rooms, and the module never draws the line between them.
- **`GC.4` is a leaf with no route off it.** The balcony is drawn as a dead end on purpose: no Skybridge access, no descent, and the region's own Danger 1 offers it as a way out of a held ramp only in the sense that waiting on a windy ledge is a way out. **A party that assumes the balcony goes somewhere loses a day**, exactly as the stub says.
- **The level has three ways in and one of them is not walkable.** `GC.1` the ramp, `GC.7` the bypass from `GA.26`, and `GC.13` the Crown's stair from below — which can only be used as an entrance by a party that was already in the cell corridors. Two honest doors and one that is only ever a door in the other direction.

**Route ends recorded.** Four edges leave region `GC`. `GA.6`–`GC.1` is the great ramp and `GA.26`–`GC.7` the bypass, both drawn at the first-level block's pass and reciprocated here. `GC.13`–`GB.12` is the Crown's own stair. `GC.15`–`GD.1` is the ramp up, and from there the party is traffic.
