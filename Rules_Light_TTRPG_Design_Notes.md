# Designer Notes

## Game Pitch

* Ruleset is inspired by Classic 1970s and 1980s TSR era Dungeons & Dragons ( seeking broad replayability of classic modules with minimal changes needed and a close emulation of the original playstyle ), Knave ( Procedural generation of content ), Errant ( Rules Light \- Procedure Heavy ), Dungeon World ( Narrative Emphasis and Class Playbooks ), GloG ( House Rule Emphasis and Simple Core Mechanics to allow broad cross compatibility ), Call of Cthulhu ( Sanity mechanic ), Worlds without Number ( Procedural generation and Feats ), Cairn ( Simplified Combat ) plus numerous other  
* Rules-Light mentality with a simple universal resolution mechanic that allows compatibility between discrete procedures. Each game table can dial in the level of complexity by using only the core procedures in a simple game, or heavily house ruling more complex procedures for a more detail oriented game or any level in between.  
* Built to handle both story driven narrative and procedurally generated environments equally well  
* System mastery expected for players is minimal, with their main focus being making decisions towards an exciting narrative and rolling dice  
* Almost all dice rolls are Player facing, with the Referee only rolling for Opposed or Encounter rolls. When rolls are needed for Encounter, Events or Weather tables the Caller rolls and the Referee narrates the result  
* ‘Zine-based’ meaning rather than a single core rulebook there are series of ‘playbooks’ that detail additional character, setting, creature, treasure or play details. These playbooks can be edited with house rules and unique combinations will will create a consistent but unique campaign

## Introduction

### Core Play Loop

* SCENE: The Referee describes the obvious details of the current setting of the players.  
* NEGOTIATION: Players discuss with the Referee to clarify details from their senses and propose actions with an idea of what risk may be suffered. The Referee evaluates the complexity of the movement and determines when multiple rounds of action may be necessary for completion of the stated objectives.  
* DECLARATION: The CALLER makes a formal declaration of what action the party is attempting and what the goal of their action is  
  * If the declaration is successful the CALLER remains for the next move, otherwise the role moves to the next at the table  
* OUTCOME: The Referee makes a ruling based on the Campaign Playbook, the current Scene and their own Judgement. While most procedures have a roll associated, the Referee is always able to simply declare the Outcome based on the information at hand and expectations of the narrative.  
* UPDATE: The Referee narrates the outcome of the players action and how their environment has changed. The loop returns to the players discussing their next course of action.

### Dice Resolution

* GROUP INITIATIVE: During normal play the party resolves actions as a group, typically rolling a single die based on their current region's DIFFICULTY DIE.  
* INDIVIDUAL INITIATIVE: In tense moments or when players are pushing their abilities to the max, the narrative will zoom in and focus on a roll of that individual player’s ACTION DIE. These  moves exist within the regional move, however when the party narrative is relying on a singular narrative the referee may treat the action die roll as the difficulty die roll as well, especially when the party has spent the entire time period focused on the singular action.  
* ENCOUNTER INITIATIVE: When hostility or open combat is encountered, each participant decides which of their ACTION DICE they would like to DECLARE and then roll their simultaneously. Each player’s actions are resolved from the highest roll to the lowest.  
* RESULTS: For most rolls, a 1 is considered a FAILURE, a 2 or 3 is considered a COMPLICATION and a 4 or greater is considered a SUCCESS.  
* MULTIPLE ROLLS: In order to adjudicate complex tasks or those that may require multiple rounds of effort to complete, instead of adjusting a difficulty level, the Referee should ask for multiple SUCCESSES for the task with a COMPLICATION losing the die but not progress and FAILURE requiring the entire process to be reattempted if possible.  
* OPPOSED ROLLS: When two actions conflict, both are rolled simultaneously and the higher roll will be  a SUCCESS, the lower a FAILURE and ties are a COMPLICATION.  
* ADVANTAGE: When the Referee determines that situation, ability or equipment favors the outcome roll twice, keeping the better result  
* DISADVANTAGE: When the Referee determines that fate has turned against the outcome or another’s ability greatly outweighs roll twice, keeping the worse result

### Regional Modes of Play

* Play shifts based on the current region the players are navigating:  
  * SAFE Regions: Points of light in an unsafe world, represented as towns, inns, temples and places of rest and safety. Distance measured in feet-indoors, yards-outdoors, miles-safe roads. Time is measured in Days. Players may embark on separate goals within a safe region and a roll on the Difficulty Die will not be required for every action, however the Referee may use the Difficulty Die to determine the quality of success from individual actions.. Roll on d6 Events table when entering and every week thereafter.  
  * WILD Regions: The untamed areas connecting safety and darkness and allowing for exploration and travel, represented as roads, landmarks, forests, plaints, mountains, hills, swamp, rivers. Distance measured in yards and miles. Time is measured in approximately 4-hour Watches, 6 watches mean a day has passed. Every failed Difficulty roll brings an opportunity for a chance Encounter.  
  * DANGEROUS Regions: Indoor or enclosed spaces of darkness and the glint of treasure, represented as tombs, ruins, keeps, dungeons. Distance measured in feet indoors and yards outside. Time is measured in approximately 10 minute Turns, 6 Turns mean an hour has passed. The d6 Danger Die decreases towards 1 with every failed Difficulty roll \- each face brings increasing Danger.  
* Measurement of time and distance is altered as danger and tension increases.  
* Procedures within each region shift in focus to reinforce expected play styles and danger levels, also serving as an instruction for newer players  
* Each Region has a DIFFICULTY DIE that is rolled for each group action, this measures the size of the town, the treacherousness of the terrain or the evil of the dungeon. An average Difficulty is represented with a d8, with d10 or d12 representing further ease of narrative success while d6 and d4 are progressively more difficult.  
* Regional Moves are resolved with GROUP INITIATIVE \- meaning all actions are declared and then a single roll will determine the outcome. Complex moves may be determined simultaneously, especially in SAFE regions where the party may split to complete tasks individually so long as time, resources and number of members in the party reasonably allows.

### Implied Setting

* Points of Light \- The world is largely wilderness with small outposts of human civilization.  
* Shadow of Ruined Empire \- The world was once covered by ancient civilizations which have left their remains for those that are courageous enough to explore.  
* Mythic Underworld \- Delving to the underworld brings players to a world antagonistic towards the light and all that comes from it.  
* Low Magic \- Magic is both poorly understood and fraught with danger making those that dare to wield it a class apart from the common man.

## Character Framework

### How to Create

* Name \- What do you call yourself?  
* Title \- How do others see you?  
* Ancestry  \- What people do you come from, what is their language ( or regional dialect ) and what special trait (or tradition) does this grant you?  
* Background  \- What was your formative history and what trinket do you carry as a reminder?  
* Class \- What archetype do you follow that defines your play style?

### Action Dice

* Players have three categories of Action Dice that they can use, ranging in size from a d4 to a d12.  
* Starting players will have each as a d6 or else a single d4, d6 and d8.  
* Action Dice are physical and mental resources that are consumed through action and reset only with rest. A level 1 character will have 1 of each type of Action Die to start with.  
* Combat \- Physical prowess, Feats of Strength, Melee Combat, Fighting Stances and Tests of Constitution  
* Skill \- Careful and precise action and thought, Maneuvers, Ranged Combat, Skills and Tests of Fate  
* Magic \- Harnessing powers from beyond, Casting Spells, Magical Talents, Tests of Sanity

### Features

* Unique rules or mechanics that enhance or reinforce the play style  
* Broadly applicable features will provide small advantages while more powerful features are balanced with limited scope  
* Features may also provide additional Fighting Stances, Skills or Magical Talents beyond what is included in the core class description

### Level

* Experience tracked by coin spent on downtime with 1 cn equalling 1 xp  
* In order to advance to the next level the player must have met the experience requirements of the next level and then rest in a SAFE place  
* Once advanced in level, the player must have gained at least 1 additional experience point before they can advance an additional level  
* Current level increases by 1  
* Roll for one additional FEATURE  
* Add one additional Action Die of the players choice up to a maximum of 6 in any one category (Combat, Skill or Magic)  
* Status Maximums are raised to the new Level  
* Luck Used resets to 0  
* Wounds and Madness do not change  
* Advancement Table  
  * Level 1: 0 XP  
  * Level 2: 1,000 XP   
  * Level 3: 2,000 XP  
  * Level 4: 4,000 XP  
  * Level 5: 8,000 XP  
  * Level 6: 16,000 XP  
  * Level 7: 32,000 XP  
  * Level 8: 64,000 XP  
  * Level 9: 128,000 XP

### Status

#### **Wounds:**

* Track the scars that the player has accumulated, gained through Forced Dangers or when attacked without any combat die remaining  
* Starts at 0 and each Wound accumulated is permanent and will  not decrease  
* Exceeding the players Maximum Wounds means Death  
* When taking a Wound \- roll on d6 table per damage type to determine the mechanical effect of the wound  
  * Piercing  
  * Crushing  
  * Poison  
  * Fire  
  * Frost  
  * Blast

#### **Madness:**

* Increases the players count of madness  
* Starts at 0 and the Madness count is permanent and does not decrease  
* Exceeding Maximum Madness means suffering a permanent psychotic break  
* When taking Madness \- roll on d6 table to determine the mechanical effect of the madness

#### **Luck:**

* When used before a roll, grants Advantage  
* When used after a roll, grants a reroll, always accepting the new result

#### **Conditions:**

* Free form status defined by the Referee that includes a Name, Effect and Duration  
* Examples:  
  * Bloodied \- Bloodshed,   
  * Exhausted \- Lack of Rest, Food or Water  
  * Encumbered \- Overburdened  
  * Poisoned  
  * Diseased

* Heavy \- Disadvantage on Skill Moves, Cannot perform Magic Moves, opponent must roll 6+ to cause Combat die loss in Encounter Initiative

  * Disguises

#### **Quality:**

* Traits that  modify the value and effectiveness of an item  
* Cursed \- DISADVANTAGE imposed when used, May have one or more special traits that provide penalties with use, May bond with the wearer and not allow removal without magical intervention  
* Poor \- DISADVANTAGE granted when used, breaks on a roll of a 1, no value for resale  
* Fine \-  Skilled or Ornate craftsmanship, Valued at 3x-10x normal, May have one or more special traits that provide bonuses with use  
* Masterwork \-  ADVANTAGE granted when used in melee, May have minor magical enchantment or special traits that provide bonuses with use, Resistant to destruction or damage, Valued at 10x-100x normal

#### **Artifacts:**

* Unique, named items with potentially wondrous power  
* ADVANTAGE granted with use plus special bonuses in certain circumstances  
* Value: Priceless  
* May have an ego/personality  
* Will have multiple major or minor special enchantments  
* Only can be destroyed by special rituals

#### **Value:**

* Value of goods uses a silver standard, measured in coins (cn)  
* Characters with a coin purse are assumed to hold copper pennies that can be used for minor consumable goods without tracking  
* Currency may be found in multiple sizes and made with many precious metals but will require changing to the common silver currency before spending  
* When changing coin typically a 3% fee is charged  
* When taking goods to resale typically a 10%-20% fee is charged  
* Tithes or Guild Fees will be expected from player characters with obligations to factions.  
* Upkeep will be expected from Players on a monthly basis

#### **Encumbrance:**

* Weight of items is measured using slots (wt)  
* 5 wt worth of equipment may be worn  
* 10 wt worth of equipment or treasure may be carried  
* A slot is taken for every 100 standard coins carried  
* Non-Encumbering \- small items that take  0 slots  
* Light \- Stack three of the same for item to count as 1 slot  
* Normal \- Items that can be held with 1 hand count as 1 Slot each  
* Bulky \- Larger or Heavier  items may count for 2 or more slots

#### **Treasure:**

* Treasure Types I, II, III, IV and V  
* Types I through IV read as a d20 table, Type V should be a bespoke table  
* I: Scavenged Loot  
* II: Foraged Goods  
* III: Caches  
* IV: Hoards  
* V: Unique Treasures \- Unique, named items with potentially wondrous power.

### Creature Types

* Men — ordinary mortal humans: bandits, soldiers, cultists, mundane human threats.  
* Humanoid — non-human, human-shaped peoples with their own culture and society (goblins, orcs).  
* Beast — natural animals, however large or dangerous, with no magical or supernatural nature (wolves, giant spiders, bears).  
* Fantasy — catch-all for setting-flavor creatures that aren't natural animals and don't fit a sharper category below (griffons, will-o'-wisps).  
* Undead — anything animated by death-tainted or necrotic energy (skeletons, ghosts, wraiths).  
* Construct — artificial, non-living bodies, usually magically animated (golems, animated armor).  
* Horror — alien or sanity-bending things that sit outside normal nature and magic entirely.  
* Wyrm — dragons and their draconic kin specifically.  
* Fey — otherworldly, fae-touched beings bound by strange rules or bargains.  
* Fiend  — extraplanar, malevolent entities of infernal or abyssal origin.  
* Giant — oversized humanoid-adjacent brutes: giants, ogres, trolls.

## Player Procedures

### Regional Moves

#### **Safe Regions:**

##### **Travel**

* Days of travel through protected areas

##### **Commerce**

* Trade and negotiate the buying or selling of goods  
* Difficulty roll to establish availability  
* SUCCESS: Available  
* COMPLICATION: Poor availability, increased price or lesser quality  
* FAILURE: Not Available

##### **Downtime \- Carousing**

* A raucous celebration of newfound and short-lived wealth with wine, women and song.  
* Minimum spend based on difficulty die d4 \- 100sp, d6 \- 500sp, d8 \- 1000sp, d10 \- 1500sp, d12 \-  2000sp  
* Roll DIFFICULTY DIE for multiplier and effect  
* SUCCESS 2x-3x multiplier but have an Positive, Neutral or Negative effect from table rolled  
* COMPLICATION 1x multiplier and have a Neutral or Negative effect from table rolled  
* FAILURE 0x multiplier and have a Negative effect from table rolled

##### **Downtime \- Philanthropy**

* Support, Reinforce or Equip a Faction, Settlement  or Cause  
* Minimum spend based on the number of action die the faction has  \- 1000sp each  
* 1x multiplier plus boon for the faction

##### **Downtime \- Training**

* Focused training or learning from a master  
* Allows picking from Features table instead of rolling  
* Instead may learn a mundane skill  or basic conversation in a new language at an amateur level  
* Minimum spend 1000sp per current level  
* 1x multiplier to experience gained

##### **Downtime \- Research**

* Develop a new spell, Plan and build a project or Consult ancient wisdom to uncover a secret  
* Minimum spend based on the difficulty die d4 \- 1000sp, d6 \- 2000sp, d8 \- 3000sp, d10 \- 5,000sp , d12 \- 10,000sp  
* Roll DIFFICULTY DIE for OUTCOME  
* Complex projects may require multiple rolls  
* All rolls must succeed or the Research fails  
* Appropriate resources must be acquired in advance  
* 1x multiplier to experience gained

##### **Gather Information**

* Learn rumors and gain job opportunities  
* Roll 1 die on rumour table per 100 cn spent  
* New rumours become available each week  
* Roll DIFFICULTY DIE for result

##### **Recruitment**

* Seek out recruits and hirelings  
* Roll 1 die per 100sp spent  
* Specialists may require multiple days of searching  
* Roll DIFFICULTY DIE for OUTCOME  
* For each SUCCESS one potential recruit or hireling is found  
* COMPLICATION, no suitable recruits available  
* FAILURE, develop a negative reputation for the week and will have to try again later

##### **Rest**

* A night’s rest in a safe place  
* All Combat and Skill Dice as well as 1 Magic Die are recovered

#### **Wild Regions:**

##### **Navigate**

* Travel between known points (3 miles per hour traveled)  
* SUCCESS: movement between areas  
* COMPLICATION: difficult trail and halve the progress made  
* FAILURE: lose the way, no progress made, further failure may bring the party to a random location while they wander aimlessly

##### **Track or Evade**

* Carefully following signs of movement or Hiding your way  
* SUCCESS: gain on your quarry or move ahead by one watch worth of movement  
* COMPLICATION: keep pace as the pursued or pursuer  
* FAILURE: lose ground by one watch worth of movement against the opponent

##### **Explore**

* Search unmapped wilderness  
* SUCCESS: find a hidden feature  
* COMPLICATION: difficult trail prevents a good reading of the area  
* FAILURE: exploration is derailed by an encounter

##### **Forage or Hunt**

* Gather resources from the wilderness  
* SUCCESS: a rare for the area or of generally high value item is foraged or a hunt sufficient for the party is captured  
* COMPLICATION:  a common ingredient is foraged or sufficient quarry for a single individual is hunted  
* FAILURE: the search brings little value to the party and may expose to outside adversaries

##### **Make Camp**

* Establish safe rest in the wilderness  
* Three separate rolls are required:  
  * 1: Secure food and water (Advantage if foraged successfully or sufficient rations)   
  * 2: Secure fire (requires tinderbox, magical fire \= automatic SUCCESS)   
  * 3: Secure campsite (requires bedroll per person)  
* 0 successes: Rest automatically fails  
* 1 success: Rest rolled with disadvantage  
* 2 successes: Rest rolled normally, one player may attempt Camaraderie  
* 3 successes: Rest with Advantage, one player may attempt Camaraderie and another may attempt Reflection

##### **Camaradarie**

* Form bonds amongst the party by sharing stories from their past  
* Happens within the Make Camp action when at least 2 of the three rolls have succeeded  
* SUCCESS grants bonus Luck die for next day for each member of the party present – expires if unused when the next sun rises.  
* COMPLICATION means only one member of the party, of the story tellers choosing, may receive Luck for the next day.   
* FAILURE the players learn something about the storyteller but gain no benefit. 

##### **Reflection** 

* Careful thought and contemplation of the mysteries surrounding the party and their future  
* Happens within the Make Camp action when all three of the rolls have succeeded  
* Pose question to referee about something encountered. Roll Difficulty Die.   
* SUCCESS provides information related to your question.  
* COMPLICATION or FAILURE gives ambiguous answers

##### **Rest** 

* Rest in the Wilderness to recover Skill and Combat dice after Making Camp  
* SUCCESS: recover all Skill and Combat dice.   
* COMPLICATION: Rest is poor, only Combat dice recovered  
* FAILURE: encounter interrupts, no recovery (may rest again after resolving).

#### **Dangerous Regions:**

##### **Careful Movement**

* Slow Movement (up to 1 room or 120'), allows mapping and checking for secrets  
* Alternatively, a single difficult terrain feature may be navigated by the party so long as a valid plan has been declared to the Referee  
* Complications may be avoided by taking additional time  
* SUCCESS the party moves while making careful observation of the areas they pass through  
* COMPLICATION the party may encounter signs of danger and choose to either spend time avoiding or risk facing Danger  
* FAILURE the party encounters Danger

##### **Rushed Movement**

* Normal speed at Disadvantage (up to 5 rooms or 600'), does not allow mapping or investigation while travelling  
* SUCCESS the party moves through the space  
* COMPLICATION the party may encounter signs of danger and choose to either spend time avoiding or risk facing Danger  
* FAILURE the party encounters Danger

##### **Chase or Flee**

* Rapid movement, rolled per 1-Minute Round until resolved ( up to 1 room or 120' )  
* Measured in rounds of travel apart from your adversary  
* Assuming that your opponent is moving at an equal 1 round of movement per round, equalling the opponents position means the direct encounter resumes and beating them by three rounds of movement means that the quarry has sufficiently escaped.  
* SUCCESS 2 rounds worth of movement  
* COMPLICATION 1 round worth of movement  
* FAILURE 0 rounds worth of movement

##### **Search**

* Systematically examine an area for hidden details ( up to 1 room or 120 square feet )  
* SUCCESS earns 1 secret that may reveal a trap, treasure or hidden feature  
* COMPLICATION no secrets found  
* FAILURE no secrets found and the DANGER DIE  is advanced by 1

##### **Listen**

* Carefully listen for noise from nearby rooms  
* SUCCESS gives important details about noises heard and their potential sources  
* COMPLICATION general level of volume can be determined but no details and certainty of the listener will be low  
* FAILURE efforts to listen proceed without effect and the DANGER DIE is advanced by 1

##### **Rest**

* Brief respite and binding of wounds to restore a Combat Die  
* SUCCESS 1 combat die is restored per 10 minutes rested  
* COMPLICATION no combat die is restored  
* FAILURE rest is interrupted and the DANGER DIE is advanced by 1

### Core Moves

##### **The General Move**

* This move provides a template for all player moves and covers any actions not covered by a specific procedure  
* The player provides a declaration of both method and intent of their actions and the Referee will clarify the possible Outcomes  
* For reasonable uses of player skill, the Referee may adjudicate immediate success  
* In circumstances of a time constraint, limited means, or an objective beyond normal human capability the Referee will call for a roll of one of the Player’s Action Dice  
* Complex actions may require multiple successes as determined by the Referee. For each roll a Success will proceed, a Complication will not proceed and loses the die rolled but further attempts may be made, a Failure loses all progress as well as the die rolled.  
* SUCCESS: The objective is achieved and the die is retained  
* COMPLICATION: The objective is partially or fully achieved but the die is lost  
* FAILURE: Tragedy strikes and the objective is missed and the die is lost  
* Combat, Skill or Magic Die as determined by the Referee

##### **Feat of Strength**

* Superhuman strength to overcome an obstacle. Bending bars, lifting gates, moving boulders  
* Combat die

##### **Strike Blows**

* Pushing for advantage through physical force while engaged in melee combat  
* Combat die

##### **Take Cover**

* Hiding with complete or partial cover  
* Combat die

##### **Fire Missiles**

* Firing a missile weapon with precision.   
* With a round to concentrate a single target may be fired upon accurately  
* When firing into combat the target will be chosen at random  
* SUCCESS means a target rolled at random will have a Test of Fate or have the BLOODIED CONDITION.   
* COMPLICATION  means the target suffers the same Test of Fate but the Skill Die is lost.   
* FAILURE means the target is missed and the projectile is lost.  
* Skill Die

##### **Interaction**

* Conversational attempt to deceive, intimidate, persuade or gain something from another character  
* Skill Die

##### **Maneuver**

* Complicated or rapid movement. Climbing, jumping, sprinting, balancing or sleight of hand.  
* Skill Die

##### **Read Magic**

* Take ownership of a Magical Tome, only 1 caster at any one time may be allowed to read and use the magical tome, in order to take over its ownership a successful Read Magic must be cast for every level of the original creator.  
* OR Invoke the magical effect of an enchanted scroll  
* OR May be used to read and understand Magical Runes or Script, 1 successful Read Magic must be cast for each level of the original caster. If a command word is included within the script to activate a magical effect, Read Magic would make the appropriate word clear to the Character.  
* Magic Die

##### **Commune**

* Speak with a supernatural entity  
* Magic Die

##### **Ritual Casting**

* Casting a Spell from a Tome that the caster has taken ownership of using Read Magic  
* Preparation: Each spell may specify one or more material components. Each requires a successful Preparation Roll, with a FAILURE indicating the component has been ruined. Components may be consumed by the casting or reusable. Valuable components should indicate the expected amount in cn that should be procured to successfully cast the spell.  
* Incantation: Once all Material Components have been prepared, the caster can continue with the Incantation which is the verbal intonation  and somatic poses required to channel the energy that brings the spell to life. Most spells will require only a single roll for the Incantation, but those that require a building of great energy may require, for example, three days of daily incantation or 2 successive hours of incantation.  
* Concentration: Most spells will spring to life and continue their effect for as long as intended by the spell without further action from the caster, however some will require the intense concentration of the spell caster, and will allow the continued effect of the spell for as long as the caster can concentrate on nothing else  
* Types of Spells:  
  * Enchantment: Magical Items, Scrolls, Symbols, Wards  
  * Alchemy: Potions, Salves, Oils, Powders, Poisons, Incense  
  * Magical Words: Curse, Blessing, Command, Boast, Oath  
  * True Names: Summoning, Divination, Dream Quests

### Class Moves \- Minor Features

* Passive features that either modify another procedures roll by adding ADVANTAGE or DISADVANTAGE or do not require a roll to activate  
* Examples:  
  * Quick Reflexes \- Advantage on Tests when quick reaction may avoid the effect  
  * Iron Constitution \- Advantage on Tests of Constitution, may  
  * Nimble \- Advantage when maneuvering over difficult terrain or attempting a difficult movement  
  * Darkvision  
  * Beast Tongue \- Advantage on reactions when encountering wild animals  
  * Keen Eyed \- Advantage while searching for traps, treasure or hidden features  
  * Last Stand \- Within the same combat round, with each lost Combat Die the player will receive a cumulative \+1 for each further roll of the die  
  * Improvisation Expert \- May used improvised or scavenged  weapons or tools without penalty  
  * Scavenger’s Luck \- Advantage when Searching bodies, ruins or trash

### Class Moves \- Fighting Stances

* Uses the Combat Die  
* Each Fighting Stance is its own procedure within Encounter Initiative  
* Examples:  
  * Unarmored Defense  
  * Free Movement / Skirmishing  
  * Parrying  
  * Targeted Attack  
  * Charging  
  * Defending  
  * Leadership \- Take command of a coordinated attack, instead of rolling for each participant the Leader adds a \+1 to their roll for each additional participant of the formation up to their current level

##### **Leadership**

* Used in combat when the Character takes lead of a formation for coordinated attack  
* Single combat die is rolled in Encounter Initiative with a \+1 for each participant in the formation  
* Leader may include as many participants as their current level, a 3rd level Character with Leadership can fight in a formation with up to 3 additional Characters for a total of a \+3 bonus to their Combat Die roll  
* If the Leader is brought to 0 Combat dice the Formation is broken and each participant beyond the leader loses 1 of their Combat Dice  
* No additional moves are allowed while participating in a Formation

### Class Moves \- Magical Talents

* Uses the Magic Die  
* Each type of talent should be  listed as its own move with one or more spell-like effects as options available to the caster  
* Additional Spells may be learned through Magical Research  
* SUCCESS means the effect is achieved with successive rolls for continuing effects. Die is retained  
* COMPLICATION means the effect is achieved but the Die is lost.  
* FAILURE means the effect fizzles or is unable to penetrate its target and the Die is lost.

##### **Cantrip**

* Minor magical effects with bounded efficacy and limited applicability  
* Additional Cantrips may be learned through Training during Downtime  
* Examples:  
  * Presdigitation  
  * Mage Hand  
  * Light  
  * Friend  
  * Cleanse  
  * Auditory Glamour  
* SUCCESS means the cantrip is successful  
* COMPLICATION means the cantrip goes off partially or with limited effect  
* FAILURE means the cantrip fizzles and the die is lost. 

##### **Detect Magic**

* Caster has a sense of the intensity and direction of magical emanations within 120’  
* Must hold an object to determine if it is specifically enchanted  
* SUCCESS means the detection is accurate  
* COMPLICATION means the detection is accurate but vague and the die is lost  
* FAILURE means that the die is lost and the player is unable to gain information about potential enchantment

##### **Songs**

* Singing or vocalizations imbued with magical energy with local effects while the caster continues the tune  
* Only a single roll is required to begin the Song and the effect will continue as long as the singer declares it as their entire action without any further rolls  
* Songs will effect all those that can hear the tune, although some effects will only target those within or against the party  
* Knowing the song will render the target immune to its effects  
* Exampes:  
  * Song of Inspiration  
  * Song of Fascination  
  * Song of Lament  
  * Battle Hymn  
  * Marching Song  
  * Dirge  
  * Anthem

##### **Prayers**

* Magical effects bringing to bear the aura of the Characters deity  
* Divine Intervention  
* Augury  
* Blessing or Curse  
* Miracles

##### **Transformation**

* Able to take the form and physical capabilities of another creature  
* Examples:  
  * Wild Shape  
  * Lycanthropy  
  * Mimic

##### **Summon Companion**

* The Player may summon a fantastical Beast with up to 4 Action Dice into their service to serve as a steed for transportation as well as combat. If killed, the Player must advance a level before a new mount may be called to service. The mount that answers the call may be randomly selected by the Referee based on the milieu or the Player may choose the mount using Research Downtime.

##### **Wild Magic**

* Wild Magic represents an innate ability to channel raw magical energy directly through the caster’s will rather than through study or divine intermediary  
* If Madness is suffered while channelling the Wild Magic table should be used for consequences  
* Once begun, Wild Magic demands total commitment and attempting any other move while channeling causes all gathered energy to release immediately which forces a Test of Sanity for each die released  
* There is no limit to how many dice of arcane energy may be in use  
* For each round, the caster must choose one of the following modes: Gathering, Releasing, Active or Defensive  
* Any physical or magical attacks targeting the caster while magical energy has been gathered will be negated by the energy if the casters roll is higher and both will cancel out if the rolls are tied or the casters is lower  
* Gathering \- The caster rolls one or more of their available Magic Dice, with a die of sorcerous energy being gathered for each SUCCESS or COMPLICATION and the die rolled being lost for each COMPLICATION or FAILURE  
* Releasing \- The caster rolls one or more of their available Magic Dice, with a die of sorcerous energy being safely released for each SUCCESS or COMPLICATION and the die rolled being lost for each COMPLICATION or FAILURE  
* Active \- The caster may use gathered energy for one of the following effects:  
  * Dispel \- Passive magical enchantment is broken down, each die invested undoes one die of enchantment  
  * Counterspell \- Active sorcery is worked against with each SUCCESS taking away one magic die invested and each FAILURE allowing one further of the opponents die to be rolled or the magical effect to succeed.  
  * Shield \- A magical barrier protecting against one magical die worth of attack or up to three die when the type of attack is specified  
  * Strike \- A magical force directed as a melee attack against a foe  
  * Mend \- Strengthening and doubly so when the specific type of protection needed is known  
  * Corrupt \- Causes a weakening of magical or physical defenses, or can be used to change the nature of an item  
* Defensive \- The caster rolls for each round with Advantage, for the purpose of defending against Physical or Magical attack

## For the Referee

### Forced Danger

* Triggered by location specific hazards such as traps or the deliberate actions of enemy combatants  
* Dangers are expressed with a trigger and a binary result, for example “any player touching the locked treasure chest Test Fate or take a Wound from the poison needle inside of the lock.”  
* If the targeted player does not have any of the required action dice remaining, the consequence of a FAILURE is automatically suffered  
* For a Test of Constitution, the player’s Combat Die is rolled  
  * SUCCESS means the vitality still holds strong  
  * COMPLICATION means a WOUND is taken  
  * FAILURE means a WOUND is taken and the roll on the Wound Table is made at DISADVANTAGE  
* For a Test of Sanity, the player’s Magic Die is rolled  
  * SUCCESS means the horror is held against  
  * COMPLICATION means MADNESS is increased  
  * FAILURE means a MADNESS is taken and the roll on the Madness Table is made at DISADVANTAGE  
* For a Test of Fate, the player’s Skill Die is rolled  
  * For this Test a CONDITION will always be specified as the penalty  
  * SUCCESS means the danger is avoided  
  * COMPLICATION means the Skill Die is lost  
  * FAILURE means a CONDITION is suffered

### Complications & Failure

* A complication gives an opportunity to avoid the current Danger or Encounter, but at the expense of lost time or poor results  
* On a Failure, the Encounter or Danger can be assumed to be triggered immediately, however the point at which the party intersects will be determined by their actions and the judgement of the referee  
* Travel Moves \- move where the Party is principally focused on moving from one place to another  
  * Suspicious Ground  
  * In need of rest  
  * Hear a noise  
  * Nearby creatures  
  * Trapped area  
  * Environmental hazard  
* Action Moves \- move where the Party is principally focused on accomplishing a task and ending the move in the same place they began  
  * Make too much noise  
  * Broken or incorrect equipment  
  * Too complicated  
* Rest Moves \- move where the Party is focused on rest and recovery, with any interruption losing the potential boon

### Encounters

* An Encounter is a neutral or unfriendly creature that has objectives that are opposed to the parties.   
* If Encounter distance determines that one or both parties is not aware of the other, then careful party actions may avoid detection with a FAILURE indicating that surprise has ended and the Encounter now begins immediately  
* If fighting breaks out then Encounter Initiative resolution starts. Each participant declares their goal for the round and which Action Dice they will be rolling.  
* When striking blows, any opponent within the same melee may be targeted.  
* When firing a ranged attack, the target can be any visible to the player.  
* Actions are resolved from highest roll to lowest  
* Actions cannot proceed against a higher rolling opponent, however a target may shift to another within the same combat  
* Conflicting Actions \- the higher roll succeeds and keeps their die, the lower roll fails and loses their die, for ties both actions succeed and they both lose their die  
* When defender has no Combat dice remaining: next successful attack causes a WOUND or DEATH  
* This continues by One Minute Rounds until resolved by participants fleeing, surrendering or falling in battle

### Tracking Time

#### **Weather Table**

* d6 table, roll at start of day when a new weather pattern begins  
* Weather lasts as many days as the die roll  
* 1: Severe Weather, next Weather roll with Disadvantage, Travel Impossible  
* 2-3: Poor Weather, Travel and Make Camp with Disadvantage  
* 4+: Calm Weather  
* Example:  
  * 6: Clear Skies & Favorable Winds  
  * 5: Mild & Overcast  
  * 4: Light Drizzle  
  * 3: Torrential Downpour  
  * 2: Dense Fog & Mists  
  * 1: Sudden Storm

#### **Events Table**

* d6 table of local events  
* Rolled when players enter the safe area and at the beginning of every week they stay  
* Example:  
  * 6: Market Day \- The square is bustling with extra stalls and traders from nearby hamlets.  
  * 5: Passing Caravan \- A merchant train bound for the distant capital is resting its oxen.  
  * 4: Local Festival \- The town celebrates the Blessing of the Blossoms with feasting and games.  
  * 3: Militia Mustering \- The Baron’s steward is conscripting able-bodied folk for a week of drills to guard against rumored bandits.  
  * 2: Quarantine & Illness \- A sweating sickness has taken hold in the poorer quarter.  
  * 1: Faction Trouble \- Tensions between two local powers boil over into a public confrontation.

#### **Encounter Table**

* d6 table of local denizens or travellers passing through  
* For each FAILURE rolled on the Difficulty Die, the encounter die is rolled and the opportunity for that encounter happens in the next watch  
* Encounters are not forced, may be signs or encounters from afar  
* If encounter distance or location is not decided in the encounter already, another d6 can be rolled to determine distance of the other party with 1- Instant Surprise ranging to  6-Signs or Tracks Only

#### **Danger Table**

* d6 table that describes the mounting tension and increasing danger of a place  
* When entering a DANGEROUS region, a d6 is placed with the 6 presented  
* For each difficulty FAILURE, the die is reduced by 1 and the next entry on the danger table is presented  
* Once 1 is reached, the count will reset to 6 and a  new table may be introduced by the referee  
* The count resets to 6 when leaving the region  
* Example:  
  * 6: Warnings and atmosphere building  
  * 5: Ominous signs and building tension  
  * 4: Environmental hazards requiring tests  
  * 3: Traps, obstacles, or minor guardians  
  * 2: Nearby danger only avoided with careful action  
  * 1: Forced encounter or major hazards

#### **Faction Turns**

* Defined by Resources, Knowledge, Reactions, Tactics and one or more Goals  
* Have one or more Action Dice based on the size of the faction, always a d6  
* Once a week the ACTION DIE is rolled for each Faction  
* SUCCESS: Their immediate objective progresses towards completion  
* COMPLICATION: Their immediate objective has met delays or complication  
* FAILURE: Their immediate objective has failed and a new path must be taken  
* Competing factions will defer progress to the higher roll  
* Relations between factions or with the party can be defined with levels, each associated die can be rolled for the success of negotiations or interactions  
  * 5: Friendly \- d12, will look to advance the causes of their allies  
  * 4: Tolerated \- d10, will look for equal gain between parties  
  * 3: Neutral (or Ambivalance) \- d8, will only cooperate with some advantage to themselves  
  * 2: Antipathy \- d6, will only cooperate with great advantage to themselves  
  * 1: Hatred (or Fear) \- d4, will only cooperate under duress, may take an opportunity to disadvantage one of their opponents, otherwise Hostility or Escape are preferred

### Non-Player Characters

#### **Hirelings**

* Hirelings are paid to complete a specific task  
* Generally do not adventure, when asked to step into danger they may outright refuse or charge exorbitant rates (10x)  
* Mercenaries may engage in combat but only when paid explicitly to do so against a known foe  
* Paid per day or per task but do not generally receive a share of treasure, pay in advance not afterwards  
* Contented hirelings may move to a monthly contract  
* Examples:  
  * Porter \- 1 cn per day  
  * Torchbearer \- 10 cn per day  
  * Driver \- 3 cn per day  
  * Sage \- 100 to 1000 cn per day depending on complexity and renown  
  * Mercenary, Light \- 10 cn per day  
  * Mercenary, Heavy \- 50 cn per day  
  * Craftsman, Common \- 100 cn per day  
  * Craftsman, Skilled \- 500 cn per day  
  * Craftsman, Unique \- 5000 cn per day

#### **Recruits**

* May adventure and gain levels  
* Receive shares of treasure as negotiated when they are hired  
* Tracked as normal player characters but referee may call for Tests against difficult actions

#### **Creatures and Non-Player Characters**

* Have a single Action Dice type rather than separate Combat, Skill and Magic dice  
* Have one or more Action Dice that scale to the overall power and ferocity of the creature  
* Have a modifier from \-2 through \+6 that differentiates sizes and impacts of creatures. One third of the creatures total AD can be used as a general reference for the modifier.  
* May have Special Abilities that create special mechanics for unique attacks, actions or skills.  
* May have a Multiple Attack feature to further modify the capabilities of the creature that allows 2 or more of its AD to be rolled in Encounter Initiative with each being treated as a separate roll with its own chance to strike  
* Action Dice are always a d6, though \+/- modifiers will customize ability levels to different creatures  
* Do not suffer Wounds or Madness  
* When no Action Dice are remaining their Morale is broken and will attempt to flee, surrender or strike a dying blow according to the Referee  
* Do not normally roll for Forced Danger, only triggering an effect when chosen by the Referee

## Conversion Guidelines

* The following guidelines can be useful in running modules meant for classic d\&d and providing instruction to players who are familiar with those game rules  
* For combat there is not a separate roll for surprise, instead when approaching an encounter with the intent of surprise a Success would indicate they are able to sneak up to the creature unaware and a Failure that they are likely the ones surprised.  
* The single action die roll in Encounter Initiative replaces the separate die rolls of initiative, to hit and damage with a single roll. Higher rolls are more effective at each of these and lower rolls are less effective.  
* Combat Dice broadly represent Hit Points and Hit Dice in classic games, a player who has exhausted their combat dice would need to rest immediately or is in danger of death. Instead of death at 0 hit points, the player would potentially face death if struck once they are already at 0, giving them just a little more vitality than in the classic games.  
* A 4 HD Creature in the classic game is approximately representative of a 4 Action Dice Creature. A 4th Level Fighter is approximately equivalent to a 4th Level Player with 4 Combat Dice.  
* Fierce creature can be assigned a Multiple Attack from 2 to 1/3rd of their total AD rounded up.  
* Wounds and Madness are in recognition of the lethality of the original game, players effectively get one chance to be brought to 0 hit points and suffer a long term consequence per level of experience, after that then death is immediate.  
* Tests largely replace Saving Throws, with Tests of Constitution and Tests of Sanity for the lethal dangers and Tests of Fate for more specific dangers.  
* Instead of modifying dice roll targets or bonuses to adjust the difficulty of a task, the Referee can offer Advantage or Disadvantage to a roll when warranted. Complex tasks can be broken down into a series of rolls, For Ex. a large room trap may require successive rolls for each piece of the mechanism or an attempt to sneak past guards may require successive rolls for each portion of the route taken
