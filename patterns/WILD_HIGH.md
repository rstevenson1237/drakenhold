# `WILD` · `HIGH`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a landmark location in a road or wilderness region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**Two things, and they are opposites.**

**The arrival.** The mode-crossing threshold — where the canopy gives out and there is sky, where the country opens and the thing the campaign is about is visible for the first time. It costs nothing, it is rolled for by nobody, and it is the establishing shot.

**The thing worth leaving the road for.** The one lethal site, or the one site rich enough to justify the walk. In a region whose whole instruction is *the road is safe and leaving it is not*, this is the counter-argument, and it must be a good one or the instruction becomes a rule rather than a choice.

One or two per region, as everywhere. In practice a `WILD` region usually wants exactly one of each.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`THRESHOLD`** at full weight — the arrival, and the merged pattern's clearest instance anywhere in the module.
- **`STANDING HAZARD`** at full weight — the lethal ground that announces itself and does not follow.
- **`CACHE`** at full weight, sited under the hazard.
- **`READING`** at full weight — the payoff marker, where a teaching sequence lands.
- **`OCCUPIED`** at full weight — the settlement, the camp, the power that lives out here.

**Absent:** `PUZZLE`, which does not populate `WILD` at any weight in the corpus. `EMPTY` and `CONNECTIVE`, as throughout.

---

## THE FORM

Five to seven features. The Player's Overview is the longest in the region and is where the Tolkien register earns its keep — landscape, weather, scale, and the sentence that goes somewhere. **The Referee Overview gets shorter as the location gets larger**, because outdoors the features carry the detail and the Referee needs orientation rather than description.

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. The arrival costs nothing

No roll, no encounter, no check. Three days under canopy, then sky, and three peaks with light on the snow, and the middle one broken.

Anything charged at that moment competes with the thing the location exists to do, which is to change the register of the players' picture rather than its contents. **This is the only kind of `HIGH` location that may carry almost no mechanism at all.**

### 2. State what makes the boundary a hard line

An arrival is a threshold, and a threshold is only legible if the change is abrupt. The wood ends at a definite edge rather than thinning by degrees — **and the thing that has been harassing the party for two days cannot cross it, and knows so.**

A party that has worked that out can end an evening's trouble by walking uphill. Learning it is the point of the harassment.

### 3. The lethal site is the most visible thing in the region

Thirty paces in good light, five in bad. The husks hung at head height, and one of them still has a boot on.

**And the same encounter is two different encounters depending on how it was reached.** A party that comes here on purpose, in daylight, with fire and a plan, is doing something reasonable. A party that blunders in going around a toll is doing the identical thing at surprise distance in the dark. Write both approaches; the statistics do not change and the outcomes do.

### 4. The `CACHE` sits under the hazard, and it is the region's only real portable reward

The trade a road region offers exactly once: the wrapped and stored husks still hold what their owners were carrying — coin, gear, a rusted blade, a crew's worth of rope.

Elsewhere in a `WILD` region, reward is knowledge, passage, and equipment that is genuinely useful and genuinely not treasure. **That distinction is taught here and the hold will make it constantly.**

### 5. A payoff `READING` is where a teaching sequence lands, and the table reads it before the Referee does

The last marker in a bilingual sequence is the one where a party that stopped at the earlier two reads the top line out loud before the Referee gets to the bottom one. That moment is the entire method paying off and it cannot be manufactured — it is bought by the two earlier stops being free, patient, and repetitive in the right places.

Give it something worth the arrival: a name the world has been mispronouncing, three gates where only one will be found open, a claim the hold spends the rest of the campaign failing to keep.

### 6. A `HIGH` `OCCUPIED` outdoors is a settlement with limits, and the limits are load-bearing

State the ceiling on what it can spend. State what its people will not do — guides who will not leave the path or go beyond the tar pits, villagers who will lend canoes and will not set foot on the island.

**A `WILD` settlement's refusals define the shape of the wilderness around it** more precisely than any description of that wilderness will.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere. Codes given as `X.n`.*

### `THRESHOLD` — the arrival

```
### `X.20 Where the Trees Give Out` — Three Days Under a Roof, Sky All At Once, Nobody Speaks

*The ground tilts up and the trees thin and the green half-light you stopped
noticing days ago gives out all at once. There is sky. There is a great deal of
sky. Ahead and below the wood ends in a ragged edge and the country opens into
broken ground and water and, beyond it, the thing you have walked eleven days
to look at, standing clear of everything around it with the light coming off
the snow. Nobody says anything for a while.*

**Referee Overview:** A mile of gentle climb north-west, canopy thinning to
scrub, the wood ending at a definite edge rather than by degrees.

* **The First Sight:** free. No roll, no cost, no encounter. Whatever the
  region has been saying about the place, this is where the party sees what the
  sentence looks like.
* **The Wood's Edge:** a hard line. The sprites cannot pass it and know it, and
  anything that has been mimicking voices for two days stops here, loudly and
  with bad grace.
* **Days Behind:** the party has been under canopy long enough that open sky
  reads as exposure rather than relief. Everything from here on is watched from
  distance.
* **On, Down the Slope:** -> `X.21`
* **Back Under the Canopy:** -> `X.19`
```

*Three features, all of them free, and the location is `HIGH` on the strength of its Player's Overview and its position. This is the one case where that is legitimate.*

### `STANDING HAZARD` with the `CACHE` beneath it

```
### `X.16 The Furred Thicket` — Grey Nap on Everything, Shapes Hung at Head Height, One Still Has a Boot

*The undergrowth has been furred over — not covered, furred, as though the
whole thicket had grown a grey nap, and where light comes through you can see
the nap is threads, and the threads go up. High up. Hung among them at about
head height are shapes the size and roughly the shape of a man, and there are
more of them than you want to count.*

**Referee Overview:** A belt of webbed thicket some two hundred paces across,
with an old made surface running through it and out the far side. It announces
itself from thirty paces in good light and from five in bad. They hunt from
above and do not pursue past the webbed ground.

* **The Announcement:** the region's one lethal hazard is also its most visible.
  In daylight a party sees this and chooses. In dark or rain they do not.
* **The Husks:** wrapped, stored, and still holding what their owners carried —
  coin, gear, a rusted blade, a crew's worth of rope. The only real portable
  treasure in the region.
* **On Purpose or By Accident:** a party arriving in daylight with fire and a
  plan is doing something reasonable. A party blundering in while going around
  the toll is doing the same thing at surprise distance. The encounter is
  identical and the outcomes are not.
* **The Made Surface Underneath:** something ran through here before the wood
  did, and it comes out the far side -> `X.17`
* **Back to the Road, South:** -> `X.13`
* **Back to the Road, North:** -> `X.15`
```

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. The arrival, charged at nothing.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, the ravine.* The only long read-aloud in the whole wilderness: forest thickening until it seems to warn you off, the thicket ending abruptly, walls rising a hundred feet, cave mouths at varying heights all round, a vulture on a dead tree, ravens rising with their cries magnified by the terrain, and bones among the rubble on the floor. No roll and no cost. **Rule 1, and the reason `THRESHOLD` and `SET PIECE` merged.**

**2. The lethal site announcing itself.**
*X1 Isle of Dread, area 5.* A lair given as loud hooting and grunting from a six-foot opening with a foul stench coming out of it, and nothing visible but a tunnel going back into the dark. The module's standing instruction is to lead with a sense other than sight. **Rule 3.**

**3. Map-driven treasure with a time cost.**
*X1, areas 16 and 19.* A map woven into a nest names a hex; the hex holds a hoard buried ten feet down, taking several turns to dig, with a wandering check each turn — and the monster, always, is the thing that hunts that ground. Both ends placed, and the danger is the clock rather than a guard. **Rule 4's structure at region scale.**

**4. Reward as logistics rather than loot.**
*X1, the plateau gold vein.* A lode whose value is real and whose extraction means mining tonnes of ore at a stated rate per man-day, hiring workers, hauling overland, shipping, and paying to refine — with the ugly player solution anticipated and priced rather than forbidden. **Treasure that is a campaign instead of a bag.**

**5. The settlement whose refusals define the wilderness.**
*X1, Tanaroa and Mantru.* Guides at a stated rate who will not leave the path or go past the tar pits; bearers who will not travel beyond the wall without a large guard; villagers who will lend canoes and will not set foot on the island; and a hard ceiling on what the villages can spend. **Rule 6, and the ceiling is what stops a settlement becoming an infinite bank.**

**6. The payoff of a teaching sequence.**
*Our own third waystone.* The stone that names the hold in its own tongue and gives the common name as the rendering — the first time anything says the place is not called what everyone calls it — and names three gates where a party will find one open. It works because the two earlier stones were free, patient, and repeated their roots. **Rule 5.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

The arrival tropes are the safest in the genre and should be used unashamedly: the first sight of the mountain, the ruin seen across water at evening, the pass that opens onto the whole of the country beyond, the last milestone before a place nobody goes.

For the lethal site, the classic outdoor lairs earn their keep here — the spider wood, the ogre's crossing, the barrow on the heath, the wyrm's scoured hillside, the drowned village at low water. **Choose one per region and make it the only one**, because a wilderness with three lethal sites is a wilderness where leaving the road is simply forbidden, and the choice the region exists to offer stops being a choice.
