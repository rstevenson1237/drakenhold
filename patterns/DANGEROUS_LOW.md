# `DANGEROUS` · `LOW`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a thin location in a dungeon region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**The largest cell in the module by room count and the smallest by content.** Peak 1's main level budgets sixty-five rooms and names thirty-four; the balance is this. Every level above it will run the same ratio or worse.

Its job is not to fill space. It is to make the landmarks legible. A `HIGH` location is only heavy against a floor of thin ones, and a module without a floor is a module where nothing stands out — a party that finds something in every room stops distinguishing between rooms. **The negative space is where a party spends its torches, loses its bearings, and earns the reward that the module gave it no reason to look for.**

The danger in this cell is depth and disorientation. It is not encounters.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`CONNECTIVE`** — the cell's default and the majority of it.
- **`EMPTY`** — nothing here, said plainly.
- **`THRESHOLD`** at its thin end — a change of condition, legible on crossing.
- **`TRACE`** in its one-detail form — history legible from a present remnant, where the remnant is the only thing in the room.
- **`READING`** at a single line — an inscription, a mark, a tally.

**Absent from this cell:** `PUZZLE`, `SET PIECE`, `CACHE`, `OCCUPIED`. A puzzle needs a stated solution and a placed clue, which is three features minimum. An occupant with a want the party can name is `MEDIUM` by definition. **And a `CACHE` here is the cell's defining error** — see the no-find rule below.

`STANDING HAZARD` and `HIDDEN CLUE` sit on the boundary. A hazard that only announces itself is `LOW`; a hazard with a decision attached is `MEDIUM`. A clue with one end is a fact and may be `LOW`; a clue with two ends is `MEDIUM`.

---

## THE FORM

Purely additive. Same fields as `MEDIUM` and `HIGH`, each cut to a sentence.

- **Player's Overview** — one sentence. The one thing that is here.
- **Referee Overview** — one sentence, **dimension-first**.
- **Features** — one or two, ordinarily the ways out, each carrying its pointer.

The register rules still apply and the variety floors do not — there are not enough sentences to vary. What replaces them is the constraint below.

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. One detail, and it is not the room

A thin location gets exactly one thing. The discipline is that the thing is almost never about the location itself — it is about somewhere else, or about someone who was here.

A staircase is nothing. A staircase with recent boot-prints in the dust is a `TRACE` that tells a party a faction is above them. A collapsed run is nothing. A collapsed run with cold air still coming through it is a fact about the ventilation of an entire level.

**Where the detail concerns the location itself, it is a traversal cost.** See rule 3.

### 2. Every significant exit carries a sensory cue, and the cue names something that is actually there

This is the cell's single highest-value rule and it costs six words.

> *North: distant rushing sound, like wind or water. East: faint green glow, warmth. South: smell of tea and crumpets, warm light, quiet bleating — words?*

Three things at once. It is navigation, so a party chooses rather than guesses. It is a warning at retreat distance, so a lethal thing downstream is negotiable before it is an encounter. And it is continuity — the ammonia smell in one room is the bat roost four rooms on, so the level coheres as a place rather than a graph.

**The cue must resolve.** A cue pointing at nothing is a lie the module tells once and is never believed again.

### 3. A `CONNECTIVE` location with no cue must declare a traversal cost

Speed, posture, footing, sound, or light. Something that changes how a fight or a retreat works in it.

- Movement reduced by three quarters, because the stalactites have grown almost to the floor.
- Three feet of headroom and ground slick with blood.
- Completely sound-proof when the door is shut — nothing passes in or out, which makes it the only safe place to argue and the only place you will not hear what is coming.
- A hard surface in a soft wood, so a party crossing it is audible further than it is used to.

None of these has an occupant. All of them are mechanically interesting.

### 4. A `LOW` location may not contain a find

Not a small one. Not a hidden one. Not thirteen silver under the trash in a room the module has already called empty.

A find in a thin location teaches a party to search every thin location in the module, which converts the entire floor into a procedural tax and destroys the pacing function of every honest empty room. If it has something in it, it is `MEDIUM`, and it should be written as `MEDIUM`.

**The exception that is not an exception:** the reward for working the empty ground is a *route*, not an object. A bypass, an older cutting under the floor, a way onto a shaft. Those are edges on the diagram, they are found by a party that worked ground the module gave it no reason to work, and they hang off the emptiest node in the region on purpose.

### 5. Load-bearing `EMPTY` is `EMPTY` plus `TRACE`

The distinction the cell most needs.

**Inert:** the room is empty, and nothing around it makes the emptiness mean anything. Fine once. Fatal in runs.

**Load-bearing:** something removed the contents on purpose and left the signature. A chapel looted and deliberately wrecked, the altar smashed, one word scrawled on the wall. Four pillar-gods described in full and then, flatly, nothing else of interest — which tells you the mortuary proper is through the arch and makes the *next* room's contents legible.

**And emptiness is legible in proportion to how badly the party wants something.** A module that opens its party out of food and water makes every empty storeroom an answer to a question they are actively asking. Where the region gives no such pressure, the thin rooms need the cue rule doing the work instead.

### 6. No more than two consecutive `LOW` locations in a region

A run of five rooms whose entire content is a statement that the room is empty is not pacing, it is abdication. The variety floor from the Playbook binds here even though the others do not.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere — no region codes, no place names from the module — so they cannot be quarried into canon by mistake. Codes are given as `X.n`.*

### `CONNECTIVE`, carrying cues

```
### `X.14 The Cross-Cut` — Four Ways, No Reason to Stop, Somebody Chalked It

*Four passages meet under a low groin vault, and someone has chalked a mark
at shoulder height on three of the four corners.*

**Referee Overview:** A crossing some twelve feet square, vault eight feet at
the crown, dressed ashlar underfoot and dry.

* **The Chalk:** three marks, the same hand, none of them on the run going
  west. Whoever made them was counting the ways they had already tried.
* **North:** a draught, and the smell of standing water -> `X.15`
* **East:** warm, and a faint regular knocking, slow -> `X.22`
* **South:** the way you came -> `X.13`
* **West:** unmarked, and quieter than the rest -> `X.31`
```

*Five features and it is still `LOW`: four are exits and the fifth is a single line. The chalk is the one detail and it is about somebody else. Three of four cues resolve at their far ends; the fourth is the absence of a cue and is doing the most work.*

### `EMPTY`, load-bearing

```
### `X.27 The Stripped Vestry` — Hooks Without Vestments, Taken Not Burnt, Clean Edges

*Every peg and hook on three walls is bare, the shelves are bare, and the
floor has been swept — not by weather, but by somebody with a brush.*

**Referee Overview:** Fifteen feet by ten, ceiling nine, plastered and
undecorated, one door south.

* **The Emptiness:** the room was cleared deliberately and unhurriedly, and
  whoever did it took the fittings as well as the goods. The hook-irons are
  gone from the wall and the holes are neatly stopped -> `X.26`
```

*The absence is the information, and it is only information because it was made on purpose. No find. One feature, and it is also the exit.*

### `EMPTY`, honest and inert — used sparingly

```
### `X.28 The Second Vestry` — Identical, Emptied, Nothing Added

*The same room again, and the same nothing in it.*

**Referee Overview:** Fifteen by ten, plastered, one door south.

* **The Door South:** back to the passage -> `X.26`
```

*Legitimate immediately after `X.27`, because it confirms the pattern of the clearing at no cost. A third would be abdication.*

### `THRESHOLD`, thin

```
### `X.40 Where the Cutting Changes` — Dressed to Raw, Colder, Older Hands

*The wall on your left stops being masonry and starts being rock, and it
happens in the space of a single course, and the air past it is colder.*

**Referee Overview:** A ten-foot reach of passage where dressed ashlar gives
out to hewn stone, the joint clean and deliberate rather than broken.

* **The Joint:** the newer work was built up to the older and stopped, which
  means the older passage was here first and somebody knew it.
* **On, Into the Rough Cutting:** -> `X.41`
* **Back Into the Dressed Work:** -> `X.39`
```

### `TRACE`, one remnant

```
### `X.9 The Turning by the Sump` — Standing Water, One Boot, Facing Out

*The passage turns around a shallow sump of black water, and on the dry side
of it a single boot stands upright, laced, pointing the way you are going.*

**Referee Overview:** A right-hand turn some eight feet wide, floor dished and
holding two inches of water across half its width.

* **The Boot:** dry inside, the leather still supple, and set down rather than
  dropped. It has been stood there deliberately, by somebody marking a route
  who had a boot to spare and no chalk.
* **On, Past the Sump:** -> `X.10`
* **Back:** -> `X.8`
```

*The remnant comes first and the history second. Present tense for what is true now; the past tense attaches to a thing currently touchable. A `TRACE` that opens with the history is a lore dump wearing a costume.*

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced. Each illustrates a trope this cell uses or reinforces a rule above.*

**1. The empty guardpost that isn't — the anti-pattern this cell exists to prevent.**
*B4 The Lost City, rooms 30 and 31.* Two identical guardposts. The first is thin and honest. The second is titled as an empty guardpost and then hides a rotted pouch of silver and a hundred-gold aquamarine under the accumulated trash. Same author, adjacent numbers. The second room teaches a party that thin rooms reward searching, and every honest empty room in the module pays for it afterward. **This is rule 4, with a control.**

**2. Emptiness that is legible because it was made.**
*B4, room 20.* A ceremonial chamber of the old gods, looted and deliberately wrecked, the altar smashed, and the name of the usurping god scrawled across one wall. Nothing to take. The room states, without a word of narration, who won and how thoroughly. **Rule 5.**

**3. Emptiness stated flatly, after full description.**
*B4, room 18.* Four thirty-foot pillar-gods described in detail — Osiris, Wadjet, Maat, Thoth — and then a flat sentence that there is nothing else of interest here. The room is a mortuary antechamber and its emptiness tells you the mortuary is through the arch. **The absence makes the next room's contents legible.**

**4. Directional sense cues as the whole content of a thin room.**
*The Hole in the Oak, areas 2, 4, 7 and 38.* Corridors whose keyed content is a compass-tagged smell or sound pointing at what lies beyond — rushing water north, a green glow and warmth east, the smell of broth east, tea and crumpets and quiet bleating south. The last is an invitation from a household of man-eaters. **Rule 2, and the demonstration that a cue can be inviting and still honest.**

**5. Traversal cost without an occupant.**
*The Hole in the Oak, areas 40, 41 and 42.* A grotto where movement drops by three quarters because the stalactites have reached the floor; a crawlway three feet high with the ground slick; a chamber that is completely sound-proof when its door is shut. Three empty rooms, three different costs, all of them changing how a fight or a retreat runs. **Rule 3, and the sound-proof cave is the sharpest — it cuts both ways.**

**6. The reward for the emptiest ground is a route.**
*Arden Vul Level 3, areas 3-30 through 3-32.* A run of trash-filled guardposts, procedurally identical, one of which carries halfling-sized footprints leading north through the dust — which is navigation and faction intelligence in a room with nothing in it. **Rule 1: the one detail is about somebody else. Compare the module's own bunk warrens, where the emptiest and darkest grouping in the region is where the bypass and the older cutting both hang.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

*Left open deliberately. Noted here so the later pass knows where this cell will take material.*

The classic furniture that belongs in a thin dungeon location is mostly **atmosphere with a mechanism**: the draught that gutters torches, the slow drip counting time, the distant regular knock, the rat that runs the wrong way, the door that swings shut behind. Traps belong to `MEDIUM` and above because a trap without a decision is a die roll. Oozes and moulds are the exception worth considering here — a sessile hazard in an otherwise empty room is a genuine `LOW` encounter, because it does not pursue, cannot be reasoned with, and is avoided rather than fought.
