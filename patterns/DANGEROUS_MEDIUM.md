# `DANGEROUS` · `MEDIUM`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a working location in a dungeon region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**One thing to find, one thing to decide.** This is the working body of a level and the tier a party spends most of its play in. `LOW` makes the landmarks legible; `HIGH` is what the region is remembered as; `MEDIUM` is where the game is played.

The test for belonging here rather than in `LOW`: **does it pose a question the party answers with an action?** Not a roll — an action. Clear it or leave it. Pay or fight. Take the thing, or leave it because you can see why it is still there. If the answer is always the same, it is `LOW` written long.

The test for belonging here rather than in `HIGH`: **does the region need it to be remembered?** One or two locations carry that load. Everything else substantial is `MEDIUM`.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`TRACE`** — history legible from a present remnant. The cell's most common tag.
- **`HIDDEN CLUE`** — one thing findable, gated on a named physical action, pointing somewhere else.
- **`STANDING HAZARD`** — a danger that does not act unless provoked and announces itself before it can hurt anyone.
- **`OCCUPIED`** — something living, with a disposition and a want.
- **`CACHE`** at its lower weight — reward with a stated reason it survived.
- **`READING`** at scale — a register, a tally, a wall of ledgers.
- **`THRESHOLD`** where the change of condition is worth a decision.

**Absent:** `EMPTY` and `CONNECTIVE`, which are `LOW` by definition, and `SET PIECE`, which merged into `THRESHOLD` and lives at `HIGH`. `PUZZLE` is possible but rare — it needs a stated solution and a clue placed in a named other location, and by the time both are written the location is usually `HIGH`.

---

## THE FORM

Three to four features. Both Overviews present. **The secret tier is optional and should appear in roughly half.** A level where every working room has something behind something is a level where players stop believing the surface, which costs more than the secrets are worth.

Variety floors bind: one sentence under eight words in the Player's Overview, feature entries varying by a factor of two between shortest and longest.

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. The decision is visible before it is made

A party cannot choose to clear a culvert if they do not know it is a culvert. The Player's Overview states the situation and the Referee Overview states the cost. Neither withholds the thing being decided about.

Withholding belongs to the secret tier. **It never belongs to the decision.**

### 2. Both branches are priced, and neither is marked correct

Clearing the silted crossing is an afternoon with tools, and it gives a dry crossing for the rest of the campaign — including for whoever is hauling weight back down this road later, which nobody will think of now. Leaving it is a chokepoint, and the thing that holds the chokepoint already knows it.

Write both. Do not write which is the mistake.

### 3. `OCCUPIED` is a want, a trigger, and branches on named player actions

**The want must be nameable by the party in one clause.** An occupant who wants nothing statable is scenery, and scenery is `LOW`.

The trigger is what makes them act — never a reaction roll. Poke the body. Move a piece on the board. Wait two turns and look careful. Feed them. Cross the line carrying an axe.

**The consequence extends past the encounter.** Six goblins holding a deadfall are cheap to pay and quick to break off, and they know the safe way past a guard post two regions on and will sell it. A party that kills all six has bought silence and lost the only cheap source on the crossing. That sentence is the pattern; the statistics are a reference.

Write reaction as behaviour: what they do when the door opens, what they want, what they will take instead.

### 4. A `CACHE` states why it survived, and the reason is usually not a monster

Thirty years of scavengers have been through most of this hold. Treasure lying in the open is a claim about why nobody took it, and the claim has to hold.

Good reasons, in rough order of how often to use them: **it does not look worth taking**; **it is behind a condition rather than a guard** — a niche that opens to a name; **those who could reach it cannot use it**; **those who want it are afraid of the room**; **it is too heavy, too many or too slow to move** — a floor covered in coin needs containers, labour and time, and somebody will notice.

Weight tracks the reason's complexity, not the hoard's value.

### 5. A `HIDDEN CLUE` exists at both ends and neither end explains itself

The half usually missed is the far end. A clue with one end is a fact; a clue with two is a thing a player can act on.

Where the far end sits in an unworked region it is recorded as a thread in the tracking file — not in Standing Mysteries, which is for what never resolves.

### 6. `STANDING HAZARD` announces itself at a distance the party can still use

The lethal thing is also the most visible thing, and that is the design rather than a concession. State the announcement distance and state what changes it — light, noise, weather, whether the party is already moving.

Distinct from `OCCUPIED` in that it cannot be negotiated with; distinct from an encounter result in that it is fixed to a place and does not follow.

### 7. Faction presence is not this pattern

A location names a faction only where something physically persistent is there — a cache, a mark, a body, a camp that stays. Where a faction *may be encountered* is the faction entry's business or the region's presence note.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere. Codes given as `X.n`.*

### `OCCUPIED` — want, trigger, branches

```
### `X.12 The Sump-Wardens' Pitch` — Three of Them, A Fire That Should Not Be Here, They Saw You First

*Three figures sit round a fire built in the bowl of an overturned bronze font,
and they have stopped talking, and one of them has a hand on a pole-arm he has
not picked up yet.*

**Referee Overview:** A chamber twenty feet square off the main run, ceiling
lost above the firelight, one further way out north behind them. Smoke goes up
and does not come back, which is why they chose it.

* **What They Want:** lamp oil, and they will trade badly for it. They have
  burnt furniture for two winters and the room is running out. They will not
  say this first; they will ask what you have.
* **What They Do:** if the party stands still and speaks, they bargain, poorly.
  If the party advances past the font, the one with the pole-arm stands and the
  other two go north. If anything is thrown, all three go north and the party
  will not find them again on this level.
* **What They Know, and Will Sell:** which of the six mouths off the
  antechamber does not double back, and that something has been moving the
  marks in the deep stores.
* **The Font:** peak-work bronze, cracked across the bowl, far too heavy to
  carry out, with a name cut round the foot of it under the soot.
* **North, Past the Fire:** the way they go if pressed -> `X.13`
* **Back Down the Run:** -> `X.11`
```

*The want is one clause. The trigger is three named player actions with three stated outcomes and no roll. The consequence extends past the scene — the information is lost if they scatter. The font is a `READING` nested inside an `OCCUPIED`, which is composition working.*

### `CACHE` — the reason is the pattern

```
### `X.19 The Stopped Tally-Room` — Ledgers to the Ceiling, One Desk Cleared, Dust Says Nobody

*Shelf on shelf of bound ledgers runs back into the dark, every one furred with
thirty years of dust — except one desk at the near end, swept clean, and swept
recently.*

**Referee Overview:** Long and narrow, some forty feet by twelve, shelving both
long walls, one door south and a hatch in the north wall at waist height.

* **The Swept Desk:** somebody works here. Not lives — works. A stub of tallow,
  a straight-edge, and a ledger open at a page ruled in a hand that is not the
  old hand.
* **The Ledgers:** thirty years of deliveries thinning to nothing, the last four
  years kept in two scripts side by side by a clerk plainly learning the second.
  Read backward they are a graph of a road closing.
* **The Hatch:** a delivery hatch, barred on the far side, the bar lifted and
  dropped often enough to wear its seat bright -> `X.20`
* **Under the Third Bay:** a coin-box set into the floor beneath a shelf-foot,
  holding a season's wages in old silver. It survived because reaching it means
  emptying two hundredweight of paper off the bay above, and nobody has ever
  had a reason to move the paper.
* **The Door South:** -> `X.18`
```

*The find has a reason and the reason is labour, not a guardian. The swept desk is the `TRACE` and it comes first, so the party knows somebody is alive here before they know anything else.*

### `HIDDEN CLUE` — both ends

```
### `X.31 The Mason's Corner` — A Signature Nobody Was Meant to See, Behind the Buttress, Two Marks

*Where the buttress meets the wall there is a hand's width of stone that was
never meant to be looked at, and somebody has cut two small marks into it.*

**Referee Overview:** A niche of dead space eighteen inches deep behind the
easternmost buttress, floor to shoulder height, dry.

* **The Two Marks:** a mason's device and, beside it, a second device in a
  different hand and a different depth. The first repeats on eight courses
  through this level. The second appears exactly twice in the hold.
* **Squeezing In:** the space takes one person sideways with their gear off,
  and there is a third mark low down that cannot be seen from outside.
* **Back Out:** -> `X.30`
```

*The far end is a named location elsewhere and is not described here. The secret tier is gated on removing gear and going in sideways, and on nothing else.*

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. Reaction as a branch on player action.**
*Arden Vul Level 3, area 3-36C.* A bound skeleton warrior lies on a bier and does nothing until the intruders do something. Three numbered branches: disturb his body and he attacks; move a piece on the chessboard and he waits to see what happens; take no action for two turns or behave cautiously and he sits up and opens negotiations for the return of the circlet that binds him. No reaction roll anywhere. **Rule 3, and the best instance in the corpus.**

**2. The want, priced, with the consequence outside the room.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, area 2.* Extortionists demand a licence at a stated rate — a sum up front, a sum per sortie, a tithe of everything found — and their response ladders on whether the party pays and how strong they look. Pay and they are merely vigilant; refuse while strong and they withdraw into guerrilla war; refuse while weak and they attack but may take prisoners. **A decision tree over player behaviour, not a die.**

**3. A guardian that is a condition.**
*B4 The Lost City, room 7.* A caged hoard in a room of killer bees, and the bees have learned not to attack anyone wearing a particular faction's mask. The masks are obtainable two rooms away. The hazard is neutralised by an object acquired elsewhere, gated on wearing it, no roll. **Rule 4, and the demonstration that a reason can be an object rather than a lock.**

**4. It survived because it does not look worth taking.**
*B2, wilderness area 2.* Under leaves near a spider lair lies a dead adventurer's kit, everything rotted away save a filthy shield that appears worthless — and cleans up to a magic one. Four lines, and the whole pattern is in them.

**5. Announce the lethal thing before it is an encounter.**
*X1 Isle of Dread, area 5, and its instruction to the Referee.* The rock baboon lair announces itself as hoots and grunts and a foul stench out of a dark opening before anything is seen, and the module tells the Referee outright to signal a difficult encounter by sound, smell or tracks rather than by sight. **Rule 6, stated as method by the source that uses it best.**

**6. The decision visible, both branches priced.**
*The Hole in the Oak, area 3.* Pushing through the grabbing roots costs a random item pulled into the wall; slashing through works easily and triggers an ominous rumbling that shakes the tunnel. Two ways past one obstacle, both stated, neither marked correct. **Rule 2 at its cheapest.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

This is where the classic furniture mostly lands. **Traps belong here** — the covered pit, the dart volley, the falling block, the cascading sand, the poison needle in the chest lock — because a trap with a tell and a decision is a `MEDIUM` location and a trap without one is a die roll. The tell is the requirement: dust lying wrong, a floor dotted with holes, a lid too well balanced.

Also here: the rival party met mid-delve, secretive and prying; the predecessor's corpse with usable gear and a bad lesson attached; the bound thing that will bargain; the magic mouth and its mocking counterpart; the fountain whose original function has gone chaotic; the sealed cellar whose seal is the reason it is still full; the ooze that has kept a stretch of corridor unnaturally clean.
