# `SAFE` · `HIGH`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a landmark location in a settlement region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**The people and places worth coming back for.** In a mode with no combat and no hazards, weight cannot come from danger, so it comes from three things:

**The person who will not simply be paid.** The one whose knowledge is the region's real treasure and whose price is not coin.
**The place that gates on standing.** Access earned by act, trophy, contribution or reputation, opening a ladder rather than a door.
**The place where the region's history is legible in one room.** The `READING` at full weight, in a settlement that does not know what it is standing on.

One or two per region. In a hub town, usually one of the first and one of the second or third.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`OCCUPIED`** at full weight — the witness, the traitor, the power who cannot be bought.
- **`THRESHOLD`** at full weight — the gate that opens on standing, and the arrival into the settlement.
- **`READING`** at full weight — the wall, the row, the record that is the region's history.
- **`CACHE`** at full weight, where the reason for survival is social and the taking of it is a social act.

**Absent:** `EMPTY`, `CONNECTIVE`, `STANDING HAZARD` and `PUZZLE`, as throughout `SAFE`.

---

## THE FORM

Five to seven features. The Player's Overview may be the most restrained in the module — a `SAFE HIGH` location is often a room with a person in it, and the lyrical register works best here on *ordinariness observed closely*: what somebody's hands are doing, what the light is like, what has been swept and what has not.

The Referee Overview stays plain. **The person gets a feature, and it is the first, and it is the longest.**

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. The important person's price is not coin, and they say so

The witness who gives nothing for coin, for rescue, for threat, or for the town's goodwill — and who is candid that the town's goodwill is worth nothing to her. The families who will not sell heirlooms to strangers and will give things to people who ask about the place as though it had been somewhere people lived.

**State the price, state what it is not, and make the refusal specific enough to be argued with.** A price a party cannot discover is a locked door with extra steps.

### 2. What they know is stated in full, in the Referee Overview, and is not doled out

Write the whole of it. The Referee needs to know what is behind the person before deciding how much comes out of any given conversation, and a Playbook that meters it out across sub-headings makes that impossible at the table.

### 3. Access gates on standing, and the ladder has stated rungs

An entry condition — a heroic act, an exceptional trophy, valuable prisoners, a contribution above a stated sum. Then a stage: a feast, close watching, careful questioning. Then a task suited to the party's ability. Then standing passes, and the right to ask for real help against a real problem.

Each rung states its condition and its reward. **And the failure branch is stated too:** behave badly and the host retires early, and they are never aided or invited back.

This is `SAFE` mode's version of the secret tier — gated on an action, and the action is social.

### 4. The traitor in the safe house works once, and the funding is what makes it work

The jovial guest everyone speaks well of, whose companions never speak, and who will readily accompany a party — and who is there to defeat them, and will do it during a crucial encounter.

**Write the funding by code.** It works because every other shopkeeper's price is honest and every other guard does his job. A town of traitors is a town where nobody bothers talking to anyone.

**And place contradictory testimony.** One respected figure privately distrusts him; that figure's own subordinates think highly of him and will say so. Two sources in one building disagree and neither is marked authoritative. That is the shape of a real suspicion and it is what turns a betrayal into something the players could have caught.

### 5. A `SAFE` `CACHE` is taken socially or not at all

Locked attics, forgotten rather than hidden, holding the residue of a partnership nobody has been up to look at in thirty years. Obtainable by asking, by buying, by marrying into, or by being the sort of people the old families decide to tell.

**Theft works.** Say so. And say what it costs — that it is the fastest way to make an entire quarter stop talking to a party, which in an information region is a heavier price than any guard.

### 6. A `READING` at full weight teaches the rite before anything needs it

A wall of names cut in rows going back further than the town has records, each with the same mark beside it, made carefully every time — and the mark is a rite, not a decoration: the name is spoken, the mark is cut, the dead is settled.

Nobody in the room calls this a mechanism. It is performed in public, for free, in front of a party who have no reason yet to care, and it is the working model of what something much later will require. **A party that has watched this has already been told the answer.**

This is the house rule about clues living outside the location holding the secret, at its cheapest and its best.

### 7. The arrival into a settlement is a threshold and is played, not described

Where a `SAFE` region is entered from the road, the entry is a scene: names asked for and given, business stated in the party's own words, a cross-examination if anything sounds wrong. **Courtesy may win a friend among the guard. Rudeness brings suspicion and enemies inside the otherwise safe base.**

`SAFE` is not "no danger." It is danger in a different currency with a much longer fuse, and this is where the players are taught which currency they are spending.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere. Codes given as `X.n`.*

### `OCCUPIED` — the witness whose price is not coin

```
### `X.13 The House Under the Elms` — A Step Worn Hollow, She Is at the Door Already, Nothing to Sell You

*Old trees standing in worked ground, spaced as though somebody had planned
them, and a low turf-roofed house among them with a stone step. The step is not
local work — square-cut, letter-cut — and the letters have been worn shallow by
one person's feet over a very long time. She is at the door before you reach
it, and she looks at what you are carrying, and at what you are wearing.*

**Referee Overview:** Forty old trees in worked ground an hour out, a
turf-roofed house, drying racks, a spring. Two approaches and she watches both.

* **What She Is:** trained in the mountain, in the order the party will not
  meet for thirty sessions, and she will not discuss it with anybody who
  arrives as a customer. Not hiding. Not waiting for anything either.
* **What She Knows:** the only living account of what happened in the high
  chambers — what was broken, what came off the mountain afterward, and in
  what order. Written out in full in the Referee's material; none of it is
  metered.
* **Her Price:** one object, still inside the mountain, of no value to anyone
  who does not know what it is. She will not describe it well enough to be
  found by anyone who has not earned the description.
* **What She Will Not Take:** coin, rescue, threat, or the town's goodwill —
  and she is candid that the town's goodwill is worth nothing to her.
* **The Step:** the only thing on the property that says what she is, and it is
  under her front door where every visitor stands on it.
* **The Path Back to the Line:** -> `X.12`
```

### `READING` — the rite taught in public

```
### `X.5 The River Temple` — More Benches Than People, Names in Rows, The Same Mark Every Time

*Cold stone and plain benches, and more of both than there are people to fill
them. Along the north wall the names of the drowned are cut in rows going back
further than the town has records, each with a mark beside it — the same mark,
over and over, made carefully every time.*

**Referee Overview:** Thirty paces by twelve, east to west, doors east onto the
square, a side door west onto the stair down to the water. Two attendants, no
hierarchy, and a cold room off the nave.

* **The Wall:** every name the river has taken. The mark is a rite and not a
  decoration — the name is spoken, the mark is cut, the dead is considered
  settled. Nobody here calls this a mechanism.
* **The Cold Room:** the temple receives a body without questions and does the
  rite for anyone, paid or not. In practice it is the only place in the region
  that can hold a corpse for a week.
* **The Attendants:** unarmed, uninterested in adventuring, unshockable. They
  know what has been brought in from the outlying holdings this season and will
  say plainly that none of them were drowned.
* **The Stair Down:** sixty steps cut into the slope, steep, wet, and no cargo
  has ever gone up them -> `X.8`
* **The Square:** -> `X.2`
```

*The rite is performed in public, for free, in front of a party with no reason to care. Nothing states what it is for. A party that has watched it has been told the answer to something they will not meet for two more regions.*

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. Access as an advancement ladder.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, the Inner Bailey.* Entry earned by a heroic act, an exceptional trophy, valuable prisoners, or a contribution above a stated sum; then a feast, close watching and careful questioning; then a mission with a stipend; then standing passes and the right to request troops, at a stated minimum and maximum. And the failure branch: behave badly and the host retires early, and they are never aided or invited back. **Rule 3, complete, in one module.**

**2. The traitor, and the contradictory testimony that funds him.**
*B2, the guest apartments.* A jovial priest everyone speaks well of, whose two companions never speak — explained as vows of silence — who will readily accompany a party to the caves and who is there to defeat them, betraying during a crucial encounter. The town's senior cleric, questioned closely by a friend, will half the time admit distrust of him. That cleric's own subordinates think highly of him and will say so. **Rule 4, including the part most modules omit.**

**3. Reputation as the arrival mechanic.**
*B2, the gate.* Each player states their character's business in their own words, and anything that sounds unnatural gets cross-examined by the corporal of the watch. Courtesy may win a friend; rudeness brings suspicion and enemies inside the otherwise safe base. **Rule 7, and the reason `SAFE` needs a threshold at all.**

**4. Knowledge priced in something other than coin.**
*X1 Isle of Dread, the plateau village.* The tribal cleric cannot travel and cannot assist, but will make his spells available to the party's task, and will pray for others given a day's notice. The village leader's good will decides how the whole settlement treats the party, and he will not tolerate mockery of his position. **Access to a resource gated on standing rather than on payment.**

**5. The `CACHE` whose reason is social.**
*Our own shuttered trade houses.* Locked attics, forgotten rather than hidden, holding the settled residue of a six-generation partnership — obtainable by asking, by buying, by marrying into, or by being the sort of people the old families decide to tell. Theft works, and is the single fastest way to make the whole upper town stop talking to a party. **Rule 5.**

**6. The rite performed before it is needed.**
*Our own river temple and the grave it settles.* The wall of the drowned performs, in public and for free, exactly the rite that settles the dead two regions later — name spoken, mark made — and a party that watched it arrives already holding the answer. **Rule 6, and the house rule about clues living outside the secret, at its cheapest.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

The `SAFE HIGH` tropes are the oldest in the genre and hold up: the retired adventurer who will not go back; the sage whose fee is an errand; the patron with a mission and a stipend; the guest who is not what he says; the family fallen out of money but not out of pride; the temple that asks nothing and is owed everything.

Two notes. **The oracle should charge in something the party will feel** — the classic pillar that answers a question about the past at the cost of a real injury is a better shape than a fee, because it makes asking a decision. And **the patron's ladder should have a rung the party can fail at without losing everything**, since a single all-or-nothing audition turns the whole social layer into one roll wearing a costume.
