# `WILD` · `MEDIUM`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a keyed location in a road or wilderness region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**The body of a road region, and nearly all of it.** Because `WILD LOW` dissolves into procedure and `HIGH` is one or two arrivals, this cell carries the region almost by itself.

Its job is different from `DANGEROUS MEDIUM`. A dungeon's working locations pose a question inside a room. A road's working locations pose **a question about whether to leave the road** — and the road is the safe thing, so every one of them is optional by construction.

The consequence: **a `WILD MEDIUM` location must be worth stopping for and must be skippable.** If a party can walk past it and lose nothing, it is procedure. If they cannot walk past it, it is a gate and needs an answer that is not the gate.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`READING`** — the waystone, the marker, the ledger in a ruin. The cell's highest-value pattern, because teaching happens on roads.
- **`THRESHOLD`** at working weight — a boundary, a crossing, a change of ground.
- **`STANDING HAZARD`** — the lethal thing that announces itself and does not follow.
- **`OCCUPIED`** with a trigger — the toll, the hermit, the camp, the thing that will not trouble you unless you set foot on its ground.
- **`TRACE`** — churned banks, dropped tools, a jacket nobody came back for.
- **`CACHE`** — rare, and sited under the one thing that can kill.

**Absent:** `EMPTY` and `CONNECTIVE`, which are procedure here. `PUZZLE`, which barely exists in `WILD` across the whole corpus — the outdoor equivalent is a decision with a stated price, which is not the same thing and is covered under `THRESHOLD`.

---

## THE FORM

Three to four features. Both Overviews present. The Player's Overview does more work than in `DANGEROUS` because there are no walls to constrain the picture — say where the thing sits in a landscape, not only what it is.

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. Every keyed site declares a non-visual approach cue at a distance the party can still turn back from

**This is the cell's defining rule and the one most often missed.**

The webbed belt shows from thirty paces in good light and from five in bad. The lair announces itself as a stench before it is a hole in the ground. The birds go quiet ahead and start again behind.

State the sense, state the distance, and state what changes the distance — light, weather, wind, whether the party is already moving. **The lethal thing should be the most visible thing**, and that is the design rather than a concession to fairness.

### 2. Occupants carry a trigger, not only a disposition

The lizard men are nocturnal and will not trouble anyone in daylight *unless someone sets foot on the mound*. The spiders are passive until you are in the webs. The witch knows because the wood tells her, and she will meet a party inside her line within the hour, on foot, alone and unarmed — **and what happens next depends entirely on whether they are carrying an axe.**

The trigger is the mechanism. Without it a `WILD` occupant is an encounter that happens to have coordinates.

### 3. Terrain is written as a rate and, where it applies, a formation constraint

Walking is easy and fast on exposed pavement and the surface is loud underfoot in a wood that is otherwise soft. Movement through thick growth is single file. Forty paces of standing water is slow, loud, and looking down.

The descriptive fact and the mechanical fact are one fact. Write it once.

### 4. A decision made here is permanent and cheap, and the module says so

Clearing a silted culvert is an afternoon with tools and the water drops within the hour. It is permanent. It gives a dry crossing for the rest of the campaign — including to whoever is hauling weight back down this road later, **which nobody will think of now**, and which is the version that matters.

`WILD MEDIUM` is where a module hands out small permanent choices at prices a first-level party can pay. State the cost, state what it buys, and do not signal which way to go.

### 5. A `READING` on a road teaches the script, and three stops is the unit

Bilingual, the same text twice, cut by the same hand at the same time. **Nothing is translated for the players.** Numbers as tally strokes in both scripts, because tallies need no vocabulary. Roots repeating from stone to stone so a table builds its own glossary.

The reckoning is the teaching aid: half a day's walking against two leagues on the stone tells a table what the unit is worth, and every distance in the region becomes readable at once.

**Spend the teaching here so nothing later has to stop and explain itself.**

### 6. The bypass is priced in what it costs, and the gate is priced in what it buys

A fallen tree across a narrow reach with a toll behind it, and a way around through the thickets. The toll is cheap, quick to break off, and buys a fact worth real money two regions on. The bypass is free of the toll and costs the only thing in the region that can kill outright.

**The module never says which is the mistake.**

### 7. Off-road sites string together rather than hanging as leaves

Two sites sharing an old spur means a party pushing into one arrives at the other from behind. A hollow that is a loop rejoining the trail at the next stop is not a dead end. Leaves are legitimate but a region of them plays as a list.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere. Codes given as `X.n`.*

### `READING` — bilingual, untranslated

```
### `X.6 The Marker at the Fork` — Two Faces, Two Tongues, One Hand

*A squared post of grey stone stands in the angle of the fork, chest-high and
canted, and both faces carry writing — the deep square letters above and, cut
beneath them by the same tool on the same day, letters you can read.*

**Referee Overview:** Four feet of dressed stone on a buried footing, sound,
legible, unmoved. Text below; the Referee reads both lines or hands the table
the paper.

* **The Two Faces:** the same two lines twice, runic above and trade tongue
  beneath, with the numbers as tally strokes in both scripts.
* **What It Says:** the north fork named, with a distance; the west fork named,
  with a distance; and beneath both a third line naming the authority that cut
  the road, which the party will meet on two further markers and nowhere else.
* **The Canting:** the post leans because its footing has been dug at, on the
  west side, recently, by something that gave up.
* **North:** -> `X.7`
* **West:** -> `X.11`
```

### `OCCUPIED` — trigger, and the axe

```
### `X.9 The Bound Saplings` — A Line You Can Step Over, Nothing Stopping You, Somebody Meant It

*For the better part of a mile the trail runs alongside a line of saplings, cut
and bent and bound at head height, one after another as far apart as a man can
throw. There is no fence. There is no ditch. It has been made absolutely
certain that you would know if you crossed it.*

**Referee Overview:** The line runs roughly north for a mile and then turns
away from the road. Nothing here penalises staying on the trail, and a party
that keeps walking never has this conversation.

* **Crossing Is a Choice:** and it is treated as one. She is not watching from
  a hide — the wood tells her — and she will meet a party inside her line
  within the hour, on foot, alone, unarmed.
* **What Happens Next:** depends on what they are carrying. An axe is an
  answer to a question she has already asked twice.
* **The Marks Themselves:** cut and bound rather than carved or planted. The
  work of somebody who could have made a wall and chose to make a sentence.
* **On, Up the Trail:** -> `X.10`
* **Across the Line:** -> `X.12`
```

### `THRESHOLD` — a crossing with a permanent decision

```
### `X.14 The Choked Crossing` — Built to Go Under, Going Over Instead, Forty Paces of It

*The road meets a stream and does not bridge it — it crosses flush, and the
stream is meant to pass beneath, and has not passed beneath for years. The
water has spread sideways instead, a slow ankle-deep sheet forty paces across
with the road somewhere under it.*

**Referee Overview:** A dwarven arch four feet at the crown, silted to within a
hand of the top. The spread costs time, footing and dry boots; the bottom is
soft off the road line.

* **Clearing It:** an afternoon with tools and hands, and the water drops
  within the hour. Permanent, cheap, and it gives a dry crossing for the rest
  of the campaign.
* **Leaving It:** a chokepoint, and the thing that waters here already treats
  it as one. Forty paces of standing water is slow, loud, and looking down.
* **The Churned Banks:** many feet, both directions, unshod. Readable with no
  roll.
* **On, North-West:** -> `X.15`
* **Back:** -> `X.13`
```

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. The trigger condition on an occupant.**
*X1 Isle of Dread, area 1 — the lizard men's mound.* Nocturnal, unknown to the nearest village, and they will not trouble anyone moving in daylight unless somebody sets foot on the mound; then they come out one at a time. The location's occupant field is a condition before it is a disposition. **Rule 2.**

**2. The escalation ladder before violence.**
*X1, the treant forest.* Five stated player actions and five graduated responses: not searching, searching, climbing, cutting, and lighting a fire — with the trees simply walking away from an axe, and a camp fire answered by the forest receding to leave a clearing with three treants positioned round it. Attack comes only after four rungs, and only to whoever throws fire. **The long announcement is more interesting than the single warning.**

**3. Announcement distance as design.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, and the corpus generally.* The instruction that a difficult encounter should be signalled first by sound, smell or tracks rather than met face to face — so the party can price it before it is an encounter. **Rule 1, stated as method.**

**4. Terrain as a rate and a formation.**
*B2, the woods near the caves.* Movement through the thick groves is slow and difficult and characters must move in single file — a tactical fact written as a description. **Rule 3.**

**5. The cache under the lethal thing.**
*B2, wilderness area 2.* A dead elf under leaves near the spider webs, everything rotted except a filthy shield that appears worthless and is not. The region's portable reward sits under the region's one killer, which is the trade a road region should offer exactly once. **Rule 6, and `CACHE`'s reason at its cheapest.**

**6. The road network that outlived its memory.**
*Our own second waystone, and the waystation ledger that confirms it.* Two destinations at stated distances, neither known to anyone alive, confirmed by a toll book counted at the far end of the same road, and answered nowhere. Logged `[setting]` in Standing Mysteries. **Rule 5's counterpart: what a `READING` teaches is sometimes that nobody knows.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

The classic road furniture belongs here and most of it is already a `THRESHOLD` or an `OCCUPIED` with a trigger: the toll bridge and the thing under it, the ford and what waits downstream, the gibbet at the crossroads, the wayside shrine with offerings recently left, the hermit whose hospitality has a price, the standing stone that is a boundary rather than a monument, the deserted camp with the fire still warm.

Two notes for that pass. **The wandering monster belongs on the table, not in a location** — a `WILD MEDIUM` creature is fixed to its ground and does not follow. And **the classic outdoor hazard is best written as terrain**: the bog that takes a pack, the scree that takes an ankle, the deadfall that takes a formation. Those are rates and constraints, and they are better than a save.
