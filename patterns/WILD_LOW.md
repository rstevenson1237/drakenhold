# `WILD` · `LOW`

*Tier two. Short by design — see below. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`.*

---

## WHY THIS CELL IS NEARLY EMPTY

**In a road region the low tier is not written as locations at all.**

The Ironwood Trail carries fourteen locations across several days of walking and **not one of them is `LOW`.** Even the plainest — four hundred paces of exposed roadway — carries three features and both Overviews. The region says where the thin material went: *the ground between is conveyed procedurally; we do not describe every mile of the trail.*

That is the correct handling and it is a real difference from `DANGEROUS`, where the thin tier is thirty-one unnamed rooms a party physically walks through and burns torches in. **A dungeon's negative space is place. A road's negative space is time.** You cannot key time, so you run it.

So this file is not a location-writing guide. It is the note on what replaces one.

---

## WHAT REPLACES THE LOCATIONS

**Watches, movement rates, and the Encounter table.** Between one keyed landmark and the next, the region is conveyed by:

- **A stated pace and a terrain modifier.** Half a day from the trailhead to the first waystone; slower in fen, slower again if searching, single file through thick growth. Terrain is written as a rate and, where it applies, a formation constraint — because a party in single file is a party that can be met at the head.
- **A revelation radius.** How far a party sees and therefore how much of the map they buy by standing somewhere. This is the `WILD` resource the way light is the `DANGEROUS` resource, and it is what makes a ridge worth climbing and a jungle hex a poor bargain.
- **The Encounter table**, on each failed Difficulty roll, resolving in the next watch, not forced. It may be signs, tracks, or a meeting at distance.
- **Navigate, Explore, Track, Evade and Rest**, which get their first outing in the safest ground in the campaign and cost an afternoon when they fail.

None of that goes in a location file. It goes in the Gazetteer entry and the region's Tables.

## THE ONE THING THAT IS WRITTEN AS A `LOW` LOCATION

**The trailhead, and only where a region needs one.** A named node that is a beginning rather than a place: last sight of the palisade, where the ruts begin, the first Navigate roll of the campaign made where failure costs an afternoon and nothing else.

It takes the `LOW` form and it exists because the region needs a code to hang the inbound edge on.

```
### `X.1 The Road Out` — Last Sight of the Palisade, Where the Ruts Begin, Nobody Waves

*The fields give out and the wood begins the way a wall begins, and underfoot
the mud has gone hard and flat and square.*

**Referee Overview:** The trailhead, half a day out at a laden pace, the canopy
closing to a corridor within a hundred paces.

* **The Roadway Underfoot:** cut blocks under a generation of leaf mould, still
  level, still draining.
* **On, Up the Trail:** -> `X.2`
* **Back to the Gate:** -> `[Code]`
```

Everything else in a `WILD` region that seems thin is either `MEDIUM` — because leaving the road is a decision and decisions are `MEDIUM` — or it is procedure.

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. Do not key the ground between landmarks

If a stretch of road has nothing to decide and nothing to see, it is a movement rate and a watch. Writing it as a location produces a page a Referee reads once and never again, and it dilutes the landmarks it sits between.

### 2. If it earns a code, it earns three features

The threshold for keying at all is higher in `WILD` than in `DANGEROUS`. A dungeon corridor gets a code because a party must physically be somewhere; a stretch of road does not, because the party is between things and the between is time.

### 3. Landmark spacing is the pacing mechanism

Space them so that the procedure between them is felt but not endured. The working figure from the corpus is a landmark every half-day to day of travel at laden pace, with the gaps longer where the region wants the road to feel long and shorter at either end.

### 4. Everything dangerous is off the road, and the region says so out loud

The road is the safe thing. State it plainly somewhere a party will hear it early, and then keep the promise — a party that keeps to the trail walks the region end to end. The generosity of the Difficulty Die is part of the same statement.

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. The distance-triggered check.**
*X1 Isle of Dread, the Central Plateau.* Encounters are checked every three hexes travelled rather than on a clock. Route choice becomes the thing that costs — a party taking the long way round pays for it in rolls, where a time-based check would punish deliberation instead. **This is the mechanism that makes `WILD` procedure a decision rather than a tax.**

**2. The revelation radius.**
*X1, the main island.* A party maps one hex in every direction as it passes through — seven new hexes at most — unless the terrain is mountainous or the foliage dense enough to block sight. Terrain costs twice: once in movement, once in how much map a day buys.

**3. Danger as a field, not a table.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, camping rules.* Nothing troubles a camp unless it is within six hexes of a keyed site; at six it is one in six, and the chance climbs by one for each hex closer until it is automatic. Danger radiates from fixed points. A party that has scouted can camp deliberately, and the frequency of the Referee's rolls is itself information.

**4. The symmetric camp choice.**
*B2, same passage.* Post no guard and the monsters surprise automatically; light a fire and the monsters are never surprised, though the party still may be. Two decisions, each with a cost on both sides, reusable every night of travel. **This is the `WILD` analogue of a puzzle and it belongs in procedure rather than in a location.**

**5. The supply tether.**
*B2 and B4 both.* Food and water are carried, hunting is a poor bet, and the module tells the Referee to make clear the party would fare better returning to town. `WILD`'s structural job is to make `SAFE` necessary — the town is only a relief if the road out of it is expensive.

**6. The honest blank at map scale.**
*B2, the Caves of the Unknown.* An area left for the Referee to develop, with the instruction that if it has not been built the players must not find it even searching the exact square — and an in-fiction cover supplied, a magical illusion, so the absence does not break the world. Compare the same module's off-map handling: a sign, a stranger, or a talking magpie redirects a party heading the wrong way, in fiction rather than by fiat.

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

There is very little here for it, and that is right. The classic road furniture — the milestone, the wayside shrine, the gibbet at the crossroads, the ford, the fallen tree — are all `MEDIUM` the moment a party can do something about them, which is nearly always.

The one genuinely `LOW` `WILD` trope worth considering is **the sign of passage that is not an encounter**: birds going quiet ahead and starting again behind, a print in soft ground going the wrong way, smoke in the distance too far to reach today. These belong on the Encounter table rather than in a location, and roughly half of that table should be made of them.
