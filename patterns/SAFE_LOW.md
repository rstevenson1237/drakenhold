# `SAFE` · `LOW`

*Tier two. Short by design — see below. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`.*

---

## WHY THIS CELL IS NEARLY EMPTY

**An empty shop is a dead town, not a thin location.**

In a dungeon, thinness is the floor that makes landmarks legible. In a settlement it reads as failure — a shuttered building, an absent shopkeeper, a room with nothing in it. Those are all legitimate *content* in a `SAFE` region, and none of them is a thin location: a shuttered row with saplings through the porch boards is a `MEDIUM` `TRACE` about an economy that stopped, and it earns three features without effort.

The whole town of Thornhaven runs twenty locations and carries **one** that is honestly `LOW`. The reconciliation note on it is the whole cell in a sentence: *it is an inn with one door on the square. That is not an unreachable location; it is a building.*

---

## WHAT ACTUALLY BELONGS HERE

Three kinds of thing, and no more.

**1. The single-purpose building with one door.** An inn, a smithy, a stable. It does one thing, the party knows what the thing is on sight, and there is nothing behind it. It exists so the region's diagram has a node for a service the party will use constantly and never investigate.

**2. The public fixture.** A well, a fountain, a notice board, a hiring line, a landing stage. Sometimes these are features of a square rather than locations in their own right — key them separately only when a party will go *to* them rather than encounter them while going somewhere else.

**3. The connective ground of a settlement.** A lane, a stair, a back way. Rare, because a town is small enough to cross without keying the crossing, and because in `SAFE` mode the interesting thing about a back lane is usually *who uses it and is not seen doing it* — which makes it `MEDIUM`.

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. If it has a person in it with a want, it is `MEDIUM`

This is the boundary and it is sharp. A smithy is `LOW`. A smith who is not so secretly in love with the storekeeper and will gossip about anyone in town is `MEDIUM`, and the smithy came with him.

Most `SAFE` buildings acquire a person the moment anyone writes them, which is why the cell stays small and why that is correct.

### 2. The service and its cost still get stated

The `SAFE` mode requirements do not relax at low weight. A `LOW` service location declares what it sells or does and what it costs — coin, standing, obligation or risk — in its one referee sentence.

> **Referee Overview:** Two storeys, six rooms above, common room below, one door on the square; upper-town rates and worth them, and the only Rest in the module that returns everything.

That is one sentence and it carries the dimensions, the shape, the price and the mechanical fact.

### 3. No combat statistics, at any weight

Stated in tier one and worth repeating where a writer is most tempted: a `SAFE` location carries no hit points, armour class or damage. The one scenario they would serve ends the campaign, and a Referee who needs them can improvise them faster than they can read them.

### 4. A single-edge leaf is not an error

A building with one door is a building. The checker should not flag it, and a reviewer should not add a back way to make the diagram prettier. **The landlord likes knowing who is in his house, and so do the people lodging there.**

## THE FORM

The standard `LOW` form. One sentence of player register, one dimension-first sentence of referee register, one or two features that are usually the ways out.

```
### `X.6 The Painted Bird` — Clean Floor, Banked Fire, Where the Charter Drinks

*Clean floor, banked fire, beds with blankets on them, and a bird painted on
the sign that nobody has let fade.*

**Referee Overview:** Two storeys, six rooms above and a common room below,
stable behind, one door on the square; upper-town rates, and a day here returns
the whole pool.

* **The Beds:** a full day's Rest, and everything comes back. The party will
  not meet this again after they leave the region, and the module never tells
  them so.
* **The Door:** onto the square, and there is no other -> `X.2`
```

*Two features, one of which is the exit, and the location still carries a service, a price and a mechanical fact the party will care about for the rest of the campaign.*

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced. Fewer here than elsewhere, because there is less to show.*

**1. The building that is honestly a building.**
*Fourtower Bridge, the stables and the well.* Listed on the map, named, and given a line each. Neither acquires a person, a price or a secret, and neither needs one. The module is complete without them being more.

**2. The one-sentence service with a stated failure condition.**
*Fourtower Bridge, the fighter's tower.* A magical vault in the basement where adventurers may store treasure — and the owner keeps it if the party dies or goes missing for more than a year. One sentence: a service, a reason to use it, a cost of failure, and a soft campaign clock. **Rule 2 at its best in the corpus.**

**3. The rule stated by a toll.**
*Fourtower Bridge, the bridge itself.* A copper pot where travellers pay, locals exempt, and anyone caught not paying is beaten and banned for life. The consequence is social and it is what makes the rule real. **A `LOW` `SAFE` location can carry a whole enforcement mechanism in two clauses.**

**4. Where the corpus gets it wrong.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, the towers and barracks.* Dozens of men-at-arms statted individually, floor by floor, with hit points and pocket change. Those numbers serve exactly one scenario — the party attacks the town — and that scenario ends the campaign. **Rule 3, with the counter-example that shows the cost.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

Very little. The classic `SAFE` furniture is nearly all `MEDIUM` because it comes attached to a person: the innkeeper, the smith, the fence, the sage, the temple almoner.

What is genuinely `LOW` and worth having: **the fixture that becomes a meeting point** — the well, the market cross, the notice board, the hiring line at the landing. Key them where a party will go to them deliberately, and give each one the thing it is for in a single line. A notice board that carries the region's paid work is doing more for a campaign than most keyed buildings.
