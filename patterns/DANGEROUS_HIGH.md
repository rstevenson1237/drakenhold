# `DANGEROUS` · `HIGH`

*Tier two. Open this file and no other when writing a landmark location in a dungeon region. Tier one is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`; the forms are in the Playbook template.*

---

## WHAT THIS CELL IS FOR

**The thing the region is remembered as.** Not the biggest room and not the richest — the one a table describes to another table six months later without checking their notes.

**One or two per region. A third is evidence the region has no landmark.** This is a hard count and it is the cell's only quota, because density is what makes weight legible. Four heavy locations in a region of twenty is a region with none.

`HIGH` earns its size two ways and only two. Either the secret tier justifies the weight rather than decorating it, or the location is a **mode-crossing threshold** and the lyrical budget belongs to it. Everything else that feels important is `MEDIUM` and should be written as `MEDIUM`.

---

## PATTERNS THAT POPULATE HERE

- **`THRESHOLD`** at full weight — the merged pattern, formerly split off as `SET PIECE`. The region's landmark and the mode change.
- **`PUZZLE`** — a mechanism with a stated solution and an in-world clue whose location is named.
- **`READING`** at full weight — the module's history delivered as a text the players assemble.
- **`CACHE`** at full weight, where the reason for survival is itself the puzzle.
- **`OCCUPIED`** at full weight — the occupant who is a faction rather than an encounter.

**Absent:** `EMPTY` and `CONNECTIVE` by definition. `TRACE`, `HIDDEN CLUE` and `STANDING HAZARD` appear here constantly, but always as components of one of the five above rather than as the location's own tag. A `HIGH` location composes; that is most of what makes it `HIGH`.

---

## THE FORM

Five to seven features. All three tiers present in at least two of them. The Player's Overview is the longest in the module and is where the Tolkien register does its work; the Referee Overview stays Hemingway and gets *shorter* relative to the size of the room, not longer, because the features carry the detail.

Variety floors bind hard here — this is the cell where a writer is most likely to produce seven features of identical length.

---

## THE RULES OF THIS CELL

### 1. A `PUZZLE` states its solution plainly and places its clue in a named other location

**Both halves are required.** A puzzle whose clue is not placed is a puzzle the Referee has to invent an answer for at the table. A puzzle whose solution is not stated in the Referee Overview is the same failure one step later.

Nothing is left to Referee discretion that a player might reasonably act on.

**The clue is placed more than once and in more than one kind of place.** The strongest instance in the corpus scatters its answer across a ceiling fresco visible only from height, a burial-niche plug, tomb frescoes in three separate chambers, and two random tables. A party can miss most of them and still arrive.

### 2. No puzzle resolves on an attribute roll, and the corpus contains the control

The same author, on the same level, wrote a puzzle with a placed clue and no roll, and a second puzzle that places its clue honestly — eighteen chessboards on the walls, all showing the same mate — and then gates *recognising* it on a roll under Intelligence. The second undoes itself. **If a player can look at the thing and reason about it, the reasoning is the mechanic.**

### 3. Wrong states are content

A mechanism with nine positions should do something at more than one of them. Failure that produces consequence keeps a party experimenting; failure that produces nothing stops them after two tries.

**And the reset is the anti-frustration device.** State how long the mechanism holds and what returns it to default. A hard puzzle without a reset strands a party that half-solved it; with one, they can leave, learn, and come back.

### 4. A mode-crossing `THRESHOLD` gets the lyrical budget and costs nothing

Three days under canopy, then sky, and three peaks with the light on the snow. The first sight of the great chasm. The broken doors from the inside with the cold air coming out past you.

**No roll, no cost, no encounter.** The Referee needs the players' picture to change register, not merely location, and anything charged at that moment competes with the thing the location exists to do. This is the one place in the module where a `HIGH` location may carry almost no mechanism at all.

The corollary: state what makes it a *hard line* rather than a gradient. The wood ends at a definite edge, and the thing that has been harassing the party for two days cannot cross it and knows so.

### 5. A `READING` at full weight is assembled across visits and is written to be partial

The module's history is delivered here, and it is delivered as a text rather than described as scenery. Which means: **write what it says, not what it is about.**

Partial recovery is the normal state and gets its own convention — what a translation cannot recover is marked so the gap is legible on the page and the Referee reads it partial. A party building a glossary occupies the middle state for the whole campaign and the text should meet them there.

Where the reading is bilingual, the two registers sit side by side and **nothing is translated for the players.**

### 6. A `HIGH` `OCCUPIED` is a faction, and its ignorance is as written as its knowledge

At this weight the occupant has a position on every other power in the region, and the useful entries are the ones where the position is *does not know they exist*. A matrix of mutual hostility is a static situation; a matrix with holes in it is one the players can edit.

State what they want, what they will take instead, what they know, what they will sell it for, and what they are wrong about.

### 7. The defiance names what funds it

A sealed tomb that is empty means something only because sealed tombs contain someone. A statue that looks like gold and is painted wood teaches that appearance is not appraisal — and only works in a module whose other hoards are real.

**Write the funding instances by code in the Referee Overview.** A module-level ratio is unenforceable across twenty-two regions written months apart; a defiance that must cite its funding is checkable where it is written.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

*Original. Set nowhere. Codes given as `X.n`.*

### `PUZZLE` — solution stated, clue placed, wrong states live

```
### `X.44 The Weighing Hall` — Three Beams and a Missing Pan, Counterweights in the Wall, It Still Turns

*The hall is built round a set of scales the size of a cart, three beams
sprung from one pillar of green stone, and the pans hang at three heights —
except the highest, which hangs at nothing, because its pan is gone.*

**Referee Overview:** Ninety feet by forty, barrel-vaulted, the pillar central
and the three beams radiating north, east and south-west. **Solution: the
mechanism opens the floor vault when the north and east pans are loaded to
within one stone of each other and the south-west is left empty.** Weight is
read by the counterweight train in the west wall, which is exposed and can be
watched working. After one hour of no change the beams settle and the vault
shuts.

* **The Beams:** they still swing true, and the counterweight train in the wall
  clicks as they do — anyone who watches it for a turn can see it is comparing
  two of the three and ignoring the third.
* **The Missing Pan:** taken, not broken; the pin is intact and the chain was
  unhooked. The pan is in the hands of somebody who wanted the hall silent.
* **Loading Them Wrong:** if all three carry weight, the train runs backward
  and the pillar sinks four inches with a noise the whole level hears, and
  everything on this floor knows where the party is for the next turn.
* **The Standard Weights:** a rack of them, stamped, and the stamps match the
  weights on the public scales at the hold's front door — which is where a
  party will have seen this system used honestly.
* **The Floor Vault:** it opens south of the pillar, four feet by four, and
  what is in it is dry -> `X.45`
* **The Hall's Two Doors:** north -> `X.43` and west -> `X.48`
```

*The solution is stated. The clue is placed twice, once in the room as a watchable mechanism and once in a named location a party has already visited. The wrong state has a consequence and the consequence is attention rather than damage. The reset is stated.*

### `THRESHOLD`, mode-crossing

```
### `X.1 The Broken Doors, From Within` — Thirty Years of Draught, Nothing Came Out, The Light Behind You

*The doors are in pieces around their own frame, and the cold comes out past
you and keeps coming, steady, from somewhere far deeper than this, and it does
not smell of the country you have just walked through. Behind you the daylight
stands in a shape twenty feet high and does not reach the floor in front of
your boots. Ahead there is a hall, and it goes on, and the far end of it is
not a thing your lamps are going to find.*

**Referee Overview:** The threshold proper is a reach of some twenty feet
between the broken leaves, floor scoured clean by the draught. No mechanism, no
hazard, no check. The party is now inside.

* **The Doors:** cut in one piece each and broken outward, which is the wrong
  direction for a siege and the right one for something leaving.
* **The Draught:** constant, cold, outward. It never varies at this door and
  it is the first evidence that the hold is a single connected volume.
* **The Scoured Floor:** three decades of moving air have taken the dust off
  the stone in a fan shape, and outside that fan it lies thick.
* **Back Out, Into the Daylight:** -> `X.0`
* **Into the Hall:** -> `X.2`
```

*No roll, no cost, no encounter. The Player's Overview is the longest thing in the region and the Referee Overview is three lines, because the features carry everything a Referee actually needs.*

## EXCERPTS FROM THE CORPUS

*Paraphrased. Cited so the original can be opened; not reproduced.*

**1. The puzzle that does everything right.**
*Arden Vul Level 3, areas 3-2A, 3-15 and 3-40.* Three statues of the same god on one level, each with arms that rotate through fixed positions. Set all three correctly and a pedestal grinds back to reveal the way to the treasury. The solution is stated plainly to the Referee. The clue is placed in at least six named locations — a ceiling fresco visible only from height, an untouched burial-niche plug, wall art in three separate tombs, and entries on two random tables. No roll anywhere in the chain. **Rules 1 and 2.**

**2. The same author's anti-pattern, as a control.**
*Arden Vul Level 3, area 3-36B.* A door opened by touching four glyphs in sequence, with the clue placed honestly — eighteen chessboards on the surrounding walls, every one arranged to show the same mate. And then recognising it is gated on a roll under Intelligence. The placed clue is undone by the roll laid on top of it. **Rule 2, with the control written by the same hand on the same level.**

**3. Wrong states as content, and the reset.**
*Arden Vul Level 3, area 3-15.* Nine arm positions on one statue: one opens a secret door, two open pits, one opens a chute to the level below, and several do nothing. After an hour of inactivity the arms return to default and the pits close. Experimentation is dangerous, informative, and recoverable. **Rule 3.**

**4. The mode-crossing threshold at full weight.**
*B2 The Keep on the Borderlands, the approach to the Caves of Chaos.* The only long read-aloud in the wilderness: the forest thickening until it seems to warn you off, the thicket ending abruptly, walls rising a hundred feet, cave mouths at varying heights all round, a vulture on a dead tree, ravens going up with their cries magnified by the terrain, and bones among the rubble. Nothing is rolled and nothing is charged. **Rule 4.**

**5. The reading assembled across visits.**
*Winter's Daughter and Arden Vul both, on the bracketing of partial translation.* The convention marks what a translation cannot recover so the gap is legible on the page — giving a third state between opaque and rendered, which is the state a party building a glossary actually occupies for the whole campaign. **Rule 5. Already adopted; not re-derived.**

**6. Defiance funded by convention held elsewhere.**
*B4 The Lost City, room 11a.* A glittering statue at the end of a corridor that appears at first to be gold, and is painted wood on a heavy stone base, worth nothing. It works because rooms 7, 18 and 22 carry real hoards, and it teaches — cheaply, early, before anything expensive — that appearance is not appraisal. **Rule 7.**

## FOR THE TROPE PASS

The heavy classic furniture lives here and most of it is a `PUZZLE` or a `CACHE` at full weight: the chessboard floor with animated pieces, the statue whose limbs are a combination lock, the sarcophagus that opens to a chalked word, the fountain of random blessings, the mouth that speaks in a dead language, the vault whose lock is a name.

Two cautions for that pass. **A `HIGH` location can only carry one central mechanism** — a chamber that is a puzzle *and* a hazard *and* a hoard is three `MEDIUM` locations that have been stacked, and it plays as none of them. And **the region's landmark should not always be the dangerous thing.** A hall the players remember for what is written on its walls is a landmark; a hall they remember for what nearly killed them is an encounter with good staging.
