# DRAKENHOLD — PROCEDURES AND RULES

*Amended at the close of engineer step 7. Changes from the original: steps 8 and 9 are swapped, reconciliation is made explicit rather than assumed, and the working unit for the remainder of the project is one region per conversation. Rules are unchanged and are restated whole so this document stands alone.*

*Amended again at the open-item resolution pass. **Step 11, playtest intake, is added**, and the practices below are renumbered as their own list rather than continuing the procedures. Rule 17 gains the `[[ playtest: ... ]]` reserved prefix and is otherwise unchanged; no other rule is touched.*

## PROCEDURES

1. **Setting** — name and three thematic tags.
2. **Setting outline** — Overview, Truths, Rumours, History, Factions, Bestiary.
3. **Regional Gazetteer** — each region a code, name, Classification, Difficulty Die and three thematic tags.
4. **Region Overview** — a referee-facing paragraph preceding the named fields, carrying whatever is overarching or not yet distributed into them.
5. **Region fields** — Ambiance, Layout, Features, Dangers, Creatures, Secrets, Treasure.
6. **Setting relational diagram** — regions and the connections between them, as tiers 1 and 2 of the diagram layer: one graph of the region blocks, then one graph per block of the regions it contains. Untyped edges at both tiers. `diagrams/Drakenhold_Relational_Diagram.md` states the tiering in full and is the head of the layer.
7. **Location stubs** — for each region, a code, name and three thematic tags per location, each carrying a working note with whatever the stub needs to survive to the next step. The region's Events, Encounter or Danger table is authored here, once there are real places for it to point at.
8. **Region relational diagrams** — tiers 3 and 4: one graph per region of its location groups, and one graph per location group resolving to individual locations. **Tier 4 is where every location edge is drawn and where connection type is drawn at all**; tier 3 is derived from it. **Drawn from the stubs, before the location outlines are written.** The diagram is a working instrument first and a deliverable second: it is where unreachable locations, single-edge dead ends, regions that have quietly become corridors, and unplaced route ends are found, and finding them here is far cheaper than finding them in prose.
9. **Location outlines** — a Player's Overview, a Referee Overview, and a list of the features the location contains. **Before writing, the writer opens exactly one tier-two design-pattern file** — `patterns/<MODE>_<WEIGHT>.md`, selected by the region's Classification, declared at step 3, and the location's weight, decided at step 7 by which locations are named and which are unnamed fill. The other eight are not carried. `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md` is tier one and binds across all nine. Working notes are absorbed and struck. **Outlines generate edges the stubs did not anticipate** — doors, shafts, sightlines, drops — so the region diagram is reconciled against the finished outlines before the region closes. The reconciled diagram is the deliverable.
10. **Location detail** — full descriptions of each feature, with landmark detail in the two Overviews, hidden detail as the feature itself, and secret detail written inside the feature description as the reward for investigative play. **Secret detail is gated on a named physical action and never on a search roll.** The same one tier-two file is open here as at step 9.
11. **Playtest intake** — what happened at the table, written back into the ground it happened on. Run after a session, never on a schedule, and never on ground the party did not reach. Each observation is written **where it occurred** — in the location, the region field or the table it concerns — as a `[[ playtest: ... ]]` note, which is the ordinary editorial mark under rule 17 carrying a reserved prefix, so no third inline mark is introduced and the note still must not survive to the finished Playbook. **A note records what happened, not what to do about it**: *the party never found the fresco, searched the east wall twice and left*, never *move the fresco*. Diagnosis belongs to the reconciliation run, which reads these notes at `J13` and routes them; a note answered in the pass that wrote it is a table anecdote promoted to a design decision without an argument. An observation with no place in the corpus to attach to is not yet a note and goes straight into `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`. **A playtest pass writes notes and ends in a question batch, and does nothing else** — it opens no region, closes none, and edits no content.

## HOW THE STEPS ARE RUN

*These are practices and they are numbered as their own list. They were once numbered 11–20 as though they continued the procedures, which made "procedure 12" and "step 12" two different things and made the second collide with a step that now exists. **Cite them as practices**; the crosswalk from the old numbers is in `DECISIONS.md`.*

1. **Iterate in ascending passes.** Run 1. Run 1–2. Run 1–2–3. Each pass re-enters at the top, revises what earlier passes established, and extends one step further. Architect covers steps 1–6, engineer 7–9, builder 10 onward.

2. **Each new step is preceded by a reconciliation pass over every step before it.** This is not a review of the whole document at every turn; it is a check that the material the new step will rest on still says what it said. Before step 10 begins anywhere, steps 1 through 9 are read back through and reconciled as a body of work. Contradictions found in reconciliation are raised, never resolved silently.

3. **One region per conversation, for the remainder of the project.** A region's diagram, outlines and reconciliation are one focused unit of work with its own context. The region file, the relevant connective block document, the setting outline and the handoff are the inputs; a closed region file is the output.

4. **Regions that share structure are worked as a block.** Where several regions share a labyrinth, a mechanism or a route system, they are worked together in staged passes rather than one at a time. A connective document holds what belongs to no single region — shared routes, budgets, cross-region threads — and the block is split into region files when it closes. The connective document survives the split. **Existing block documents remain authoritative** and are updated rather than superseded: `FIRST_LEVEL_BLOCK`, `PEAK_1_BLOCK`, `PEAK_2_BLOCK`, `PEAK_3_BLOCK`, `I_AND_J_BLOCK`.

5. **Cross-region threads are reconciled at both ends.** A region does not close while it holds an edge, a name or a promise whose far end has not been checked. Where the far end is in a region not yet worked, the thread is recorded in the handoff rather than assumed.

6. **Each pass adds detail and audits for inconsistency.** Unresolved items and open questions accumulate in **`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`** rather than being decided silently. That file is the single register and it is architect material. The setting outline stays referee-facing: its Unanswered Questions section holds only what the module leaves open at the table on purpose, and moving something into it is a decision that the setting will not answer that question — not a place to park unfinished work. Ratified calls go to **`DECISIONS.md`**.

7. **Later passes may revise earlier material,** but changes to established content are proposed and flagged, never applied without direction.

8. **Each pass ends in a question batch,** delivered whole, with suggested answers attached. Suggestions are expected to include genuine alternatives, not only the conservative option. **Where a pass decides nothing requiring direction, the batch is omitted rather than manufactured.**

9. **The design-pattern catalogue is retrieved by grouping, and its patterns are applied after writing.** The retrieval key is `mode × weight`, and both are known before a word is written, which is why one file is opened rather than nine. The ten **patterns** are the opposite: they are applied to a finished location as classification, and **the classification reaches no page.** There is no pattern field and no pattern tag — reading a location against the list changes the location, not a label on it, and the pattern-conditional checks in `RECONCILIATION.md` are judgement checks for exactly that reason. A location that fits no pattern is evidence the list is wrong before it is evidence the location is.

10. **The record is updated at the close of every region**, each part of it in its own place: `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` for what was opened and answered, `DECISIONS.md` for what was ratified, the block document for cross-region threads, `regions/00_INDEX.md` for counts and phase, and `HANDOFF.md` for what the next conversation does first. **`HANDOFF.md` is kept to a page.** It was previously comprehensive, became a fourth place the truth lived, and drifted; it is now a pointer document and is not allowed to grow back. A fresh conversation should be able to pick up the next region from the handoff and the files it points at.

## RULES

1. Follow the TTRPG Design Notes. **`Rules_Light_TTRPG_Design_Notes.md` is the single source of truth for game mechanics** — dice resolution, Difficulty and Action Dice, regional modes, time and distance, Status, Encumbrance, the Weather and Encounter procedures, Rest. It is authored in a separate project and mirrored into this repo read-only: it is never edited here, never extended here, and is replaced wholesale when a new revision is uploaded. **No mechanic is invented in setting content.** Where a pass needs a procedure the Notes do not cover, or covers ambiguously, the gap is written into the setting outline's Unanswered Questions and raised in the pass's question batch for resolution *in the rules project* — not settled in a region file. Where a region must operate before the gap closes, it states the effect in Referee terms the Notes' existing procedures can carry, and names the dependency. Rules usage may be proposed; explicit test calls should be rare, and sufficient detail lets the Referee rule without instruction.
2. Follow the Setting Playbook Template's formatting. Content inside the template is illustrative and never enters the setting. **Its `STYLE` section binds every section below it** — the two registers, the vocabulary rule and its reconstructibility constraint, the variety floors and the paraphrase test — and is the authority J8 checks against.
3. **Sandbox.** Situations to explore, not a story to follow.
4. **Lived-in world.** Constructed language and proper names throughout; avoid tropey shorthand.
5. **The path is not clear.** More than one route to most places, usually with a choice attached. Players are allowed to lose the way.
6. **Every gate has an answer that is not the gate.** A lock may be genuinely hard, but the network around it always offers another way in — longer, darker, or watched by something worse. The alternate route is a cost, never a free pass.
7. **Negative space.** Detail is distributed, not uniform. As a working proportion: a third low detail, a third medium, a third high — or a third combat and hazard, a third puzzle and conundrum, a third connective tissue. Rooms may be empty. Roughly half a region's room budget is stubbed as named locations; the rest is unnamed fill inside the stated groupings. **These thirds govern how detail is distributed and are not a count of the `HIGH` form.** The form is capped separately at one per ten locations, maximum two in any region, per the template and `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`. The two measures were once written as though they were one and they are not: a region can be a third high in detail and still carry two `HIGH` locations.
8. **Three tiers of detail.** Landmarks are stated in the Overviews. Hidden detail answers a general question. Secret detail answers a specific one, sits inside the feature it belongs to, and **is reached by a named physical action rather than by a roll**. `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md` states the same three tiers as a placement rule — which layer lives where, is reached how, and is written in which register — and that table is the working form of this rule, not a second one. The same three tiers govern regions, where landmark locations draw the eye from afar and the ground between them is conveyed procedurally.
9. **Layers of history.** Every place carries a reason it was built, a history of what it was, and a reality of what it is now. Untangling those layers is player work.
10. **Player mechanics.** Every element is written from the direction of how a player will meet it. Clues to hidden and secret detail always exist and are not always easy to find.
11. **Prose for the referee.** Hemingway — hard-bounded, spatially precise, measurements and cardinal directions stated, because mapping is expected.
12. **Poetry for the players.** Tolkien — lyrical, hinting at the wider world. Player-facing text asks the questions that referee text answers, and that exchange is what drives the setting's growth.
13. **OSR playstyle.** Enough for the Referee to rule; not so much that agency narrows.
14. **B/X cameo.** Classic material is salt and pepper, never the spine. The pleasure is a player recognizing an old danger wearing a new name.
15. **Tables follow the Classification, not the terrain.** SAFE carries Events, WILD carries Encounters, DANGEROUS carries Dangers, regardless of where the region sits. Tables ascend from 1 to x; the Danger table is the sole exception and counts down.
16. **Names compound** from the established root vocabulary. New roots are proposed and added to the record rather than coined in passing.

17. **Two marks are written inline and are not prose.** A reference into a setting-outline field is written **`(SECTION, key)`** — `(BESTIARY, Goblin)`, `(TREASURE, II)`, `(GRAFFITI, d20)`, `(LORE, The watch log)`. It survives to the finished playbook and becomes a link there, so it is a promise that the thing named exists, and `M9` holds it to that. An **editorial note is written `[[ ... ]]`** in double brackets, is designer register, and **must not survive**: it is struck at the final pass alongside working notes and the location-entry field labels. `M10` reports outstanding notes on every run and fails on them under `check.sh --final`. **One prefix inside that mark is reserved: `[[ playtest: ... ]]` is a step-11 observation from the table**, and it is the same mark under the same rule — designer register, reported by `M10`, and struck before the final pass. It is reserved so that `J13` can find every one of them and so that a reader can tell an author's note to themselves from a fact about a real session. **`J13` is what strikes it**, and it strikes it by resolving or promoting it, never by deleting it for being stale; a playtest note removed to get `check.sh --final` green is the one abuse of this mark the file forbids by name. Location codes are not written either way — they keep their backticked form and belong to `M1`. Both forms are ratified in `DECISIONS.md` under `## NOTATION AND EDGE TYPES`.
