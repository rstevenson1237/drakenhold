Where a decision has produced setting content, that content is authoritative in its own file and this document only records that the decision was made and why.*

## MECHANICAL RULINGS

*`Rules_Light_TTRPG_Design_Notes.md` is the source of truth for game mechanics and is read-only here. The rulings below were raised as gaps against it after step 8 and **answered in the rules project**. They are recorded here so setting content can lean on them, and they are expected to be folded into the Notes at the next upload. **Nothing below is invented in this repo.**  Where the Notes and this section ever disagree, the Notes win and this section is stale.*

**Light in the deep — ratified as a region procedure, standing as written.** The three-state rule in `blocks/I_AND_J_BLOCK.md` — open flame, shielded light, no light — is a legitimate one-off Referee-facing procedure rather than a mechanic invented outside the Notes. The Notes' universal resolution is explicitly built to carry discrete, region-scoped procedures, and this is one. Tunable in playtest. **Procedural bloat is the failure mode to watch**: a one-off procedure earns its place where players will engage with it as a system, and nowhere else.

**Rest — three levels, tied to the region's Classification and nothing else.** The time matches the region: **a day** of rest in a SAFE region, **a watch** in a WILD region, **ten minutes** in a DANGEROUS region. The consequence is the whole point of the Action Dice economy: Combat Dice recharge — and are lost — quickly; a lost Skill Die is far more impactful; a Magic Die is an order of magnitude above that. `A.6`'s claim to be the only Rest that recovers everything is correct and needs no amendment.

**Weather on the Skybridge.** With the Notes' Severe result reading *Travel Impossible*, and given what the span is, a Referee should rule confidently that **merely being outside on `I` is hazardous and that movement is near-certain lethality**. This is a ruling to be made plainly, not a table to be written.

**The Danger Die.** Resets to **6 every time the region is entered**. The Referee is responsible for refreshing the table once expended. The system can be gamed by walking a line between regions; any real movement inside a region engages it as intended, so no counter-mechanic is added. **A region's Danger Die and its Danger table do not connect to each other in any way.**

## THE SETTING'S STANDING CALLS

**The seal.** One tile-gated door on the lateral run between the Peak 1 and Peak 2 warrens, closed since the civil war. Hard lock, and the tile is at `C.1` on a goblin chief's neck. The network's answer is not a second door but the Ash Run and Cold Run in series, at three times the distance through the worst-mapped ground in the block. A second, copied tile is held by the Peak 2 survivor pocket at `GA.19`, who live inside the sealed segment. Taking it by force is legitimate, priced, and the module never says which line is correct.

**Three arteries, no procedure.** Long Run (F→G→H), Ash Run (F→G), Cold Run (G→H). Veins between groupings, capillaries between everything. No landmark system, no service marks, no mechanic — a sheer numbers game of twisting paths and trying to keep track. The survivors' only advantage is thirty years of memory a party cannot buy.

**Survivor pockets are not a faction in practice.** Three groups, discrete, no leader, no council, at least two actively disliking each other. Smallest and failing in Peak 1 (three, needing food, light and medicine, too frightened to leave, hiding a walled-in fourth). Most hostile in Peak 2 (nine, behind the seal). Largest in Peak 3 (fourteen or more, the only one with a goal beyond survival). **Credit does not transfer between pockets.**

**The Processionals are not two accounts of one history.** The Living proclaims the wealth and greatness of a never-ending hold, present tense, standing in its own ruin. The Dead reveres the ancestors' foreknowledge while carving the literal wedge that came down. Both hazardous to cross badly; both carry secrets for anyone who reads rather than walks. The **Monument of the Driving Down** stands where they meet, in `GA` — mostly untouched, the longest continuous bilingual surface in the hold, and its few defacings are late, specific and informative.

**The waystones are the module's entire teaching method for runic script.** Full bilingual text at `B.2`, `B.5` and `B.8`, roots hyphenated, numbers as tally strokes. Nothing is translated for players. Five roots added at that pass and recorded in the outline's TRUTHS: *rok* road, *lath* league, *ven* toward, *ost* water, *vir* wood. Thornhaven's unused dwarven name is **Ostgir**.

**Artifact pieces — seven, and the count is itself a clue.** Two ever left the mountain, `E.5` and `D.3`, and the evacuation register at `E.6` proves it. Five remain inside: `FD.14`, `GD.10`, `HB.14`, `HD.14`, `J.18`. **All seven placements are ratified**, `GD.10` last — the third sub-vault, placed under Royal authority in the season of the Breaking. Seven were broken at `HE`; two were carried out at `E.6`; **the module never performs the subtraction.**

**Tiles gate sideways, never up.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found the Truth promising four applications where the corpus draws two.* A carved tile buys **lateral** movement — the three Skybridge termini, and the seal across the servants' main run — and never vertical movement. **The great ramps and both Processionals stay open to anyone**, and their price is exposure rather than a lock: being seen on them, not being stopped at them. This is the reading all three peaks arrived at independently and it is now what the Truth says. Step 9 may add a tile lock to a warren mouth where a region earns one; it may not put one on a ramp or a Processional.

**Rumour 19 is half true, and the true half is readable rather than walkable.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep.* The northern road under the mountain, out the far side of the Broken Teeth, **existed** — cut off at the Driving Down and closed with words rather than rock, like everything else at `J.4`. The inscription there names the road, names what it traded with, and states why it was shut. **No edge, no stub, nothing passes.** It is the module's only account of what lies north and it is a script reward, not a route. The rumour is wrong about the road being open and right about the road.

**Ashen's Crew are a clock, not a location.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found the module's most urgent faction stated only outside the mountain.* They are in the squat at `E.7` when the party arrives — **rumour 14 runs ahead of the fact** — and they go up on a schedule the Referee advances whether or not the party engages. Which level they have reached when they are met is a function of how long the party took. They can be raced, beaten, followed or caused, and they are still the ones who will open the wrong vault. Peak 3's passes inherit them; see `PEAK_3_BLOCK`.

**The Silver Veins seam is ambient in the three level-2 regions.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found it the only stated History trace with no home.* `FC`, `GC` and `HC` each carry it at their own step-9 passes as an architectural fact a party crosses rather than finds — finer, later work in a different stone above the Main Level, the join running clean. **Written three times in three peaks rather than once**, because that repetition is how a party works out the join is hold-wide. No stub, no new location.

**The three Man-type Bestiary entries are used where they already belong.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep.* `Sellsword` at `D.10`, `Delver` at `E.7`, `Camp Hand` at `A`'s hiring — the prose in all three already describes exactly those templates and only the names are missing. They are named when those locations are written. Nothing is cut from the Bestiary.

**The outpost at the crossing died at its own door, and the hold's own people killed it.** *Ratified after `D`'s step-9 pass.* The post at `D.2` held a confiscation order two years past the existence of any government that could lift it. At the Descent the last flood down the road went through it: fourteen dwarven guard dead facing the mountain door, dwarven wounds, the river door standing open behind them. **No new creature, and no horror on the approach road.** The courier stopped under that order died two years earlier and the piece he carried went under the guardroom floor, which is why the strongroom explains the dead and the watch log dates them. **"Thirty years" is the round number everyone alive says**, and the two-year gap between the courier and the garrison is legible only in the weathering of the bones — inside the room and outside it.

**The toll has two numbers and only one of them is ever quoted.** *Ratified after `D`'s step-9 pass.* A small price a head going up the road, and **a share of the load coming back down, assessed by eye at the booth.** The Grellick Charter has paid the first four times and has never yet paid the second. This is the module's spine priced at a booth in the second week and **no one performs the subtraction** — not the hobgoblins, not the Charter, not the region text.

**The open gate is the Hammer Gate.** *Ratified after `E`'s step-9 pass.* The waystone at `B.8` names three — the Wyrm Gate, the Hammer Gate and the Deep Gate — as though a traveller would need to know which to use, and promises that a party will find exactly one standing open. **It is this one:** the great doors at `E.12`, whose frame carries the hold's name and the door's own name cut in both scripts. **The other two are untouched and unpointed-at.** Nothing at `E.12` says where they are, no later region owes them a location yet, and the question `B.8` planted stays planted.

**The city guard went up the road before the descent.** *Ratified after `E`'s step-9 pass.* Two-thirds of Girkel's strength had already gone up as escort and as labour for people carrying their households out of the mountain, which is why the last muster roll at `E.8` is short and **why nothing held at the wall** — no arrowheads in the ground, no dead in the four breaches, the gate bar drawn back. `E.1` asks the question and `E.8` answers it, two streets apart, with no clue added to either.

**Names ratified at `D`.** **Vashek**, the hobgoblin captain at `D.8`, unique and stated inline. **Halvard Grellick**, the charter-holder's factor at `D.10`. **Emmet Tarr**, the Charter's missing surveyor, whose satchel is the one in the nest at `C.9` — named from `D`'s side only, with `C` unamended. **Brynost**, the crossing's dwarven name, *bryn* and *ost*, span and water, compounded from roots already on the record and cut on the parapet at `D.7` and in the bilingual ordinance at `D.5`.

**The stub ratio.** Roughly half a region's room budget is stubbed as named locations; the balance is unnamed fill inside the stated groupings. This is what makes the negative-space rule real rather than stated.

**Pass-4 decisions, in force and not to be re-litigated:** room budgets, difficulty dice, mode rationale, the three tiers of detail, the Secrets/Treasure split, empty fields, unique NPCs held for the location pass, the authorial registers, the B/X cameo, and the three-tag stub heading.

## NOTATION AND EDGE TYPES

**`one-way` is load-bearing and is never used loosely.** It is a declared edge type in every region's Connections preamble, and it is also what exempts an edge from M2's symmetry check. A route that is merely *hard* in one direction, or *not yet open*, is not one-way — it is `conditional on mechanism`, or it is priced in prose. **Three edges carry the declared type:** `FA.8`⇢`FB.9`, the kitchen chute, which is a fall; `J.9`⇢`D.6`, the outfall, which is water the whole way and comes up below the near bank; and `I.15`⇢`GE.3`, the crater descent, which arrives on top of a sleeping dragon. **Two more are called one-way *in practice* in prose** — the root sink at `C.7`–`C.9`, and `GA.27`, the wrong mouth onto the Processional, which nothing marks from the far side. Those are descriptions of what a route costs, not types: they are drawn undirected, they reciprocate, and M2 checks them like any other edge. **Do not promote a prose "one-way in practice" to the declared type**, and do not demote the declared type to prose.

**The great doors are undirected.** `E.12`═`FA.1`, drawn `===` at both ends. `E`'s diagram formerly drew an arrowhead for emphasis, which made it the one place in the corpus where the same glyph meant two things — on the module's only opening to the outside world, in a module whose spine is extraction. **Corrected: emphasis is carried by the heavy edge alone, never by an arrowhead.**

**The `HC.12`–`HD.4` excavation is bidirectional.** Ratified. Its gate is **time and player intervention** — the dig completing on its own clock — and never direction. Until it is through it carries nobody; once it is through it is a hole in a floor. `HC`'s bullet said one-way and neither diagram nor `HD.4`'s own Connections agreed; the bullet was corrected, because the diagrams are authoritative. **Consequence to be written, not argued away:** the ring at `HD` now has a second way off it that nobody paid the circuit for, and `HD`'s step-9 pass owns pricing that against `HE`'s rule that the earned rod at `HD.10` buys a way back out.

**The location-entry field labels come off at the final pass.** *Ratified during `A`'s step-9 pass, which is where they were first written.* A finished location entry carries no labels at all: the **Player's Overview is indicated by being italic**, the **Referee Overview by being plain text**, and the **Features by being a bullet list**. `*Player's Overview:*`, `**Referee Overview:**` and `**Features:**` are scaffolding for the authoring phase, exactly as the working notes were, and they are struck the same way. **The full strike list lives at step 12**, which is where the sequencing and the gate are stated; this entry records only that the labels are on it.

**The `Connections:` field disappears entirely.** Not relabelled — removed. Every edge it carries is already a feature a player can meet, and the `->` pointer at the end of that feature's own entry is where the way out is stated. The way out is found where the thing itself is described, which is the template's rule and the whole point of the pointer. **Step 9 keeps the field for now**, because `scripts/check.sh` M3 reads it and the region's tier-4 diagrams are checked against it; the field is dropped and M3 is repointed at the feature pointers together, in one pass, never separately. Until then, every edge in the field is also written as a feature pointer, so that removing the field later loses nothing.

**The diagram layer is five tiers, one file per diagram, in `diagrams/`.** Tier 1 the setting, showing the region blocks; tier 2 a block, showing its regions; tier 3 a region, showing its location groups; tier 4 a location group, showing its locations; tier 5 the location, which has no diagram. **Each tier draws only the tier below it.** **Connection type is drawn at tier 4 and nowhere else** — above it, two connected children carry one plain untyped `---` however many typed routes run between them and whatever kind they are. **A tier-4 edge leaving its group draws the destination location outside the frame**, which is the only duplication the layer permits and is what lets a group diagram be read alone. **Tier 4 is authored; tiers 1 to 3 are derived** by `scripts/diagrams.py --write` and re-derived by M11, so no fact about the graph is written down twice. Host documents carry a marker line — `<!-- DIAGRAM: T3_FA.md -->` — and the PDF and web builds splice the file into place.

**Blocks on the graph are not blocks in `blocks/`.** The five graph blocks are the approach, the three peaks whole, and `I` with `J`. The authoring partition puts `FA`, `GA` and `HA` together because they share a warren, a seal and a room budget; that is a writing unit, and a peak drawn without its main level is not a legible diagram. `blocks/FIRST_LEVEL_BLOCK.md` is therefore the one block document with no tier-2 graph of its own, and says so.

**`S` is not a node.** The servants' passages are location groups inside `FA`, `GA` and `HA`, and the runs between them are ordinary region-to-region edges. The old overview drew `S` — and the deep cells, the observation rooms and Torvin Ganthur's niche — as boxes hanging off a region because it had no tier below it to resolve into. It has one now, and each of them is a location with a code.

**Connection pointers in location text use `->`.** Written as plain ASCII in the markdown, because the source must stay human-writable in any editor. The renderers substitute a single typographic glyph for the web and PDF targets. See `Setting_Playbook_Template.md`.

**Setting-outline references are written `(SECTION, key)`.** The section name is ALL CAPS and matches an outline `##` heading exactly, plus the literal `LORE` for `LORE_INDEX.md`. The key names what is being pointed at, and a token may carry two. `(BESTIARY, Goblin)` is one creature. `(TREASURE, II)` is the whole Type II table and means roll it; `(TREASURE, II, 14)` is one row of it. `(GRAFFITI, d20)` is the die and means roll the table. `(TRAPS, The covered pit)` and `(LORE, The watch log)` are one entry each. **A reference token never carries a location code** — codes keep their backticked form, `` `A.13` ``, and stay M1's business. The token is checked by M9 and becomes a link in the web and PDF targets, so a token that names nothing is a build-visible error rather than a piece of dead prose.

**Editorial notes are written `[[ ... ]]`, in double brackets.** Designer register, per `CLAUDE.md`: it must not survive. `[[ needs a second exit once GB closes ]]`. **Single brackets are not available** — `[local]`/`[setting]` already scope Standing Mysteries and `[tile]`/`[rod]`/`[hidden]`/`[secret]`/`[vert]`/`[mech]`/`[faction]`/`[size]` already type diagram edges, and both are declared vocabularies rather than free-form annotation. Double brackets collide with neither. A note that records something already written elsewhere takes the form `[[ already: ... ]]`. **Notes are struck at the final pass**, alongside the working notes M8 governs and the field labels above; M10 reports them during authoring and fails on them under `check.sh --final`. The renderers strip them unconditionally, so a missed note can never reach a reader — but that is a floor, not a licence to leave them in. **The full strike list lives at step 12.**

## STRUCTURAL FINDINGS THAT NOW GOVERN

*Drawn at step 8 and true of the module rather than of one region. Carried forward into the location passes.*

**Each peak answers its gates from a different direction.** Peak 1 answers **downward** — the freight hub at `FD.8` is reachable because two routes were bought a level below. Peak 2 answers **from anywhere but the floor itself** — not one gate on the peak is answered on its own level. Peak 3 answers **upward**, in the direction the party is already climbing, and the price climbs with it.

**Three regions have no lock of any kind.** `GE`, `HE` and `J`. In `GE` the d4 is the lock; in `HE` the lock is the map; in `J` the locks are the dark and the distance, and both are procedures rather than doors.

**Every peak has an express route and the module performs no subtraction on it.** A party that stops for nothing crosses `D`, `E` or all four levels of Peak 3 in a handful of nodes, and arrives holding none of what makes the next place survivable. The module never warns anyone.

**Two regions have a hub that is not a room.** `I.7`, the assessment, and `HE.5`, the broken geometry — the mechanism is the corridor a party walks rather than an obstacle it meets. Arrived at independently in the two regions furthest apart in the module.

**Every descent from every peak lands on `J`'s spine**, which is why the light rule can be taught once, at `J.6`, and why that teaching is unmissable rather than lucky.

## THE DESIGN PATTERN CATALOGUE

*Ratified when the catalogue was wired into the procedures. The catalogue itself is `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md` (tier one) and `patterns/` (tier two, nine files). The reasoning behind these calls, as it stood at the harvest close, is archived at `archive/DESIGN_PATTERNS_HANDOFF_at_harvest_close.md` — **where the archive disagrees with a working file, the working file is right.***

**The retrieval key is `mode × weight`, and one file is opened.** Mode is a property of the region, declared at step 3. Weight is a property of the location, decided at step 7 by which locations are named and which are unnamed fill. Both are known before a word is written, which is what makes the catalogue nine small files rather than one large one. A writer opens the cell for the region they are in and does not carry the other eight.

**Patterns are applied after writing and reach no page.** The ten patterns — `CONNECTIVE`, `EMPTY`, `THRESHOLD`, `READING`, `TRACE`, `HIDDEN CLUE`, `STANDING HAZARD`, `OCCUPIED`, `CACHE`, `PUZZLE` — classify a finished location. **There is no pattern field and no pattern tag.** Reading a location against the list changes the location, not a label on it. The pattern-conditional checks are judgement checks at `RECONCILIATION.md` J11 for exactly that reason, and the sequencing problem a tag would have created — a checker obliged to run before the scaffolding is struck — does not arise.

**`SET PIECE` is merged into `THRESHOLD` and is not a pattern.** They were one pattern at two weights. A threshold inside one mode is thin; a mode-crossing threshold carries the heaviest landmark writing in the module, because the players' picture has to change register and not merely location. Recorded because the old name will be met in older material.

**The nine cells are unequal on purpose and are not padded to match.** `DANGEROUS LOW` is the largest cell by room count and the smallest by content. `SAFE LOW` is near-empty because an empty shop is a dead town, not a thin location. `WILD LOW` dissolves into procedure — watches, Navigate, the Encounter table — rather than being keyed as locations.

**`HIGH` is capped at one per ten locations, maximum two in any region.** A twenty-stub region carries two; a ten-stub region carries one. **This is a cap on the structural form and is not the same measure as rule 7's negative-space thirds**, which govern how detail is distributed. The two were written as though they were one thing and they are not; both documents now say so in the same words. A third `HIGH` is evidence the region has no landmark rather than three of them.

**No secret is gated on a search roll, anywhere in the module.** Gates are physical: standing somewhere, clearing something, opening something, speaking a name, looking back. The previous revision of `Setting_Playbook_Template.md` allowed secret detail to surface on "a granted Difficulty roll" and **that allowance is withdrawn**. Recorded rather than deleted quietly, so a later pass reads the absence as a decision and not as an oversight. A `PUZZLE`'s solution chain carries no attribute check at any link.

**The three location forms bind from the first-level block forward.** `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` and `LOW` share their fields and differ in how much of each is written; the form declares the weight and there is no weight tag. They bind from `FA`/`GA`/`HA` onward. **`A` through `E` were written before the forms existed and are not read against them here** — the retrofit is a booked pass of its own, recorded in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

**A `LOW` location may not contain a find.** Not a small one, not a hidden one. A thin location with treasure in it teaches a party to search every thin location in the module and destroys the pacing function of every honest one. If it has something in it, it is `MEDIUM` and is written as `MEDIUM`.

**A location may belong to two regions of different modes.** Fenn's boundary is one node on two diagrams. This is legal; the edges must be identical at both ends, and an asymmetry is the error rather than the sharing.

**The catalogue is process material.** `DESIGN_PATTERNS.md` and `patterns/` are not assembled into the Playbook and are not published to the web view, exactly as `PROCEDURES_AND_RULES.md` and `RECONCILIATION.md` are not. They are also outside the corpus `scripts/check.sh` reads, which is why their worked examples may carry `X.n` codes and `->` pointers without tripping M1, M2 or M3.

**The template's `STYLE` section binds every section below it** and is the authority J8 checks against. Its worked examples, like all template content, are illustrative and never enter the setting — and the catalogue's worked examples are deliberately set nowhere, using `X.n` codes and no Drakenhold names, so they cannot be quarried into canon by accident.

**The rumour truth-marker scheme is adopted, with four deliberate blanks.** Every rumour carries `(T)`, `(P)` or `(F)` in the source, stripped by the renderer from anything player-facing. Sixteen of the twenty are marked. **Rumours 4, 7, 9 and 15 carry no marker on purpose** — each rests on a question the module has not answered, and a marker would settle that question by side effect. They are open in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` with the pass that owns each; rumour 4, the lance, stays unmarked permanently, because its properties are a Standing Mystery. **An unmarked rumour is a warning and never a failure**, and `RECONCILIATION.md` states it that way. **The markers record current state and are not locked**: a marker changes when the pass owning its question decides differently, and that is a revision rather than a contradiction. Only the true set is constrained — expanding it devalues every clue in the module.

**The six Standing Mysteries are scoped.** `[local]` — hireling statlines, Baldrun's prepared and never-filled interment at `HB.6`, the cutter at `A.12`, and the Hobgoblin lean-season raid. `[setting]` — the properties of the lance, and the Knight's fate, which History preserves as unknown while `HB` resolves it on the ground.

**Two template changes were proposed by the incoming revision and declined.** *Recorded because the archived catalogue handoff still asserts both, and a later pass reading it must not reinstate them.*

- **The `Ooze` creature type is not adopted.** The one candidate — the Bestiary's `**Ooze** (Fantasy Creature)`, met at `FB.12` and in `J`'s flooded galleries — stays `Fantasy Creature`, which is the catch-all and is carrying it correctly. Typing an entry named *Ooze* as `(Ooze)` reads as an error on the page, and the retype would also have stripped its Morale by making it a no-Morale type. The type is removed from the template's Creature Types **and from the Morale standing rule**, which now scopes to `Undead` and `Construct`. No Bestiary entry and no region file changed.
- **The all-pairs faction relation requirement is not adopted.** The existing Factions format stands. The template keeps the original position — factions need not know one another exist, ignorance is its own relation, and discovery should be worth something — and drops the added requirement to state a relation for every ordered pair. Stating the full matrix is work the module has not asked for and would fill the outline with pairs that never meet.

## TROPE CONTENT

*Ratified at the trope content pass, which added the Bestiary's classic mainstays and authored the setting's Treasure, Traps, Graffiti and Procedural Tables sections. **No region file was edited in that pass**, by direction: recommended placements went to `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` addressed to each owning region's own step-9 pass, so that nothing established was rewritten and nothing was decided on a region's behalf.*

**The treasure taxonomy is I Scavenged Loot · II Equipment and Armaments · III Luxury and Trade Goods · IV Caches · V Magical Baubles.** *Ratified by direction, and it diverges from the Notes.* `Rules_Light_TTRPG_Design_Notes.md` reads II Foraged Goods, III Caches, IV Hoards, V Unique Treasures. The Notes are read-only here and are amended upstream, never in this repo, so **the divergence is booked as a rules-project question in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` and the setting content is written to the ratified taxonomy in the meantime.** Until the Notes are re-uploaded this is the one place in the project where setting content and the Notes disagree on purpose, and it is recorded here so a later pass reads it as a decision rather than an error. Types I–IV read as d20 tables and Type V is bespoke, which is the Notes' own shape and is unchanged.

**Foraging is a procedure, not a treasure type, and it is region-locked.** *Ratified by direction when the taxonomy was settled.* Foraged Goods held the Type II slot under the Notes' older taxonomy and hold no slot under this one, because forage is not carried out and sold — it is eaten, traded to the people who live where it grows, or left. **Two d6 tables carry the whole of it: one locked to `B` and `C`, one locked to `J`.** A region that is not one of those three has nothing to forage, and that is a decision rather than an omission. **Nothing a party forages is worth a slot on the way home**, which is the reason it is not treasure and the reason it cannot compete with the extraction decision the Treasure tables exist to force.

**Type V stops below Artifact tier.** Magical Baubles are enchanted weapons and armour, scrolls, rings, wands, oils and potions. Artifacts are unique, named, priceless, and placed by hand. The module has one and it is the lance, whose properties are a `[setting]` Standing Mystery — **no rolled result may settle them**, and the Type V table is written so that nothing on it is a lance, resembles one, or hints at what one does.

**Flavour procedural tables may be written into a location or region description.** *This narrows the earlier position, which held that one-off procedures were rules-project proposals and none might be written into a region file.* The distinction is now explicit and it is the same distinction the light-in-the-deep ruling turns on: **a table that only supplies colour is content; a table that scores something is a mechanic.** Colour tables — what is on the board, who is drinking, what forages in a clearing, what the midden holds — track nothing the table was not already tracking and go straight into the file. Anything that keeps state, escalates, or asks the players to hold a number remains a rules-project proposal and is not written. Procedural bloat is still the failure mode to watch.

**Two mechanical constraints bind any procedural table in a region file.** First, **the table's bold name line must be more than one word, and the table must sit after the region's mandated Events, Encounter or Danger table** — `scripts/check.py` M5 locates the mandated table by the first single-word bold name followed by a die, so `**Menu** — d20,` above it is parsed as the region's table and M5 fails, while `**What Is On The Board** — d20,` is safe. Second, **a procedural table never substitutes for the mandated one**: table type follows Classification and a procedural table is always in addition.

**Traps are a placed catalogue and never a rolled table.** They sit at `MEDIUM` weight or above, per the pattern files, and every entry states a tell, a trigger, an effect and a decision before it is written. The module's traps are dwarven security built by people who expected to catch a thief rather than bury one; the Runemaster wards are the stated exception. **No trap in the Traps section introduces a mechanic** — resolution rests on the Notes' existing Find and Remove Traps move, the Keen Eyed advantage, and the successive-rolls guidance for large mechanisms.

**Graffiti rolls generic names, and placed names are substituted deliberately.** The scratched-name index is a real device across `D.6`, `E`, `GB.9` and `GA.16`, and a rolled name that collided with a placed NPC would manufacture a thread the module cannot support. The table therefore rolls names built from the recorded roots that mean nothing, and states that handing a party a placed name instead — Torvin Ganthur under a bench, most of all — is a Referee's decision to open a thread rather than a random result. **Carved and scratched stay distinct words**: carved is official and authorised, scratched is not, and the difference is how the King's struck name and the Knight's absent one are eventually read.

**`LORE_INDEX.md` is an index and not a source.** It registers documentary items that have a far end, one row each, pointer and gist only. The text lives in the region file, which stays authoritative, and a pass finding the two in disagreement fixes the index. It is deliberately outside `scripts/check.sh`'s corpus until it has stabilised; that is booked in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

## THE OPEN-ITEM REGISTER AND THE TABLE

*Ratified at the open-item resolution pass, which found the register at roughly forty live items and no stated way to tell one kind from another, and found no route at all by which a real session could inform the module.*

**Open items carry a route, and there are six.** `A` strike as already answered · `B` direct edit · `C` editorial note, item stays open · `D` regenerate by re-running the owning step · `E` ratify only · `F` refer out to the rules project. The routes are stated in full in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, which is where they are worked; they are recorded here because assigning one is a decision. **The two items under `OPEN — DELIBERATELY, AND STAYING THAT WAY` carry no route**, and routing one is a decision the routing pass was not asked to make.

**Architectural items regenerate rather than get patched.** Where an item changes how content was *produced* rather than what it says — the `A`–`E` retrofit to the three location forms, the unconsumed `## FOR THE TROPE PASS` sections, the assumptions the catalogue rests on — the resolution is to re-run the owning step over that ground with the one correct cell file open. **Patching the prose toward the new shape is not a resolution**: it closes the symptom and leaves the region unable to survive its own re-run, and the next pass cannot tell a patched region from a generated one. This is the whole reason the routes were written down.

**The `D` passes wait on the checker.** The predicate backlog in `RECONCILIATION.md` and M3's repoint come first. Regenerating a region before the predicates that would check it exist means regenerating it twice, and the ordering is therefore part of the ratification rather than a scheduling preference.

**Playtest feedback enters at step 11 and resolves at `J13`.** A session's observations are written where they happened as `[[ playtest: ... ]]` notes — the ordinary editorial mark carrying a reserved prefix, **so the two-inline-marks rule is unchanged and no third mark exists**. The prefix is reserved so `J13` can find every one and so a reader can tell an author's note to themselves from a fact about a real session. **`J13` reads and routes them; `M10` only counts them.** A playtest note is never deleted for being stale and never struck to make `check.sh --final` pass — that is the one abuse of the mark named in both files.

**A note records what happened, not what to do about it, and one table is not a finding.** Diagnosis belongs to the reconciliation run and not to the pass that wrote the note; a party that missed a clue is a fact about that party until the clue chain actually fails J3, or a second table reports the same thing. **A party that hated a gate may be evidence the gate worked.** The module is not tuned toward what players wanted at the table.

**The practices are their own numbered list, and the old numbers are retired.** `PROCEDURES_AND_RULES.md` numbered its procedures 1–10 and then continued 11–20 through *how the steps are run*, which made "procedure 12" and "step 12" different things — tolerable while step 11 did not exist and not tolerable now that it does. **The practices restart at 1 and are cited as practices.** Crosswalk, once: old 11–20 are now practices 1–10 in the same order, so old 12 → practice 2, old 14 → practice 4, old 16 → practice 6, old 17 → practice 7, old 20 → practice 10. **The `RULES` list is untouched**, and a citation reading "rule 7" or "rule 17" still means what it always meant. Recorded rather than renumbered silently, because older material and the archive both cite the old numbers.

## THE STRIKE, AND THE CHECKER AS A BLOCK

*Ratified at the strike pass, which found the final pass owned by nobody, described in six documents and sequenced in none, with its only machine-side handle a CLI flag.*

**The strike is step 12, and it is the only step that is neither iterative nor additive.** It runs once per edition, last, and nothing runs after it. Steps 1–11 may be run through reconciliation as often as wanted; **step 12 may not be run at all until they are finished**, because it removes the data they read. `M3` parses the `Connections:` field and `M8` parses the working notes: after the strike most of the checker cannot run, and no earlier step can be re-run on stripped ground. **A boundary that behaves like this is a phase, not a mode**, which is why it stopped being `--final`'s job to carry it.

**The strike list is now stated once, at step 12.** Working notes · the four location-entry field labels · the whole `Connections:` field · every `[[ ... ]]` note including `[[ playtest: ... ]]` · any surviving architect register. Previously this lived in rule 17, two entries in this file, an italic in `Setting_Playbook_Template.md`, a clause in `HANDOFF.md` and an item in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, and no two of them listed the same things. **Those statements are not deleted and are not wrong** — they now point at step 12 rather than each carrying a partial list.

**It runs on a release branch cut from `main`, never merged back.** `main` keeps the pre-strike source, stays checkable in full, and is where the next edition is authored. This is what makes a once-only step survivable: **the edition is struck, the module is not.** The archive recorded the underlying constraint at the catalogue harvest — *the checker must run before the final write, or on the pre-strike source* — and it was recorded nowhere else until now.

**The removal is scripted; the gate is judgement.** A deterministic `scripts/strip.py` does the cutting, so the strike is reviewable as a diff and cannot drift between editions. What it may not decide is whether the module is ready: every region closed through step 11, `J13` clear, `M3` repointed, every predicate built, `check.sh` green on the pre-strike source, and no route `B`, `C` or `D` item outstanding against shipped ground. **`J13` removes the question and step 12 removes the mark** — arriving at the strike with an unrouted playtest note is a failure of the gate, never something the strike tidies away.

**An unbuilt mechanical check blocks new ground.** *This is the call that changes the work order.* The predicates under `RECONCILIATION.md`'s `MECHANICAL — NOT YET BUILT` stop being a backlog and become a block: **no further step-9 region is opened while that list is non-empty**, so `FA`/`GA`/`HA` waits on the checker. The reasoning is the reasoning behind route `D` — a check that will exist shapes what an author writes, and seventeen regions written ahead of the checks are seventeen regions to regenerate. **The judgement checks are not covered by this**; J1–J13 are complete as written and are not scriptable in any case. **Predicate failures on `A`–`E` are expected and are evidence for the retrofit**, which those regions predate; a red check is not a reason to soften the check.

**Step 10 is the decorator phase and owns the trope pass.** *Newly named at this pass; the repo previously had no name for step 10 beyond "location detail" and the trope pass had no owner at all.* The nine `## FOR THE TROPE PASS` sections were written as advisory inventories and left unconsumed because no step was responsible for spending them. Step 10 is that step. **Rules 4 and 14 are unchanged and still bind** — constructed names throughout, classic material as salt and pepper and never the spine — and the two cautions from the catalogue carry: one central mechanism per `HIGH`, one lethal off-road site per `WILD` region. **Decoration is detail and not furniture**: a location that needs a trope to become interesting was thin at step 9, and that is a step-9 defect.

## THE ROUTE-A SWEEP

*Ratified at the route-A sweep, the first pass to work the register as a whole rather than a region at a time.*

**A route may be carried by a section, and two sections carry none.** The register turned out to be route-homogeneous almost everywhere — the mechanical dependencies are route `F` entire, the steps 1–8 watch items are all `C`, the trope recommendations are all `C`, the editorial-markup items are all `E`. **The route is therefore stated once per section and an item is tagged only where it differs**, because forty-five identical tags would have buried the exceptions, which are the part worth reading. `OPEN — DELIBERATELY` and the *techniques noticed and not yet used* carry no route at all: the first is answered by being unanswered, the second holds candidates rather than questions.

**The `A`–`E` retrofit is route `B` applied per location under a `D`-shaped audit, and this is a named exception to route `D`.** *Ruled by direction.* Route `D` would regenerate the five closed regions by re-running step 9. That is refused here for a specific reason: **the prose has been read and approved, and `J2` demotion has already run against it clause by clause** — a regeneration discards both, and the demotion ledger in `A`'s commit records a real loss that was caught and restored. The audit instead reads every written location against the form it should take, the rule that a `LOW` may hold no find, the adjacency floor and the `HIGH` cap, and **changes only what violates one**. Recorded as an exception rather than applied quietly, because an unrecorded exception to a route is how the routes stop meaning anything.

**The retrofit runs before `FA`/`GA`/`HA` and after the predicates.** *Ruled by direction.* Before, because `A`–`E` are the worked examples every later step-9 pass opens and an example that is wrong teaches wrong — this is not the checker's hard block, which is a separate call, but it lands in the same place. After the predicates, because an audit against constraints no script enforces is an audit done by eye, and the whole point of the block is that a check that will exist should exist before the ground is read against it.

**Rumour 8's sleep cycle is `GE`'s to settle.** *Assigned at this sweep, which found it the one item in the steps 1–8 section carrying no owner.* `GE` is where the Dragon is, and it is the only pass that can decide a cycle without deciding it by side effect from outside. Nothing else in the module may confirm or falsify *he sleeps a year at a stretch and wakes for a year*.

## THE DELIVERABLE'S SHAPE, AND THREE PLACEMENTS

*Ratified by direction at the route-A sweep's batch. These are the calls that had been sitting longest — the first had been open since the editorial markup and outline split pass, and none of them would have been answered by any pass arriving, which is what route `E` means.*

**The blocks publish as they stand, under thematic names.** *Answer (c) of the three that were written.* The three-tier hierarchy — setting, block, region — **is a reading structure and not only an authoring one**, and the blocks go to the reader as parts. This was the option recorded as carrying a cost, and the cost is now paid rather than accepted: **the twelve pass-shaped headings across the five block documents lose their `PASS N — ` prefix at step 12**, along with any trailing step citation, leaving `MAJOR ROUTES`, `MAJOR GROUPINGS`, `LANDMARKS AND ROUTES` and `THE REGION DIAGRAMS`. Those are reader-facing headings as they stand. **Stripping a prefix is removal**, which is why it belongs to the strike rather than to a rewriting pass, and it is the difference between (c) and (c) done badly. What is *not* adopted is (b): the blocks are not renamed to `BOOK I` and are not given a new reader-facing register, because that is a writing job the module has not asked for.

**Ashen is a woman, and the House rod is carried by one of the two crew who read runic.** *Written at `E.7` and ratified here; the content did not change and only the call was missing.* **`E`'s Creatures field wording stands exactly as written** — *one carrying a House rod he cannot identify* — and a later pass must not undo it by tidying the pronoun toward Ashen. The two facts are separate: Ashen leads the crew, and the rod is carried by somebody else.

**`Orc` and `Ghoul` are the `A`–`E` audit's to place.** Both have their natural homes in closed regions — `Orc` as the Hobgoblins' hired spears in `B` or `D`, `Ghoul` as the unburied of the Outer City in `E` — and placing either revises an established Creatures field. **The audit is the one pass entitled to do that, because that is what it is for**, and it is already booked ahead of `FA`/`GA`/`HA`. Neither entry is cut and neither is left unplaced. **The Girkel-is-quiet-at-night line is kept**: moving the `Ghoul`'s fiction to `HB`'s untended tiers was the alternative that cost it, and that alternative is not taken.

**Rumour 10 is re-marked `(P)`, and the `Wearer` is placed.** *"Every dwarf in Drakenhold is dead. Whatever you hear moving in there is wearing them."* It was `(F)`, and placing a shape-taking creature anywhere would have made its second sentence true. **The answer is at the rumour rather than at the placement**: it is one fact that reads in two directions at once, which is precisely what the partial marker is for and is the rumour table doing the work it exists to do. The first sentence stays false — every dwarf is not dead — and the second becomes true where the `Wearer` is. **`HA` and `HC` site it in the service warrens** at their own step-9 passes. **Expanding the partial set is free and only the true set is constrained**, so this costs the clue economy nothing; sixteen of twenty rumours remain marked and the four deliberate blanks are untouched.

## WHAT `->` MEANS, AND WHAT THE STRIKE REACHES

*Ratified by direction at the strike-tooling pass, which found both questions by building the tooling rather than by reading for them.*

**`->` means an exit and nothing else.** A feature pointer is the record of a connection a party can use, and after step 12 it is the *only* record, because the `Connections:` field is gone. **`B.6` was using it for something else** — its *Way Around* feature pointed at `B.10` while describing a bypass that runs `B.5`→`B.10`→`B.7`, around the deadfall rather than out of it, and both the diagram and `B.6`'s own field agreed there is no such edge. One instance in 195 pointers. **The sentence stays and the arrow goes**, with a line added saying where the spur actually leaves the trail: the prose was already doing the work, and nothing about what a player learns at the deadfall changes. **A marker distinguishing exit-pointers from reference-pointers was considered and refused** — rule 17 allows two inline marks and this would have been a third.

**Step 12 strikes the architect register inside region files, sentence by sentence.** All twenty-two carried italic process prose — *"Procedure step 7 for the code, name and three thematic tags"*, *"Feature detail is written at step 10"*, *"the step-7 working notes have been absorbed"* — and one sentence described the `Connections:` field that the same step removes. **It cannot be struck by the line**: in several files those sentences share a line with genuinely referee-facing content, and the fill descriptions — *"the balance is unnamed fill — bunk rows, kit rooms, empty cells by the dozen, the ordinary rooms of a garrison with the garrison taken out"* — tell a Referee what the unnamed rooms are. That is play content and it stays. **Block cross-references stay too**, because the blocks are published parts and a pointer at one now leads a reader somewhere real. Room budgets and stub counts are left alone deliberately: whether a count belongs in front of a reader is a different question and the strike does not answer it.

**A check may declare itself not applicable, and that is not a pass.** `M8` reads working notes and `M12` reads the `Connections:` field; step 12 removes both, so under `--final` each returns `N/A` rather than reporting clean. **Step 12's acceptance test must not be able to pass by having nothing left to check** — that is the one failure mode a final gate cannot have, and it was the state the gate was in until this pass.
