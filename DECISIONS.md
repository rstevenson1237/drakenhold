## MECHANICAL RULINGS

*`Rules_Light_TTRPG_Design_Notes.md` is the source of truth for game mechanics and is read-only here. The rulings below were raised as gaps against it after step 8 and **answered in the rules project**. They are recorded here so setting content can lean on them, and they are expected to be folded into the Notes at the next upload. **Nothing below is invented in this repo.**  Where the Notes and this section ever disagree, the Notes win and this section is stale.*

**Light in the deep — ratified as a region procedure, standing as written.** The three-state rule in `blocks/I_AND_J_BLOCK.md` — open flame, shielded light, no light — is a legitimate one-off Referee-facing procedure rather than a mechanic invented outside the Notes. The Notes' universal resolution is explicitly built to carry discrete, region-scoped procedures, and this is one. Tunable in playtest. **Procedural bloat is the failure mode to watch**: a one-off procedure earns its place where players will engage with it as a system, and nowhere else.

**Rest — three levels, tied to the region's Classification and nothing else.** The time matches the region: **a day** of rest in a SAFE region, **a watch** in a WILD region, **ten minutes** in a DANGEROUS region. The consequence is the whole point of the Action Dice economy: Combat Dice recharge — and are lost — quickly; a lost Skill Die is far more impactful; a Magic Die is an order of magnitude above that. `A.6`'s claim to be the only Rest that recovers everything is correct and needs no amendment.

**Weather on the Skybridge.** With the Notes' Severe result reading *Travel Impossible*, and given what the span is, a Referee should rule confidently that **merely being outside on `I` is hazardous and that movement is near-certain lethality**. This is a ruling to be made plainly, not a table to be written.

**The Danger Die.** Resets to **6 every time the region is entered**. The Referee is responsible for refreshing the table once expended. The system can be gamed by walking a line between regions; any real movement inside a region engages it as intended, so no counter-mechanic is added. **A region's Danger Die and its Danger table do not connect to each other in any way.**

## THE SETTING'S STANDING CALLS

**The seal.** One tile-gated door on the lateral run between the Peak 1 and Peak 2 warrens, closed since the civil war. Hard lock, and the tile is at `C.1` on a goblin chief's neck. The network's answer is not a second door but the Ash Run and Cold Run in series, at three times the distance through the worst-mapped ground in the block. A second, copied tile is held by the Peak 2 survivor pocket at `GA.19`, who live inside the sealed segment. Taking it by force is legitimate, priced, and the module never says which line is correct.

**Three arteries, no procedure.** Long Run (F→G→H), Ash Run (F→G), Cold Run (G→H). Veins between groupings, capillaries between everything. No landmark system, no service marks, no mechanic — a sheer numbers game of twisting paths and trying to keep track. The survivors' only advantage is thirty years of memory a party cannot buy.

**Survivor pockets are not a faction in practice.** Three groups, discrete, no leader, no council, at least two actively disliking each other. Smallest and failing in Peak 1 (three, needing food, light and medicine, too frightened to leave, hiding a walled-in fourth). Most hostile in Peak 2 (nine, behind the seal). Largest in Peak 3 (fourteen or more, the only one with a goal beyond survival). **Credit does not transfer between pockets.**

**The Processionals are not two accounts of one history.** The Living proclaims the wealth and greatness of a never-ending hold, present tense, standing in its own ruin. The Dead reveres the ancestors' foreknowledge while carving the literal wedge that came down. Both hazardous to cross badly; both carry secrets for anyone who reads rather than walks. The **Monument of the Driving Down** stands where they meet, in `GA` — mostly untouched, the longest continuous bilingual surface in the hold, and its few defacings are late, specific and informative.

**The waystones are the module's entire teaching method for runic script.** Full bilingual text at `B.2`, `B.5` and `B.8`, roots hyphenated, numbers as tally strokes. Nothing is translated for players. Five roots added at that pass and recorded in the outline's TRUTHS: *rok* road, *lath* league, *ven* toward, *ost* water, *vir* wood. Thornhaven's unused dwarven name is **Ostgir**.

**Artifact pieces — seven, and the count is itself a clue.** Two ever left the mountain, `E.5` and `D.3`, and the evacuation register at `E.6` proves it. Five remain inside: `FD.14`, `GD.10`, `HB.14`, `HD.14`, `J.18`. **All seven placements are ratified**, `GD.10` last — the third sub-vault, placed under Royal authority in the season of the Breaking. Seven were broken at `HE`; two were carried out at `E.6`; **the module never performs the subtraction.**

**Tiles gate sideways, never up.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found the Truth promising four applications where the corpus draws two.* A carved tile buys **lateral** movement — the three Skybridge termini, and the seal across the servants' main run — and never vertical movement. **The great ramps and both Processionals stay open to anyone**, and their price is exposure rather than a lock: being seen on them, not being stopped at them. This is the reading all three peaks arrived at independently and it is now what the Truth says. Step 9 may add a tile lock to a warren mouth where a region earns one; it may not put one on a ramp or a Processional.

**Rumour 19 is half true, and the true half is readable rather than walkable.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep.* The northern road under the mountain, out the far side of the Broken Teeth, **existed** — cut off at the Driving Down and closed with words rather than rock, like everything else at `J.4`. The inscription there names the road, names what it traded with, and states why it was shut. **No edge, no stub, nothing passes.** It is the module's only account of what lies north and it is a script reward, not a route. The rumour is wrong about the road being open and right about the road.

**Ashen's Crew are a clock, not a location.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found the module's most urgent faction stated only outside the mountain.* They are in the squat at `E.7` when the party arrives — **rumour 14 runs ahead of the fact** — and they go up on a schedule the Referee advances whether or not the party engages. Which level they have reached when they are met is a function of how long the party took. They can be raced, beaten, followed or caused, and they are still the ones who will open the wrong vault. Peak 3's passes inherit them; see `PEAK_3_BLOCK`.

**The Silver Veins seam is ambient in the three level-2 regions.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep, which found it the only stated History trace with no home.* `FC`, `GC` and `HC` each carry it at their own step-9 passes as an architectural fact a party crosses rather than finds — finer, later work in a different stone above the Main Level, the join running clean. **Written three times in three peaks rather than once**, because that repetition is how a party works out the join is hold-wide. No stub, no new location.

**The three Man-type Bestiary entries are used where they already belong.** *Ratified at the steps 1–8 sweep.* `Sellsword` at `D.10`, `Delver` at `E.7`, `Camp Hand` at `A`'s hiring — the prose in all three already describes exactly those templates and only the names are missing. They are named when those locations are written. Nothing is cut from the Bestiary.

**The outpost at the crossing died at its own door, and the hold's own people killed it.** *Ratified after `D`'s step-9 pass.* The post at `D.2` held a confiscation order two years past the existence of any government that could lift it. At the Descent the last flood down the road went through it: fourteen dwarven guard dead facing the mountain door, dwarven wounds, the river door standing open behind them. **No new creature, and no horror on the approach road.** The courier stopped under that order died two years earlier and the piece he carried went under the guardroom floor, which is why the strongroom explains the dead and the watch log dates them. **"Thirty years" is the round number everyone alive says**, and the two-year gap between the courier and the garrison is legible only in the weathering of the bones — inside the room and outside it.

**The toll has two numbers and only one of them is ever quoted.** *Ratified after `D`'s step-9 pass.* A small price a head going up the road, and **a share of the load coming back down, assessed by eye at the booth.** The Grellick Charter has paid the first four times and has never yet paid the second. This is the module's spine priced at a booth in the second week and **no one performs the subtraction** — not the hobgoblins, not the Charter, not the region text.

**Names ratified at `D`.** **Vashek**, the hobgoblin captain at `D.8`, unique and stated inline. **Halvard Grellick**, the charter-holder's factor at `D.10`. **Emmet Tarr**, the Charter's missing surveyor, whose satchel is the one in the nest at `C.9` — named from `D`'s side only, with `C` unamended. **Brynost**, the crossing's dwarven name, *bryn* and *ost*, span and water, compounded from roots already on the record and cut on the parapet at `D.7` and in the bilingual ordinance at `D.5`.

**The stub ratio.** Roughly half a region's room budget is stubbed as named locations; the balance is unnamed fill inside the stated groupings. This is what makes the negative-space rule real rather than stated.

**Pass-4 decisions, in force and not to be re-litigated:** room budgets, difficulty dice, mode rationale, the three tiers of detail, the Secrets/Treasure split, empty fields, unique NPCs held for the location pass, the authorial registers, the B/X cameo, and the three-tag stub heading.

## NOTATION AND EDGE TYPES

**`one-way` is load-bearing and is never used loosely.** It is a declared edge type in every region's Connections preamble, and it is also what exempts an edge from M2's symmetry check. A route that is merely *hard* in one direction, or *not yet open*, is not one-way — it is `conditional on mechanism`, or it is priced in prose. **Three edges carry the declared type:** `FA.8`⇢`FB.9`, the kitchen chute, which is a fall; `J.9`⇢`D.6`, the outfall, which is water the whole way and comes up below the near bank; and `I.15`⇢`GE.3`, the crater descent, which arrives on top of a sleeping dragon. **Two more are called one-way *in practice* in prose** — the root sink at `C.7`–`C.9`, and `GA.27`, the wrong mouth onto the Processional, which nothing marks from the far side. Those are descriptions of what a route costs, not types: they are drawn undirected, they reciprocate, and M2 checks them like any other edge. **Do not promote a prose "one-way in practice" to the declared type**, and do not demote the declared type to prose.

**The great doors are undirected.** `E.12`═`FA.1`, drawn `===` at both ends. `E`'s diagram formerly drew an arrowhead for emphasis, which made it the one place in the corpus where the same glyph meant two things — on the module's only opening to the outside world, in a module whose spine is extraction. **Corrected: emphasis is carried by the heavy edge alone, never by an arrowhead.**

**The `HC.12`–`HD.4` excavation is bidirectional.** Ratified. Its gate is **time and player intervention** — the dig completing on its own clock — and never direction. Until it is through it carries nobody; once it is through it is a hole in a floor. `HC`'s bullet said one-way and neither diagram nor `HD.4`'s own Connections agreed; the bullet was corrected, because the diagrams are authoritative. **Consequence to be written, not argued away:** the ring at `HD` now has a second way off it that nobody paid the circuit for, and `HD`'s step-9 pass owns pricing that against `HE`'s rule that the earned rod at `HD.10` buys a way back out.

**The location-entry field labels come off at the final pass.** *Ratified during `A`'s step-9 pass, which is where they were first written.* A finished location entry carries no labels at all: the **Player's Overview is indicated by being italic**, the **Referee Overview by being plain text**, and the **Features by being a bullet list**. `*Player's Overview:*`, `**Referee Overview:**` and `**Features:**` are scaffolding for the authoring phase, exactly as the working notes were, and they are struck the same way.

**The `Connections:` field disappears entirely.** Not relabelled — removed. Every edge it carries is already a feature a player can meet, and the `->` pointer at the end of that feature's own entry is where the way out is stated. The way out is found where the thing itself is described, which is the template's rule and the whole point of the pointer. **Step 9 keeps the field for now**, because `scripts/check.sh` M3 reads it and the diagram is checked against it; the field is dropped and M3 is repointed at the feature pointers together, in one pass, never separately. Until then, every edge in the field is also written as a feature pointer, so that removing the field later loses nothing.

**Connection pointers in location text use `->`.** Written as plain ASCII in the markdown, because the source must stay human-writable in any editor. The renderers substitute a single typographic glyph for the web and PDF targets. See `Setting_Playbook_Template.md`.

## STRUCTURAL FINDINGS THAT NOW GOVERN

*Drawn at step 8 and true of the module rather than of one region. Carried forward into the location passes.*

**Each peak answers its gates from a different direction.** Peak 1 answers **downward** — the freight hub at `FD.8` is reachable because two routes were bought a level below. Peak 2 answers **from anywhere but the floor itself** — not one gate on the peak is answered on its own level. Peak 3 answers **upward**, in the direction the party is already climbing, and the price climbs with it.

**Three regions have no lock of any kind.** `GE`, `HE` and `J`. In `GE` the d4 is the lock; in `HE` the lock is the map; in `J` the locks are the dark and the distance, and both are procedures rather than doors.

**Every peak has an express route and the module performs no subtraction on it.** A party that stops for nothing crosses `D`, `E` or all four levels of Peak 3 in a handful of nodes, and arrives holding none of what makes the next place survivable. The module never warns anyone.

**Two regions have a hub that is not a room.** `I.7`, the assessment, and `HE.5`, the broken geometry — the mechanism is the corridor a party walks rather than an obstacle it meets. Arrived at independently in the two regions furthest apart in the module.

**Every descent from every peak lands on `J`'s spine**, which is why the light rule can be taught once, at `J.6`, and why that teaching is unmissable rather than lucky.
